# Unified strategic framework from the Alex Hormozi source library

Status: SOURCE INGESTION output, 7. 9. 2026. This document consolidates what the ten ingested sources say. It is not a strategy for Antam Homes, it applies nothing to Antam Homes, and it makes no recommendation. That phase starts only after the owner says SOURCE INGESTION IS COMPLETE.

## 0.1 What this is and how to read it

- Evidence base: 3 books ($100M Offers 2021, $100M Leads 2023, $100M Money Models 2025) and 7 YouTube transcripts (one uploaded file was a byte-identical duplicate and was discarded). Per-source notes live in `notes/`, the topic-by-source index, the register of named frameworks, the tension list and the stage register live in `SOURCE-MAP.md`.
- Tags on every line: [S] the author's stated principle or rule; [E] the author's own example or number; [I] an inference or a cross-source link that the author does not make himself.
- Citation keys: [Offers pNN], [Leads: chapter], [MM: chapter or offer], [T-Sales hh:mm] (2,5 h sales lecture), [T-Content mm:ss] (55-min content keynote), [T-Moats #n] (13 ways to destroy competition), [T-OffersLM mm:ss] (offers and lead magnets workshop clip), [T-CaseSaaS mm:ss] ($2M SaaS onboarding and retention case), [T-Affiliates mm:ss] (affiliate launch structure), [T-LeadMagnets mm:ss] (lead magnets deep dive).
- Every topic section follows the same template: core statements, decision rules and thresholds, when and when not (stage and business type), the author's examples with numbers, connections and tensions, and what the sources do not cover. Variants of the same rule are kept side by side on purpose; they are evidence, not noise.
- Nothing here has been softened or balanced. Where the author's claims rest on unverifiable statistics or on his own portfolio results, `SOURCE-MAP.md` (section "Open questions / limitations") lists them.

## 0.2 The spine: how the sources fit together (in the author's own framing)

- [S] Offers = what to sell; Leads = how to find the people to sell it to; Money Models = how to get them to buy, and to pay for the next customer [MM: Ten Years In Ten Minutes]. Leads declares itself to sit on top of Offers [Leads: Section I].
- [S] The order of levers is market (a "starving crowd") over offer over persuasion [Offers p52]. The sales lecture agrees from the other end: the multipliers outside the call beat anything said on the call [T-Sales 00:01–00:20].
- [S] Growth has only two sources: more customers, or customers worth more [Leads: The Problem This Book Solves]. Advertising produces engaged leads, not leads [Leads: Leads Alone Aren't Enough]; the Core Four ({warm, cold} × {1-to-1, 1-to-many}) is the complete taxonomy of ways to get them [Leads: Section III].
- [S] A money model is a deliberate sequence of offers (attraction, upsell, downsell, continuity); the bare minimum is that a customer's gross profit in the first 30 days exceeds the cost to get and serve them; the "$100M" standard is that one customer funds at least two more [MM: Section I; Section VI].
- The transcripts add what the books leave out: a full sales system (T-Sales), a content system and the "why should I listen to you" frame (T-Content), moats (T-Moats), onboarding and retention operations (T-CaseSaaS), affiliate launch mechanics (T-Affiliates), and deeper lead-magnet and offer-vector material (T-LeadMagnets, T-OffersLM).

## 0.3 Cross-cutting rules that recur in several sources

1. [S] Volume first, then skill. Rule of 100 and Open To Goal [Leads: More Better New; Advertising in Real Life]; the flyer test size 300 vs 5 000 [Leads; T-Content ~08:30]; volume × time = skill [T-Content ~42:00]; "he who asks the most times gets the most sales" [T-Sales 01:30].
2. [S] The 3:1 floor. LTGP:CAC at or above 3:1 is the observed pattern where portfolio businesses "take off" [Leads: Paid Ads II], the minimum for cold outreach [Leads: Cold Outreach], the ceiling for total acquisition cost as a third of lifetime profit [Leads: Employees], and the floor for affiliate payouts [Leads: Affiliates]. Status differs (pattern vs rule) but the number does not.
3. [S] Cash inside 30 days. Client-financed acquisition [Leads: Paid Ads II] and the Money Models minimum [MM: Section I] both anchor on the credit-card float. This is the hardest constraint in the library for anyone bootstrapping.
4. [S] Proof before persuasion. "Why should I listen to you" [T-Content ~22:00], evidence over confidence and damaging admissions [T-Content ~34:00–39:00], perceived likelihood of achievement in the value equation [Offers p75–87], proof-promise-plan openings and testimonials for closer conviction [T-Sales].
5. [S] Speed and ease beat more promise. "Fast beats free" and the bottom of the value equation (time delay, effort and sacrifice) is where differentiation lives [Offers p83]; commoditized offers win on speed, risk or ease [T-OffersLM]; response inside 60 seconds [T-Sales 00:03]; a quick win in the first 48 hours [Leads: Customer Referrals; T-CaseSaaS].
6. [S] Give first, ask later, but ask. Give:ask ratios and "give until they ask" [Leads: Post Free Content II] and goodwill compounding [T-Content ~47:00] coexist with injected CTAs that grew the podcast 20× [T-Content ~17:00] and with Money Models' scripted asks. The author's own resolution is by context: give in public, ask in private; integrate the ask after value.
7. [S] Never sell the same thing for less. Change terms (payment plan) or components (feature downsell), never the price mid-sale [T-Sales 02:31; MM: Rules of Downselling]; price discrimination only by tweaking the offer [T-Moats #7].
8. [S] Track or you do not care. Track → Lose → Print [Leads: Paid Ads II]; one test per week per platform, logged [Leads: More Better New]; pilots with pre-defined success statistics on 15 % of customers [T-CaseSaaS]; measure a money model in quarters [MM: Section VI]; record every sales call [T-Sales].
9. [S] Stage discipline. Warm outreach and first-five-free before anything else [Leads: Warm Outreach]; paid ads last [Leads: Paid Ads II]; no money means no agency [Leads: Agencies]; 10–20 affiliates before scaling [Leads: Affiliates]; build one offer of the money model at a time [MM: Section VI]; niche until $10M [Offers p54]. The consolidated stage map is in cluster F.
10. [S] Most businesses degrade with scale; a durable business has one advantage that compounds with scale [T-Moats, recurring]; the referral milestone (≥25 % of customers from referrals at Roadmap Level 4) is the point where product quality becomes the constraint [Leads: The Roadmap].

## 0.4 The tensions that matter most (full list of 23 in SOURCE-MAP.md)

- Delay the ask for years [T-Content; Offers p128] vs cash inside 30 days [MM; Leads]. Reconciled by the author only implicitly: the audience asset was funded by other businesses; a bootstrapped operator "runs out of cash".
- Grand Slam dream outcome [Offers p76] vs lower the promises until close rates drop [Leads: Customer Referrals; T-CaseSaaS]. Offers itself moves the emphasis to the bottom of the value equation.
- Guarantees as "the number one way to increase conversion" [Offers p156] vs "he does not guarantee most of what he sells" and the control-vs-guarantee rule [T-Sales 02:03] vs guarantee as a paid upsell or a removable feature [MM]. Same tool, different positions in the sequence, conditioned by ticket, fulfilment cost and control.
- Premium pricing from entry [Offers p68] vs price low, then raise until revenue falls [MM: Section VI] vs first five free [Leads]. Stage: low while learning, high once reliable.
- One-to-one service businesses deteriorate as customers are added [T-Moats #1] vs the service-scaling advice everywhere else; the author's answer is productizing or one-to-many delivery.
- Direct-to-offer ads (higher ROAS up front) vs lead-magnet ads (higher ROAS over the horizon, scales further) [T-OffersLM]; "try the core offer first" [Leads: Lead Magnets].
- Hard selling is for weak products [MM] vs ask the most times [T-Sales]; the lecture's own limit is to stop when the prospect is better served not buying.

### 1. Market selection and niching

**Core statements**
1. [S] Order of levers: Starving Crowd (market) > Offer Strength > Persuasion Skills. Rate each great/normal/bad; a "great" higher up overpowers everything below; a bad market stops the equation: "you can't be in a 'bad' market, or nothing will work" [Offers p52-53]. Hot-dog parable: with enough demand "you can be mediocre at business, have a terrible offer, and have no ability to persuade people, and you can still make money" [Offers p47].
2. [S] Precondition for the whole Offers book: at least a "normal" market (growing at the marketplace rate) with unmet needs in health, wealth or relationships; in a dying market "nothing in here would have worked" [Offers p48-49]. "We are not trying to create demand. We are trying to channel it." [Offers p48]; the same stance is repeated for desire: "Our goal is not to create desire. It's simply to channel that desire" [Offers p78].
3. [S] Four market indicators: massive pain ("The degree of the pain will be proportional to the price you will be able to charge"; "The pain is the pitch"), purchasing power, easy to target, growing [Offers p49-51]. Variants: "the pain is the pitch" is a team ism inside the sales lecture's pain cycle [T-Sales 01:00]; Leads defines a lead as someone with "the problem to solve and the money to spend" and uses exactly that as the ads-vs-sales diagnostic [Leads: Section I intro; Leads: Employees].
4. [S] Method: pick a subgroup inside health/wealth/relationships that is growing, has buying power and is easy to target [Offers p51]. "Don't be romantic about your audience. Serve the people who can pay you what you're worth." [Offers p48]
5. [S] Commit ("niche slap"): two normal markets, "Either would work, just not both. You must pick one"; hopping restarts trial and error from zero [Offers p53]. Leads' "Size Of The Pie Fallacy": owners who reached $1M-$3M/yr from one platform call the niche saturated when the truth is "I'm not as good at advertising as I thought" [Leads: More Better New].
6. [S] Riches in niches, with a stage threshold: "if you are under $10M per year, niching down will make you more money"; beyond $10M it depends on TAM, then broaden up-market, down-market or adjacent. "If you are at $1M or $3M, thinking you have capped and must expand, you are wrong. You just need to be better." [Offers p54]
7. [S] Niching raises price for the same product: "you can literally charge 100x more for the exact same product" (credited to Dan Kennedy) [Offers p54-55]. Positioning template: "I solve this type of problem for this specific type of person in this unique counter-intuitive way that reverses their deepest fear." [Offers p56]
8. [S] Sell better customers: find what the most successful customers share and "sell only" people meeting those criteria; narrower callouts made leads cheaper and referrals arrive "like clockwork" [Leads: Customer Referrals, six ways #1]. Ads: narrow the haystack first, then expand puddle, pond, lake, ocean "as big as possible while still turning a profit" [Leads: Paid Ads I]. Local callout rule "LOCAL AREA + TYPE OF PERSON" [Leads: Paid Ads I]; naming rule "Not Baltimore but Towson, MD. Not Chicago, but Hinsdale" [Offers p175].
9. [S] Content follows the same ladder: a small local business "probably shouldn't make general business content. Not at first"; be king of the puddle, then pond, lake, ocean [Leads: Content II lesson 3]. Keynote variant: become a "niche-specific authority" where "you compete against 20 guys" and walk the talk; sequence Gym Secrets podcast, Gym Launch Secrets book, then $100M Offers [T-Content 30:00-34:00].
10. [S] Segment to the wallet: be clear on "the one or two customer segments or avatars you really serve" and segment them "so you can sell to the right wallet" [T-Moats #7]. If the premium version (faster, less risky, easier) cannot be sold to current customers, "go up-market to wealthier customers who absolutely value speed, risk and ease" [T-OffersLM 01:40]. Moat form: own "a small network of a very small niche", or dominate a small area and expand outward [T-Moats #13; T-Moats zoom-out].
11. [S] Warnings: "A grand slam offer given to the wrong audience will fall on deaf ears" [Offers p44]; "If your offer doesn't work, it doesn't mean you suck. It means your offer sucks"; keep the assessment skill so "when lightning strikes, you can make sure it strikes twice" [Offers p56-57].

**Decision rules, formulas, thresholds**
- Market > offer > persuasion; normal market + Grand Slam Offer is the book's target case ("tons of money even if you're bad at persuasion") [Offers p52].
- Niche until $10M/yr; $10M/yr = top 0.4% of businesses (1 in 250) [Offers p54, p31]. Single-niche companies reached $30M+/yr (chiropractors, gyms, plumbers, solar, roofers, salons) [Offers p54].
- Niche pricing ladder: generic time management $19; for sales professionals $99; B2B outbound reps $499 (25×); power-tools and gardening outbound reps $1,000-$2,000; weight loss $19 generic vs $1,997 for shift nurses [Offers p55-56].
- Reach cost and fatigue: ~$20 per 1,000 people; a 200,000-person area ≈ $10,000 to reach everyone once; "the smaller the radius, the faster offers fatigue" [Offers p172, p180].
- Typical single-platform plateau: $1M-$3M/yr [Leads: More Better New].

**When / when not, and stage**
- Under $10M: narrow. Over $10M: TAM decides; broaden up, down or adjacent [Offers p54].
- Local businesses: hyper-local avatar in names and callouts [Offers p175; Leads: Paid Ads I]; local marketing is "both easier and harder": trust in the familiar converts a higher share in person, but the TAM is the radius, so offers fatigue fast and wrappers must rotate [Offers p180]; seasonal urgency is "my number one strategy for local businesses" [Offers p144].
- Starting content: narrow to what you do and where; "you will not gain a mega following at first, but you will accomplish, which will set you on the path to be broader later" [Leads: Content II lesson 3; T-Content 32:00].
- Enterprise and government buyers (6-12-month committee cycles): most close-now sales techniques "not going to apply" [T-Sales 02:05].

**Author's examples with numbers** [E]
- Covid toilet paper sold at $100+ per roll with no offer, no pitch [Offers p47]. Lloyd's newspaper software: great product, zero-risk revshare, market shrinking 25%/yr, he downsized; pivot to mask manufacturing → "millions per month" within five months. "Same entrepreneur. Different market." [Offers p47-48]
- Resume system sold to the unemployed: no purchasing power [Offers p50]. Rich doctors wanted, ads served to nursing students [Offers p50]. Relationship coaching: seniors beat college students on pain, money, targetability and growth [Offers p51-52].
- Gym Launch avatar: microgym owner, ~100 members, signed lease, at least one employee, weight-loss focus; personal trainers and online coaches turned down "for product focus and high-converting messaging" [Offers p54].
- PR company: lowest-churn customers were 15% of the book, all in one fundraising niche; refocusing callouts on them broke a years-long plateau, "on track to adding millions per month" [Leads: Customer Referrals].
- Chiropractor-niche agency at $2M/yr on one platform called a $15.1B industry saturated while a peer spent "$30k in advertising, across four platforms, last week" [Leads: More Better New].

**Connections and tensions**
- [I] Offers caps niching at $10M by TAM; Leads places the typical plateau at $1M-$3M and blames single-channel advertising. Both say the same thing below $10M: the fix is skill (offer, advertising), not a wider market.
- [I] "advertising lets you have a terrible product, be terrible at sales... and still make money" [Leads: Section I intro] fits the Offers hierarchy: volume of advertising plays the role of the "great market" case, buying chances while the offer is still bad.
- Tension (open): T-OffersLM says go up-market when the premium cannot be sold to current customers [T-OffersLM 01:40]; Offers says commit to the niche, "you just need to be better" [Offers p53-54]. The author gives no ordering; [I] the up-market move is a segment shift inside one market category, not a market hop.
- [S] The author links niche authority to money himself: the gym-growth content "made a lot of money and didn't make me famous at all" [T-Content 39:00].

**Not covered by the sources**
- No method for measuring market growth or TAM; no quantitative test of purchasing power beyond "they have the money, or access to it".

### 2. Offers

**Core statements**
1. [S] Offer = "the goods and services you agree to give or provide, how you accept payment, and the terms of the agreement"; "the lifeblood of your business" [Offers p26]. Quality ladder: no offer → no business; bad → negative profit; decent → stagnation; good → some profit; Grand Slam → "Fantastic profit. Insane business. Freedom." [Offers p27]. Leads variant: offer = "what you promise to give in exchange for something of value"; core offer = product or service for money; "Advertising your core offer might be all you need... Try this way first" [Leads: Offers and Lead Magnets].
2. [S] Commodity problem: "Commoditized = Price Driven Purchases (race to the bottom). Differentiated = Value Driven Purchases (sell in a category of one with no comparison)." The prospect who thinks "these are pretty much the same, I'll buy the cheaper one" has commoditized you; commodities settle at "just enough to keep the lights on" [Offers p39]. The two root problems (not enough clients, not enough cash) interlock through price competition [Offers p27-28].
3. [S] Grand Slam Offer = "an offer you present to the marketplace that cannot be compared to any other product or service available, combining an attractive promotion, an unmatchable value proposition, a premium price, and an unbeatable guarantee with a money model (payment terms) that allows you to get paid to get new customers" [Offers p39]. Same eyeballs, three outcomes: more response, more conversion, premium price [Offers p40]. "a business does the same work in both cases... The fulfillment is the same"; the offer makes the prospect "re-calibrate" their value meter [Offers p40].
4. [S] 2025 workshop variant of the commodity fix: where prospects can easily compare (explicitly home services: kitchen remodelling, roofing, concrete) you must win on at least one of three vectors, faster, less risky or easier; "focus on one and hammer it"; there is always a way on each; and raise prices to reflect the premium because you are no longer a commodity [T-OffersLM 00:25-02:20, 12:37]. Speed is "one of the first places I look" [T-OffersLM 01:20]. Ease = pre-work done for the customer in intake and onboarding; so few do it that customers "almost always appreciate and recognise it" [T-OffersLM 03:10].
5. [S] Fourth lever: wrap what you already sell in a Money Models attraction offer (giveaway, win your money back, decoy, pay less now or pay more later, buy X get Y free) when the complaint is "if I could just get cheaper leads" [T-OffersLM 04:03].
6. [S] Five-step creation: (1) dream outcome ("I wasn't selling the plane flight. I was selling the vacation"); (2) list every problem in the order the customer meets it, before, during and after, in "insane detail", each problem carrying four negatives that match the four value drivers; (3) turn each into a named solution ("how to" + reversed problem); (4) list every delivery vehicle, including ones you refuse to do; (5a) trim; (5b) stack [Offers p96-116]. "if only one of these needs is missing in a solution, it can cause someone not to buy" [Offers p100-101]; "one single item becomes the reason someone doesn't buy" [Offers p111]. "Don't get romantic about how you want to solve the problem. Find a way to solve every problem a prospect presents with." [Offers p112]
7. [S] Delivery cube, six dimensions: attention (1-on-1 / small group / one-to-many); effort expected (DIY / DWY / DFY); live medium (in person, phone, email, text, Zoom, chat); recorded format (audio, video, written); response speed and window (24/7, 9-5, 5 min, 1 h, 24 h); the 10× and 1/10th test [Offers p110-111]. Money Models restates the same axes as a feature ladder for down- and upselling: availability, days, hours, session length, location, cancellation terms, response and delivery speed, service ratio, channel, provider qualification, live vs recorded, in-person vs remote, DIY/DWY/DFY, expirations, personalisation, guarantee length/coverage/terms [MM: Feature Downsells].
8. [S] Trim: remove high-cost/low-value first, then low-cost/low-value; keep low-cost/high-value and high-cost/high-value; test each item against the four drivers [Offers p112]. "If there's one type of delivery vehicle to focus on, it's creating high value, 'one to many' solutions" (largest gap between cost and value; "This is exactly why software becomes so valuable"); keep 1-on-1 "for big value adds only" [Offers p113].
9. [S] Stack: Problem → solution wording → "sexier" bundle name with an ascribed value → delivery vehicles; the bundle (1) solves all perceived problems, (2) gives you conviction, (3) makes comparison impossible, so prospects "will now only make a value-based rather than a price-based decision" [Offers p114-117].
10. [S] Sales-to-fulfillment continuum: the less you do, the harder to sell; the more you do, the easier to sell and the harder to fulfil. "Create flow. Monetize flow. Then add friction." Over-deliver first, then use cash flow to build systems that deliver the same value at lower cost: "this is how businesses beat one another" [Offers p106-107]. "My promise was fundamentally the same: I will fill your gym in 30 days. It was simply the how and what I did that changed." [Offers p107]
11. [S] Four offer types: Attraction (strangers → customers), Upsell (more cash), Downsell (yes instead of no), Continuity (keep buying); "Combining offers in a sequence makes a Money Model"; "When I look at my most profitable businesses, I used all four" [MM: Four Types]. Fifteen named offers: attraction = Win Your Money Back, Giveaways, Decoy, Buy X Get Y Free, Pay Less Now or Pay More Later; upsell = Classic, Menu, Anchor, Rollover; downsell = Payment Plan, Trial With Penalty, Feature; continuity = Bonus, Discount, Waived Fee [MM: Sections II-V]. Ground rules: any offer works alone and in any order; give refunds when asked; "Hard selling is for weak products"; obey advertising law, which tightens "especially when it comes to 'free'"; "Be transparent... Don't lie" [MM: Four Types].
12. [S] Upsell logic: "When an offer solves a problem, another appears. You upsell the solution to the problem your offer reveals"; "Often, upsells make the majority of the profit" [MM: Section III intro]. Two universal upsells: more of what they just bought, or more help with it; uptake is highest immediately after purchase, because buying comes in identity-shift "hyper buying" windows, not at intervals [T-CaseSaaS 08:40-11:55; MM: Classic Upsell]. Downsell logic: "any offer you make after someone says 'no' is a downsell"; two levers, how they pay and what they get; "the offer is never the same stuff for cheaper" [MM: Section IV intro; Downsell Conclusion]. "It's less about having 100 products to offer, and more about having 100 ways to offer your product." [MM: Section VI]
13. [S] Presentation: every business has a three-pillar pitch "buried in it"; humans think in threes; the product gets about two minutes of the call [T-Sales 01:03-01:07]. Splinter offer: pull component B out of an A-B-C-D offer, give it free or at 90% off; once they have B they want A, C, D [T-OffersLM 11:00].
14. [S] Stability: "Once you've monetized an offer, rarely should you change it. Just rinse and repeat"; change the machine "only as a last resort and for a darn good reason, especially once you get traction" [Offers p179-180]. Fatigue is handled by changing wrappers, lightest first: creative → copy → headline → duration → enhancer → monetization structure [Offers p178-179].

**Decision rules, formulas, thresholds**
- Problem inventory: 16 core problems × 2-4 sub-problems = 32-64 problems [Offers p98].
- 10×/1/10th test per problem [Offers p111]. Speed prompt: "what would it take to get this outcome for a customer in one-third the time?", then "can we sell that to customers?": yes = "we have a winner", no = go up-market [T-OffersLM 01:20-01:40].
- Attraction-offer fit: service where the customer must act → Win Your Money Back; transactional → Decoy; Buy X Get Y Free "in so many different versions" [T-OffersLM 04:53]. Downsell fit: Pay Less Now for physical products and one-time services; Trial With Penalty for recurring, and only where the customer must do work [MM: Trial With Penalty].
- Anchor at 5-10× first; main offer keeps the primary features at about 1/5 the price [MM: Anchor Upsell]. Bundle priced equal to the pricier single option (Economist play) [MM: Menu Upsell]. Remove features from highest to lowest value; 1-10 temperature check after two downsells, 8+ → payment plan, 7 or below → recombine [MM: Feature Downsells].
- "If you try one hundred offers, I promise you will succeed" [Offers p56]; "Figuring out what works best may take up to a year"; "Measure in quarters, not weeks" [MM: Section VI].

**When / when not, and stage**
- First Grand Slam Offer: "over-deliver like crazy. Maybe flying out isn't such a bad idea in the beginning"; DFY first, then DWY and one-to-many once cash flows [Offers p105-107]. "Your first true grand slam offer should be able to get you to your first $100,000." [Offers p187]
- Money model stage rule: "none of my businesses started with a fully forged Money Model. They all start at Stage I. Even Acquisition.com!"; perfect one offer until reliable, then the next; "Do not try and implement a full Money Model at once. It will break your business." [MM: Section VI]. Continuity last, because as an attraction offer it "crashes 30-day profits" [MM: Continuity Conclusion].
- Direct core-offer ads have higher ROAS up front and are "for sure" right if you only want a smaller amount of money; for "huge higher realms" soften the ask with a lead magnet and build the conversion process around it [T-OffersLM 05:42-08:15]. Leads: try the core offer first; a lead magnet when people want to know more before buying, "common for businesses that sell more expensive stuff" [Leads: Offers and Lead Magnets].
- Enhancers "cannot make a business" [Offers p169]; pricing "can't (and shouldn't) overcome a terrible product" [MM: Waived Fee].

**Author's examples with numbers** [E]
- Agency: old offer $1,000 down + $1,000/mo retainer, 0.5-1 ROAS, 5 clients, break-even at 30 days. Grand Slam version ("Pay one time... Just cover ad spend... only pay me if people show up... 20 people in your first month, or you get your next month free", plus the full playbook) → 2.5× response × 2.5× close × 4× up-front price = 22.4×: "you spent $10,000 to make $112,000" [Offers p41-44].
- Weight-loss stack: seven named bundles, $1,000 + $600 + $500 + $699 + $199 + $1,000 + $349 = $4,351, "All for only $599"; facilities now sell it for $2,400-$5,200 [Offers p115-116]. Meal-plan Excel app: ~100 hours to build, ~15 minutes per plan, used by 4,000+ gyms [Offers p113-114].
- Early Gym Launch DFY: 21 days on site, $500 refundable deposit, ~$100,000/mo up-front cash solo, a team of eight; teaching instead earns "maybe a third" per gym but serves hundreds of gyms a month [Offers p106-107].
- Decoy: free (one session/week) vs "Ultimate" $399 → ~8 of 10 chose $399 [MM: Decoy]. Storage "free month" = $127 per customer; rental car $19/day → $100/day through five offers; burger profit $0.25 → $2.00 (8×) → $3.00 with supersize [MM: Start Here; Section I; Section III intro].
- Gym Launch money model built in stages: Decoy → $476,000/mo in three months; $42,000/yr upsell with payment-plan and continuity downsells → ~$1,500,000/mo; menu and feature downsells → $2,300,000/mo within 14 months; plus Prestige Labs → $4,400,000/mo by month 20 [MM: Section VI].
- SaaS case: re-scripting the sales call to promise less "sold the same amount"; ascension 20% → 48% after one-to-one onboarding [T-CaseSaaS 07:27, 16:32].

**Connections and tensions**
- [I] Component (e) of the Grand Slam Offer, the money model "that allows you to get paid to get new customers" [Offers p39], is the thesis of Money Models; MM calls Win Your Money Back "the first Grand Slam Offer that Gym Launch taught" without redefining the term [MM: WYMB].

### 3. Value equation

**Core statements**
1. [S] Four drivers as a division: Dream Outcome and Perceived Likelihood of Achievement on top (increase), Time Delay and Effort & Sacrifice below (decrease); figure missing, [I] rendering Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice) [Offers p75-76]. They are the father's four questions [Offers p75-76]. Leads imports them unchanged as the script "I help (ideal customer) get (dream outcome) in (time period) without (effort and sacrifice) and (increase perceived likelihood)" and pairs each with its opposite (Nightmare, Risk, Speed, Ease) for ads [Leads: Warm Outreach step 6; Paid Ads I].
2. [S] Hardest and most differentiating: "larger-than-life claims are the easiest to establish (and therefore less unique)... The harder, and more competitive, are the Time Delay and Effort & Sacrifice"; "Get the bottom to zero", since bottom = 0 makes value infinite [Offers p76-77]. "people pay for certainty" [Offers p80-81]. Variants: three vectors for comparable offers, faster, less risky, easier, "focus on one and hammer it", speed first [T-OffersLM 00:25-01:20, 12:37]; a refused free offer usually fails on hidden costs, "your 'free' stuff is too expensive" [Leads: Warm Outreach step 7].
3. [S] Perception is what counts: unperceived improvements "are not valuable" (the Underground's next-train map beat faster trains); prefer psychological to logical solutions; "it takes great marketing to sell the same product for a premium" [Offers p77-78]. SaaS: a perceived support gap vanished once a chat bubble made existing support reachable [T-CaseSaaS 17:08].
4. [S] Dream outcome: channel desire, do not create it; the status-raising outcome is valued most, framed from others' viewpoint [Offers p78-80]. Same desire served → dream outcome cancels and the other three variables decide "$50,000 and another $5" [Offers p80]. Sales lecture: "Sell the vacation", Maui not the flight; the product gets about two minutes of the call, the rest goes to the prospect's pains [T-Sales 01:06-01:07]. "The pain is the pitch": deprivation creates motivation; prior attempts "are an advantage for you because they are here, which means it didn't work" [T-Sales 01:00-01:03].
5. [S] Likelihood is carried by messaging, proof, what the offer includes or excludes, and guarantees [Offers p81]; Leads: proof and guarantees "are huge" [Leads: Warm Outreach step 6]. Deliver value before the ask: how dialled the business is "before you ask for money" is a huge boost; "The best way to get someone to expect value in the future is to provide value today" [T-CaseSaaS 07:45-08:40]. Lower promises raise belief: lowering expectations "can increase perceived likelihood of achievement because the promise becomes believable"; re-scripted reps "sold the same amount" [T-CaseSaaS 16:32-16:50]; be specific on the negatives so you can be vaguer on the positives [T-LeadMagnets 12:22].
6. [S] Time delay: outcome plus a short-term experience that keeps them, "a big emotional win early" [Offers p81-82]. "Fast beats free": "The only thing that beats 'free' is 'fast'"; "if you find yourself in a market competing against free, double down on speed" [Offers p83]. Variants: 48-hour impression, seven small wins, "updates are wins", +50% timelines [Leads: Customer Referrals, six ways #4].
7. [S] Effort and sacrifice: ancillary costs; "done for you services are almost always more expensive than do-it-yourself", effort removed plus likelihood "if an expert does it" [Offers p83-85]. Ease = pre-work in intake and onboarding [T-OffersLM 03:10]; "too techy" = effort, grade the reading level of every page and script [T-CaseSaaS 15:07, 18:50]; monthly fix-the-most-common-problem loop [Leads: Customer Referrals, six ways #5].
8. [S] Scoring 0/1 per driver (Meditation vs Xanax; supplements $123B vs clubs $62B); "You can either be right or you can be rich"; monetize what people value to give them what they need [Offers p85-87]. The equation is the recurring test: four negatives per problem, likelihood "the most unique and service-specific" [Offers p98]; "Tools & checklists beat additional trainings... The value equation still reigns supreme" [Offers p149]; cold outreach needs "Big Fast Value" [Leads: Cold Outreach, Problem #2].
9. [S] SaaS complaints mapped: "too techy" = effort; "not enough support" = likelihood that "dropped on contact with reality"; "not soon enough" = time delay, "the nightmare version"; fix by adding to the back end or setting expectations up front [T-CaseSaaS 14:33-17:08]. The money objection is the equation from the buyer's side: "nothing is ever too expensive, it's just not worth it" [T-Sales 01:46].

**Decision rules, formulas, thresholds**
- [I] Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice); bottom → 0 = infinite [Offers p75-77]. Charge up to 100× fulfilment cost, still a steal [Offers p73-74].
- Competing against free: double down on speed [Offers p83].
- Fast-win targets: ads live and first $2,000 sale in seven days [Offers p82]; the first 24-48 hours fix the impression [Leads: Customer Referrals; T-CaseSaaS 03:22].
- 10×/1/10th test per problem [Offers p111]; one-third-the-time, then "can we sell that?" [T-OffersLM 01:20-01:40].
- Refused free offer: outcome, likelihood or hidden costs; ask "why?" [Leads: Warm Outreach step 7]. Lower promises until close rates lower, then stop [Leads: Customer Referrals, six ways #2].

**When / when not, and stage**
- Early career the top, later the bottom [Offers p76]. First offer: over-deliver, DFY first (topic 2) [Offers p105-107].
- Comparable markets (home services): win on one vector; premium unsellable to current customers → up-market [T-OffersLM 00:25-01:40].
- Customer must act → price must sting (topic 4) [Offers p67]. Post-launch expectation gaps show as churn, low CSAT, low upsell take-up; pilot fixes on 15% [T-CaseSaaS 00:28, 03:57].

**Author's examples with numbers** [E]
- Photography: ticket $300 → $1,500, profit $1,000/wk → $38,000/wk (38×) [Offers p74].
- Liposuction $25,000 in an afternoon vs a $100/mo bootcamp [Offers p83-84].
- Gym owners who ran ads and sold in the first seven days had 3× LTGP [Leads: Customer Referrals, six ways #3].
- SaaS: one-to-one onboarding restating Clarify, Label, Overview and re-selling the vacation moved ascension 20% → 48%; ALAN tied next steps to the stated "why", doubling or tripling follow-through [T-CaseSaaS 05:39, 07:27].
- Website speed test: 9 s → 3 s = +18% conversion at 3%/second [T-LeadMagnets 11:06].

**Connections and tensions**
- Tension (topic 2 in equation terms): "solve every problem", "over-deliver like crazy" [Offers p105, p112] vs lower promises until close rates lower [Leads: Customer Referrals] and "lowering expectations can increase perceived likelihood" [T-CaseSaaS 16:50]. [I] Deliverables sit on the bottom, promises on top; the author maximises the first and calibrates the second; the re-script that "sold the same amount" is his evidence that calibrating is free.
- Tension (open): "Fast beats free" [Offers p83] vs "free gets 10× the interest of not free" [T-Moats #12] and "the greatest discount of all being free" [MM: Section II intro]. Offers' condition is a market already competing against free; MM's free targets strangers; no ordering given.
- [S] Author's own links: Leads "assumes you already have a Grand Slam Offer" [Leads: Section I intro]; the SaaS case names the equation and CLOSER together [T-CaseSaaS 04:41, 07:45].

**Not covered by the sources**
- No way to measure perceived value or weight the drivers beyond 0/1 scoring; no case isolating which driver moved conversion, except the SaaS complaint mapping.

### 4. Pricing

**Core statements**
1. [S] People buy when value received > price paid and stop the moment it dips below [Offers p61]. Lowering price is "most of the time, the wrong decision"; compete on price only with costs at 1/10th of competitors [Offers p62]. Raise price only after raising value [Offers p62]; once you win on speed, risk or ease, "raise prices to reflect the premium, because you are no longer a commoditised product" [T-OffersLM 00:55].
2. [S] The typical process (market average, slightly below, "more for less") copies competitors who "are dead broke"; the bottom 10-20% wash out each cycle [Offers p63-64].
3. [S] Virtuous cycle: cutting price lowers emotional investment, perceived value and results, attracts the worst clients, destroys margin; raising it reverses each [Offers p64-66]. "People want to buy expensive things. They just need a reason" [Offers p66]. If the customer must DO something, price so "it stings a little"; but "Your product must deliver" [Offers p67]. "99 percent of businesses need to raise their prices to grow, not lower them. Profit is oxygen." [Offers p69].
4. [S] Price to the wallet: niching lets you charge 100× for the same product (topic 1) [Offers p54-56]. Small-business price discrimination: know the one or two segments "so you can sell to the right wallet"; never the same thing at two prices, "tweak the offer slightly" (a free cocktail) so it is defensible if buyers compare; "especially works well with services" [T-Moats #7].
5. [S] Raising over time: "Never raise your prices without letting people know"; announcing the rise brings "a nice little influx of cash" [Offers p144]. MM: price new offers cheap, then keep raising "until you cannot make up for the nos with the extra cash you make from the yeses" [MM: Section VI]. Leads' ladder for any new product: first five free (use it, feedback, review); when referrals start, charge 80% off the next five, then 60%, then 40%; +20% every five until the sweet spot [Leads: Warm Outreach steps 7, 9].
6. [S] Never cut the core price; move something else. "never discount the main offer. It teaches your customers that your prices are negotiable" [Offers p148]. MM: "Customers talk about price", test prices deliberately [MM: Rules of Downselling]; "People who demand to pay less for the same thing are business terrorists"; pay less now → payment plan, pay less overall → feature downsell [MM: Feature Downsells]; "You just can't offer the same thing for less" [MM: Section IV intro]. Lecture: never change price mid-sale, only terms or components; counter to "can you do it for less?": "we could do it for more" [T-Sales 02:30-02:32]. "That's expensive" is "a voiced reaction, not an objection", say "yeah, totally" and continue [T-Sales 02:29].
7. [S] Payment plans are terms: "'it costs too much' really means 'this costs too much up front'"; present prepayment as the discount; seven steps from most up-front to most over time; "If you give people the option to pay slower, they will pay slower" [MM: Payment Plan Downsells]. Profitwell: monthly billing 10.7% monthly churn, quarterly 5%, annual 2%, so start high [MM: Payment Plan Downsells]. A referral can be the term: $500 price, $400 wanted, discount only for three introductions [Leads: Customer Referrals, seven ways #4].
8. [S] Anchor: premium at 5-10× first, "The Gasp", then the main offer with the primary features at a fifth of the price; "If you treat the anchor like a fake, so will the customer" [MM: Anchor Upsell]. Menu: three options, bundle priced equal to the pricier single option [MM: Menu Upsell]. Small-medium-large: $5/$8/$9 pushes large, $6/$7/$9 pushes medium [MM: Classic Upsell]. Made-up bonus values "won't anchor the customer and you'll lose trust" [MM: Continuity Bonus].
9. [S] Discount framings. Strangers "absolutely understand the price", so discounts make anything a deal, "the greatest discount of all being free"; free, discount and $1 are one continuum [MM: Section II intro]. Four framings of one saving: 25% off, $300 off, 3 months free, "One Year For $900 ($1,200)" [MM: Decoy]. Buy X Get Y Free: "Permanently raise prices to accommodate the discount. Don't lie."; more free than paid [MM: BXGY]; $100 × 12 becomes buy four get eight at $300 each [T-OffersLM 05:11]. Giveaway discount = 10-30% of gross margin [MM: Giveaways]. Pay-now discount 20-50% plus bonuses [MM: Pay Less Now]. Splinter rule: 10-20% discounts "no point", 80-90% changes behaviour [T-OffersLM 12:14].
10. [S] Continuity pricing, tested against $199/mo: standalone at 1.33× → 50% choose continuity; 1.66× → 60%; 2× → 70%; 2.33× → 80%; 2.66× → 90%; "People pay 33% more to avoid continuity" [MM: Continuity Bonus]. Bill every four weeks: +8.3% revenue, +41% profit at 20% margin; "plus a 3% processing fee": +30% profit at 10% margin [MM: Continuity Discount]. Waived Fee: setup fee 3-5× the monthly rate (1.5-3× for more up-front cash), waived for a year or more; cancellation fee = the discount received [MM: Waived Fee; Continuity Discount].
11. [S] Downgrading is priced deliberately: a lower price for only the features they use; such customers "have the second highest value" [MM: Feature Downsells]; proactively downgraded customers "never cancel", ten months at half price beats two at full [T-CaseSaaS 39:41]. Affiliate buy-in: 10-20% of first-year affiliate earnings ($40,000 → $4,000-$8,000) [Leads: Affiliates step 3].

**Decision rules, formulas, thresholds**
- Compete on price only at 1/10th cost [Offers p62]. Never raise silently [Offers p144]. Raise until revenue falls [MM: Section VI]. First five free, then 80/60/40% off per five, then +20% every five [Leads: Warm Outreach].
- Rollover: next offer ≥ 4× the credit; gift card $200 for $20, card = 20% of the next offer, limit two, only for others [MM: Rollover Upsell].
- Payment-plan diagnostic: closes up but paid-in-full count must not fall; 1-10 check, 8+ → plan, 7 or below → features [MM: Payment Plan Downsells]. Demand fractal: one fifth pay 5× [Offers p127].

**When / when not, and stage**
- Premium presupposes delivery [Offers p67]; pricing "can't (and shouldn't) overcome a terrible product" [MM: Waived Fee].
- New product: free first five, charge when referrals start [Leads: Warm Outreach]; new money-model offer: cheap, then raise [MM: Section VI].
- Wallet pricing by tweaking the offer, especially services [T-Moats #7]; premium unsellable to current customers → up-market [T-OffersLM 01:40].
- Costly no-shows (doctors, lawyers, dentists) → discount rather than free [MM: Decoy]. BXGY only if you can manage a year's cash arriving in a month [MM: BXGY].
- Pay Less Now = physical products and one-time services; Trial With Penalty = recurring [MM: Trial With Penalty].
- Waived fee: commitments of a year or more, slow-to-work services [MM: Waived Fee].

**Author's examples with numbers** [E]
- Gym Launch entered at 3× the highest competitor ($5,000) and 32× the lowest ($500): $16,000 for 16 weeks; 35% upsold to $42,000/yr × 3 years; average gym-owner take-home $35,280/yr [Offers p68]. Father pitch: $239,000 for $42,000 [Offers p60].
- Argue $10M → $23M run rate "just from changing our pricing, our packaging" [Offers p5-6].
- Licence by accident: "$6000" quoted to scare a gym owner off, he said yes; next calls $8,000, then $10,000 [Leads: How I Got Here].
- Anchor: $16,000 suit → $2,200 plus $300 accessories on a $500 budget [MM: Anchor Upsell]. Economist $59 / $125 / $125 [MM: Menu Upsell]. Decoy: free vs $399, ~8 of 10 chose $399 [MM: Decoy]. Boot Factory: $200 boots as "Buy one pair for $600, get two pairs free" [MM: BXGY].

**Connections and tensions**
- Tension (stage): Offers prices at a premium from entry [Offers p68] vs MM "new offers cheap at first... until you make less money" [MM: Section VI] and Leads' free-first-five ladder [Leads: Warm Outreach]. Partly resolved: Offers conditions the premium on "having delivered many times" [Offers p67]; Leads' ladder is for a brand-new product. [I] All three raise price with proof; they differ on the starting point.
- Tension: "never discount the main offer" [Offers p148] vs attraction offers discounted up to 100% [MM: Section II intro] and 80-90% splinter discounts [T-OffersLM 12:14]. Author's boundaries: "Permanently raise prices to accommodate the discount" [MM: BXGY]; what expires is the promotion, not the service [Offers p144]. [I] The core price is held; discounts live in wrappers, components and terms.
- Tension (open): T-OffersLM "10-20% discounts, no point" vs MM's giveaway discount of 10-30% of gross margin framed against an anchored prize [MM: Giveaways].
- [I] One stance across 2021, 2024, 2025: never negotiate ("negotiable", "terrorists", "customers talk") [Offers p148; T-Sales 02:30; MM: Feature Downsells]. The price-value gap [Offers p61] is Leads' "goodwill" [Leads: Customer Referrals]: the purchase driver in 2021, the referral driver in 2023.

**Not covered by the sources**
- No method to locate the ceiling other than raise-until-revenue-falls; nothing on list-priced, regulated or procurement-driven markets, currency, tax or regional differences.

### 5. Guarantees and risk reversal

**Core statements**
1. [S] "The single greatest objection for any product or service being sold is... risk"; reversing it is "the number one way to increase the conversion of an offer"; Fladlien: conversion 2-4× "simply by changing the quality of the guarantee" [Offers p156, p169]. Leads calls them "huge" for perceived likelihood [Leads: Warm Outreach step 6]. "You must always hit your guarantee hard, even if you don't have one. Say it boldly and give the reason why." [Offers p156-157]
2. [S] Structure with teeth: "If you do not get X result in Y time period, we will Z" [Offers p158]. Name it ("Club a Baby Seal Guarantee") [Offers p161]. Method: reverse what they are most afraid of; "The more specific and creative the guarantee is, the better" [Offers p169]. 2025 risk vector: warranty, guarantee, or the "personal favourite", an insurance policy bought against the customer's biggest fear ("we cover damages up to X"), premium priced in; risk is one of the three levers that de-commoditise an offer [T-OffersLM 00:25, 02:22-03:00].
3. [S] Refund math: 130% sales with refunds doubling from 5% to 10% gives 117 net vs 95, 1.23×; "Don't be emotional, just do the math" [Offers p157]. Warnings: guarantee buyers "can become very shitty customers", tie it to required actions [Offers p157]; high fulfilment cost → conditional or anti-guarantee [Offers p157-158]; a guarantee covering a poor sales team or product "will backfire into lots of refunds" [Offers p169].
4. [S] Four types [Offers p158-159]. Unconditional: strongest, effectively a trial, "a LOT more people buy". Conditional: "the ones you can get VERY creative on", key success actions as conditions; ideally all qualify and none claim, since most "will take the outcome". Anti-guarantee: "all sales are final" with a reason why; consumables and things that diminish once given. Implied: performance structures, "if I don't perform, I don't get paid"; needs transparent measurement and control over collection. Fit: broad guarantees for lower-ticket B2C, specific ones as ticket and business orientation rise [Offers p162]. Stacking: unconditional plus conditional, or two conditionals on sequential outcomes, which "shifts the burden of risk back from them onto us" [Offers p159-160].
5. [S] Named guarantees, condition and fit [Offers p160-169]: (a) No Questions Asked refund: simplest, "very risky". (b) Satisfaction-based refund: "highest form", lower ticket, only if you fulfil well. (c) Outsized refund (double, triple, fixed sum): high-margin products, with a consumption condition; "typically outperforms a 30-day money-back". (d) Service guarantee, work free until X: "my personal favorite guarantee of all time", money never at risk; no client has ever taken it up. (e) Modified service guarantee, another period free, ≥ 2× the original, "an easier place for you to start". (f) Credit-based: best in an upsell. (g) Personal service guarantee, "a service guarantee on crack", needs contingencies. (h) Hotel and airfare perks for workshops. (i) Wage-payment for a worthless session, tax return ÷ 1,960 hours. (j) Release of service: if you never enforce contracts "you have nothing to lose". (k) Delayed second payment until the first outcome: needs a systematised first result, focuses the team on activation. (l) First outcome, ancillary costs paid until it. (m) Anti-guarantee "all sales are final" for un-seeable IP and high-ticket customisation: "you are not the type of person we want to work with"; the reason why, "Just make one up that sounds compelling". (n) Implied: per-sale fees, 10% of top line, 20% profit share, 25% of growth from baseline, ratchets 10/20/30%; "one of, if not THE most desirable setup"; drawbacks "tracking and collection". He has used all except (h) and (i) [Offers p169].
6. [S] Anti-guarantee and control: in the lecture he "doesn't guarantee most of what he sells"; close, "is it fair to guarantee work I have no input in?"; "the more control you have over the outcome, the more you can guarantee"; takeaway sale, "I can't eat the food or work out for you" [T-Sales 02:03]. Offers' own weight-loss guarantee drew the same line, "I can't eat the food for you" [Offers p161-162].
7. [S] Guarantee in the close: best case / worst case, "worst case six weeks of free service and your money back if you hate us"; guarantees "boil the decision down so low it's a simple yes/no" [T-Sales 01:47-01:49]. Same close in 2021 [Offers p162]. Outreach and content-ask scripts carry "or I work with them until they do" [Leads: Warm Outreach step 6; Content II].
8. [S] Conditions = the actions that produce results: "if you know the key actions for success, make them the conditions" [Offers p159]; "match the conditions of your guarantee to the actions that get the best results" [Leads: Customer Referrals, six ways #3]. MM operationalises it: WYMB criteria (results, actions, or both; easy to track, realistic, "advertises the business") [MM: WYMB]; Trial With Penalty as the inverse, pay only on missed terms [MM: Trial With Penalty].
9. [S] Win Your Money Back: "the first Grand Slam Offer that Gym Launch taught"; "Only use a Win Your Money Back Offer if your refund rate is below 5%. Otherwise, fix your product before doing this"; observed "about 10% of all customers will ask for their money back"; store credit converts as well as cash; pairing with an unconditional satisfaction guarantee "never materially affected" refund requests; spread the credit ($600 → $50/mo off for 12 months) [MM: WYMB]. Decoys carry no guarantee; the premium carries "results or repeat free" [MM: Decoy].
10. [S] Guarantee as a priced component. Upsell: "add 5-50% onto the price in exchange for a guarantee", warranties and insurance alike [MM: Classic Upsell]. Downsell: a high-ticket seller tripled close rate 25% → 75% by removing the money-back guarantee ("you can pay less. Or, you can keep your money-back guarantee, which would you prefer?"); many keep it, so full-price buyers and up-front cash rose too; "If you're okay without a guarantee, I can knock off $400" [MM: Feature Downsells; Ten Years].
11. [S] Ground rules: "If a customer asks for their money back, give it back... get better customers"; "Don't take blood money" [MM: Four Types; WYMB]; roll over before refunding [MM: Rollover Upsell]. Stage: "Start selling service-based guarantees or setting up performance partnerships. This will make all sales final... From there, either keep that guarantee and scale (perfectly fine), or move up the food chain to less restrictive guarantees to increase volume." [Offers p169]

**Decision rules, formulas, thresholds**
- Refund math 1.23× at +30% sales and doubled refunds [Offers p157]; guarantee quality 2-4× conversion [Offers p156].
- WYMB only if refund rate < 5%; expect ~10% ask-backs; store credit = cash; next offer ≥ 4× the credit [MM: WYMB; Rollover Upsell]. Pay Less Now: > 10% pay-later cancellations = promised too much, conditions too low, or price too high [MM: Pay Less Now]. Waived-fee early cancels > 5% → investigate [MM: Waived Fee].
- Guarantee upsell +5-50% [MM: Classic Upsell]; feature-downsell the guarantee first [MM: Feature Downsells].
- Performance floors: "greater of $1,000 or 10%"; $1,000/mo × 3 then 100% performance [Offers p168-169]. Modified service guarantee ≥ 2× original duration [Offers p164].

**When / when not, and stage**
- Start with service or performance guarantees; relax later for volume [Offers p169].
- Low-ticket B2C → broad, unconditional; high-ticket, business-oriented → specific, conditional; high fulfilment cost → conditional or anti [Offers p157-158, p162].
- Anti-guarantee: consumables, un-seeable IP, high-customisation work, outcomes you do not control [Offers p167; T-Sales 02:03]. Implied: quantifiable outcomes plus control over collection [Offers p159, p168-169].
- WYMB: services where the customer must act, things people "start and quit" [MM: WYMB; T-OffersLM 04:53]; decoys carry none [MM: Decoy]; pay-now buyers get discount and bonuses "instead of" the guarantee [MM: Pay Less Now].

**Author's examples with numbers** [E]
- Satisfaction guarantee: 2 refunds in 4,000 sales over 3.5 years [Offers p161-162].

### 6. Scarcity, urgency, bonuses and naming

**Core statements**

### 1. Lead generation fundamentals

**Core statements**

1. [S] A lead is "a person you can contact. That's all." An engaged lead is a person who shows interest (gives contact info, follows and is contactable, replies to a campaign). "Engaged leads are the true output of advertising." [Leads: Leads Alone Aren't Enough]. Advertising = "the process of making known" [Leads: Section I intro].
2. [S] The diagnosis behind the whole Leads book: "You're not getting as many leads as you want because you're not advertising enough. Period." [Leads: The Problem This Book Solves]. Restated as the Core Four diagnostic: not enough leads means "you're not doing the core four with enough skill or with enough volume" [Leads: Section III intro].
3. [S] Two ways to grow: more customers, or make them worth more; more customers = more, better, cheaper, reliable leads ("from lots of places"); "when you double your leads, you double your business" all else equal [Leads: The Problem This Book Solves]. Variant: Offers lists three ways (more customers, higher average purchase, more purchases) and admits strictly there are two, split for the later money-models material [Offers p37].
4. [S] The Core Four taxonomy: audiences are warm (gave permission) or cold (strangers, other people's audiences); paths are 1-to-1 (private) or 1-to-many (public). Warm 1-to-1 = warm outreach; warm 1-to-many = posting content; cold 1-to-1 = cold outreach; cold 1-to-many = paid ads. Automation does not change the category: emailing 10,000 people once is "one-to-one really fast by a machine" [Leads: Section III intro]. Lead getters (customers, employees, agencies, affiliates) are not a fifth way; you do the core four to get them, then they do it for you: "the core four stacks" [Leads: Section IV intro].
5. [S] Advertising vs sales problem, three framings by the same author:
   - Paid-ads framing: "If your engaged leads have the problem you solve and the money to spend, and they're not buying, then your ads work fine, you have a sales problem." [E] A portfolio company quit after 12 weeks and $150,000 in ads with the right leads on the phone; estimated cost ~$30M in enterprise value [Leads: Paid Ads II].
   - Employees framing: ask "Do my engaged leads have the problem I solve and the money to spend?" No → not qualified → advertising problem. Yes, buying, but too few → advertising problem. Yes but not buying → sales problem. "Don't fire your sales guy if you've got advertising problems" and vice versa [Leads: Employees].
   - Sales-lecture framing: "lead quality going down" is usually a lead-working problem; response after 5 minutes cuts close likelihood by 80% [T-Sales 00:05].
6. [S] Advertising vs business-model problem: research the industry-average CAC. CAC below 3× the industry average → fix LTGP (the business model); above 3× → fix advertising. "The cost to acquire customers, between competitors in the same industry, is much closer than you'd think. The difference between the winners and the losers is how much they make off each customer." [Leads: Paid Ads II]. Variant on the same threshold: CAC "within 3× industry average" = "doing good enough," focus on LTGP [Leads: Employees].
7. [S] Offer-side diagnosis of expensive leads: "your offer sucks, which is why you can't get cheap leads": no promise on speed, ease or risk reversal, and no lead magnet in front of it [T-OffersLM 12:37]. The Offers hierarchy places market above offer above persuasion: a great market carries a bad offer; a normal market plus a Grand Slam Offer makes "tons of money even if you're bad at persuasion" [Offers p52]. A Grand Slam Offer at the same ad spend raises response 2.5× and conversion 2.5× [Offers p43].
8. [S] Volume rules. Rule of 100: "do 100 primary actions every day, for 100 days in a row"; the author's one promise: "you will get more engaged leads." Applied: 100 warm reach outs/day; 100 minutes/day of content plus at least one post/day; 100 cold reach outs/day; 100 minutes/day making ads plus 100 days at the computed budget [Leads: More Better New]. Open To Goal ("Rule of 100 on steroids… for the big kids"): commit to a number of outcomes, not attempts; [E] a gym chain required sales managers to sign five members per day no matter what [Leads: Open To Goal].
9. [S] "The right action in the wrong amount still fails." [E] 300 flyers, zero leads; the mentor's test size was 5,000, then 5,000 per day for a month; 0.5% response = decent, 1% = winner; 300 flyers "was 1/1500th the level of effort required" [Leads: Open To Goal]. Same story in the keynote: the effort gap between "motivated" and "clockwork" is "not 2× or 4×, it's like 50×" (10 calls every three days vs 200 dials every day before lunch) [T-Content 08:30–09:00]. "100 over six weeks" is 1/42 of "100 per day" [Leads: Open To Goal].
10. [S] Choosing the method: "After I do warm outreach to get my pool of customers going, if I have more time than money, I move to posting content. If I have more money than time, I go with cold outreach or running ads." "You only need to do one to get engaged leads. So, just pick one. Then, max it out. Do more. Do better. Do new." [Leads: Section III conclusion]. Paid ads come last because the skills from the other three transfer and because ads cost money you will have from doing the other three first [Leads: Paid Ads II]. "If you have no money, then agencies are out of the question" [Leads: Agencies].
11. [S] More, Better, New. More = volume to max capacity ("the biggest increases often come from advertising more"). Better = test at the constraint, the step with the biggest drop-off; one split test per week per platform, Monday cadence, running test log; if four tries (one month) fail to beat the control, move to the next constraint. New = when more/better returns fall below what a new placement would give; order: new placements → new platforms → new core-four activity [Leads: More Better New]. The "Size Of The Pie Fallacy": one method, one platform, one audience, three or four visible competitors, and the owner concludes the market is saturated [Leads: More Better New].
12. [S] The Roadmap (used to scale portfolio companies to $100M+): L1 friends know (one offer, one avatar, one platform; warm outreach). L2 consistently everyone you know (as much warm outreach and content as you can). L3 employees do more advertising. L4 product good enough for consistent referrals, target 25%+ of customers from referrals. L5 at least two methods on multiple platforms. L6 experienced executives run channels. Timeline "anywhere from five to ten years" for those who know what to do [Leads: The Roadmap]. Author's businesses: first business content + warm outreach; gyms free content + paid ads; Gym Launch paid ads + cold outreach; Prestige Labs affiliates; ALAN paid ads + affiliates; Acquisition.com content [Leads: Section III conclusion].
13. [S] Sales and marketing are one continuum: a fixed amount of information is required to buy; sales is "personalised marketing" filling whatever gaps marketing left; a sales team "will almost always improve overall ROI/ROAS" of any acquisition process [T-Sales 02:17–02:19]. Many 2–4-call closes mean insufficient pre-education before the first call [T-Sales 02:20].
14. [S] Free content "makes all other advertising more effective"; [E] 78% of gym-owner clients had consumed at least two long-form pieces before booking a call; when the author stopped making gym content, paid ads stopped working [Leads: Content II].
15. [S] Cash constraint on lead generation: "Bootstrapped businesses must get customers at a profit," otherwise "you run out of cash and go out of business before you even have a chance" [MM: Section VI]; the cut-ads death spiral (spend more than you make → cut ads → fewer customers → float on credit) [MM: Section I, Beware].

**Decision rules, formulas, thresholds**

- Lead ≠ engaged lead; count engaged leads as the output [Leads: Leads Alone Aren't Enough].
- Not enough leads → more skill or more volume in the core four [Leads: Section III intro].
- Leads have problem + money, don't buy → sales problem; leads unqualified or too few → advertising problem [Leads: Paid Ads II; Leads: Employees].
- CAC < 3× industry average → work on LTGP; CAC > 3× → work on advertising (or sales) [Leads: Paid Ads II; Leads: Employees].
- Response > 5 min → close likelihood −80%; <60 s → +391% [T-Sales 00:03–00:05].
- Rule of 100: 100 actions/day × 100 days [Leads: More Better New]. Open To Goal: work until N outcomes/day [Leads: Open To Goal].
- Flyer/print test size 5,000; 0.5% decent, 1% winner [Leads: Open To Goal].
- Constraint arithmetic: 30% opt-in, 5% apply, 50% schedule; +5 points at the 5% step = 2×, at the others 1.16× and 1.1× [Leads: More Better New].
- One test per week per platform; four failed tries → next constraint [Leads: More Better New].
- New: placements → platforms → core four, "nine times out of ten" [Leads: More Better New].
- Time vs money: more time → content; more money → cold outreach or ads; ads last [Leads: Section III conclusion; Leads: Paid Ads II].
- Set aside 1%, 5% or 10% of ad budget for tests with no expected return [Leads: Section V intro].
- Expect three to six months to crack a new lead source ("and this isn't my first rodeo") [Leads: Section IV conclusion]. Attraction-offer fit "may take up to a year" [MM: Section VI].
- Starting out: at least four hours per day on getting customers, first thing in the morning [Leads: Warm Outreach].
- Market check before anything: pain, purchasing power, easy to target, growing [Offers p49–51].

**When / when not, and stage**

- Stage: the Leads book is ordered from zero (warm outreach, no money, no skills) to a $10M month; "Masters never don't do the basics" [Leads: The Problem This Book Solves].
- Stage: warm outreach for the "First Five Clients" of any new product; advanced firms use it for re-engagement and new lines [Leads: Warm Outreach].
- Stage: businesses that hit a wall at $1M–$3M/year usually got there on one platform and believe they cannot grow; the fix is More Better New, not a new market [Leads: More Better New]. Offers agrees from the pricing side: "If you are at $1M or $3M, thinking you have capped and must expand, you are wrong. You just need to be better." [Offers p54].
- Stage: L4 is "where most people mess up. They let their product slip and never recover." [Leads: The Roadmap].
- Do not run paid ads or hire agencies without money; do not add a new core-four method before exhausting more/better on the current one [Leads: More Better New; Leads: Agencies].
- Enterprise/government sales with 6–12-month cycles: the sales-lecture speed rules "are not going to apply" [T-Sales 02:05].

**Author's examples with numbers**

- [E] Rebuild month: $3,300/day on a credit card, $100,117 collected month one; $215,000 profit in the 30 days after the licensing pivot; year one $6.82M revenue [Leads: How I Got Here].

[I] Read as stage-dependent: ads buy chances early; L4 requires product quality.

- Tension on timelines: Leads "three to six months" to crack a source vs Money Models "up to a year" to find the attraction offer vs Leads "took almost a year" for cold outreach [Leads: Cold Outreach]. [I] The shorter figure is the author's own expectation with experience; the year is what actually happened.

### 2. Lead magnets

**Core statements**

1. [S] Definition: a lead magnet is "a complete solution to a narrow problem," typically free or low cost; solving it reveals another problem that the core offer solves. Quality bar: "valuable enough on its own that you could charge for it." "A person who pays with their time now is more likely to pay with their money later." [Leads: Offers and Lead Magnets]. Same definition read on camera from "page 31" [T-LeadMagnets 02:00]; a "mini offer" that reveals the next problem [T-LeadMagnets 01:30].
2. [S] When to use one: try advertising the core offer first ("the direct path to money… Try this way first"); use a lead magnet when "people want to know more about your offer before they buy. This is common for businesses that sell more expensive stuff." [Leads: Offers and Lead Magnets]. A "book a call / request a quote" CTA "only works if you already have an informed audience"; colder or smaller traffic needs a better reason to hand over contact info [T-LeadMagnets 01:00]; "no one wants to request a quote" [T-OffersLM 08:15].
3. [S] Big brand does not remove the need: Acquisition.com runs direct-to-workshop ads (higher ROAS up front) and scaling-roadmap lead-magnet ads (higher ROAS over an expanded horizon, and "scales better"). The direct CTA reaches only the warmest two awareness segments; the lead magnet reaches "way more people up here than all of this put together." Rule: higher long-horizon ROAS plus more reach = "by far the better front end." People who say lead magnets don't work are "small businesses (small is relative)"; a direct ask gives higher ROAS if you want less money; "huge higher realms" require softening the ask and building a conversion process behind it [T-OffersLM 05:42–08:15].
4. [S] Three types: (a) reveal a problem (diagnosis; works great when the problem worsens while waiting: website speed test, posture analysis, termite inspection); (b) samples and trials (full but brief access; limit uses, time, or both; works great when the core offer is recurring); (c) one step of a multi-step process (works great when the core offer solves a complex problem: this book, one coat of three, finance calculators that reveal the DIY effort) [Leads: Offers and Lead Magnets]. Same taxonomy in both workshop clips [T-OffersLM 08:51; T-LeadMagnets 05:55]. Reveal-a-problem is his "personal favourite" because it "literally creates deprivation"; be very specific on the negatives so you can be vaguer on the positives (more compliant, more believable) [T-LeadMagnets 10:12–12:22]. Trials must run a full loop so that removing them creates deprivation [T-LeadMagnets 12:53]. One-step is "particularly effective for more complex products and services" [T-LeadMagnets 15:10].
5. [S] Four delivery vehicles: software (calculators, spreadsheets, tools), information (courses, interviews, guides), services (do work free), physical products. 3 × 4 = "up to twelve lead magnets that solve a single narrow problem"; make many versions and rotate them [Leads: Offers and Lead Magnets]. Information "costs nothing… infinitely scalable… no operational drag"; services are "slept on" but must be restricted to qualified leads; combinations are normal (the scaling roadmap = tool + information) [T-LeadMagnets 15:56–22:30].
6. [S] Naming and packaging: headline = 80 cents of the advertising dollar (Ogilvy); test headline, then image, then subheadline; improving the name "can 2x, 3x, or 10x your engagement" [Leads: Offers and Lead Magnets]. He is "adamant" about testing packaging more than contents, on condition the contents clearly solve a problem [T-LeadMagnets 05:14]. The name "determines engagement rate more than anything else"; the real secret is "ask your audience" [T-LeadMagnets 22:30]. Naming conventions: number + outcome + time frame; how to X without Y / even if Z; adjective + thing; X mistakes [T-LeadMagnets 25:35]. Offers gives the M-A-G-I-C formula (Magnet reason-why, Avatar, Goal, Interval, Container; use 3–5 of 5) and the same 2×–10× response claim [Offers p173–180].
7. [S] Make it easy to consume (every format; 2–4× take rates) and "make it darn good": "Give Away The Secrets, Sell The Implementation." "You can never provide too much value. But you can provide too little." "99% of people aren't gonna buy, but they will create (or destroy) your reputation based on the value of your free stuff." [Leads: Offers and Lead Magnets]. Keynote variant: "If you're not afraid that you're giving away too much, you're not giving away enough"; "1% of people will do it. My business model relies on the 1% who do it" [T-Content 43:00–44:00]. Workshop variant: "give something other people charge for… the more real the lead magnet is, the more it actually has true cost, the more likely it is that it will work" [T-OffersLM 13:07].
8. [S] The "too much value" objection resolved: do not solve with the free thing the problem the core offer solves; solve the problem that leads to it, so the person stays deprived of what you sell. Restaurant analogy: the dessert (core offer) should be significantly more expensive than the entrée you give free or at cost. "Sell at the point of greatest deprivation." [T-LeadMagnets 07:20–10:12]. Leads' version: the Problem-Solution cycle; pick a narrow, meaningful problem whose revealed next problem your core offer solves [Leads: Offers and Lead Magnets].
9. [S] CTA formula: what to do plus a reason to do it now; "Don't be clever, be clear"; give the CTA often; reasons ranked: any reason beats none, good reasons beat bad; stack scarcity (real capacity limits), urgency (deadlines), and "fraternity party planner" reasons ("because it's my birthday") [Leads: Offers and Lead Magnets]. Video version: clear not clever + exact next action + reason to do it now; embed CTAs within, before and after the lead magnet; optimise "ability to ask × number of asks" by keeping value per second high [T-LeadMagnets 26:45–31:23].
10. [S] Freebie seekers: qualify with a dropdown; route unqualified people to something they might qualify for [T-LeadMagnets 07:20]. Free services only for leads with budget, authority, need and an intention to act now; giving free work to "someone broke, indecisive, without need and unsure of timing is a terrible waste of your time" [T-LeadMagnets 18:42].
11. [S] Money math (Leads): core offer $10,000 profit; $1,000 ads per call, close 1 in 3 → $3,000 CAC. Lead magnet costing $25 to deliver: $75 ads + $25 = $100 per call, ten times more engaged leads for the same cost; 1 in 10 buys → $1,000 CAC, 10:1 return, same budget "triple our business." "Even if your lead magnet costs money to deliver, it should still lower your cost to get a new customer." [Leads: Offers and Lead Magnets]. Video variant: 1 hour labour, $25 hard cost, $250+ market value, 1 in 4 buys → $100 CAC [T-LeadMagnets 19:17]. Moats variant: freemium CAC = cost to deliver the free thing ÷ conversion rate (1 cent, 1 in 100 → $1 CAC); "free gets 10× the interest of not free" [T-Moats #12].
12. [S] Service-business capacity as scarcity: "78% of businesses are service"; you cannot take 1,000 customers tomorrow; if four more customers fill you, say so, because prospects assume they know how big you are and do not. Big business with capacity → rolling cohorts ("we can only start this many per week") [T-LeadMagnets 30:11]. Offers: "reality/ethical scarcity," advertise "three-fourths of the way to capacity"; a free checklist limited to 20 people per week becomes "a desirable thing not everyone has access to" and is consumed more [Offers p137–138]. Leads: "If you sold 1000x the customers tomorrow, could you handle it?" Advertise the natural limits [Leads: Offers and Lead Magnets].
13. [S] Splinter offers: an offer with components A, B, C, D sold at $2,000; pull B out and give it at 90% off or free; once they have B they want A, C, D. Discounts of 10–20% are "no point"; a discount must change behaviour, 80–90% does; affordable because applied to something that costs little [T-OffersLM 10:58–12:35].
14. [S] Attraction offers as lead magnets (Money Models): "anywhere I say free you can substitute discount or $1"; "people look for one thing and then buy another by accident all the time. Attraction Offers get them to do it on purpose" [MM: Section II intro]. Fit rules by business type: service where the customer must act → Win Your Money Back; transactional → Decoy; Buy X Get Y Free "in so many different versions"; check advertising law by region [T-OffersLM 04:03–05:40].
   - Giveaways: advertise a big prize for contact info plus eligibility answers; after the winner, offer everyone else the prize at a discount ("partial scholarship"). Discount = 10–30% of gross margin; entry window 3–7 days; claim window 7 days; cap entries to what you can call in seven days; two prizes (referrer wins if referral wins) for referrals; "if it doesn't work, the Grand Prize wasn't grand enough" [MM: Giveaways]. Workshop example: kitchen-remodel giveaway, second place = $2,000 discount [T-OffersLM 04:47].
   - Decoy: advertise a free or discounted stripped version (no guarantees), present the premium side by side; [E] ~8 of 10 chose the $399 option over free; "Discount offers have higher show-up rates than free offers": free → more leads, discount → fewer but more show [MM: Decoy].
   - Pay Less Now or Pay More Later: advertise "free," card on file, billed later unless cancelled after meeting conditions; pay-now at 20–50% off plus bonuses; >10% pay-later cancellations = over-promised or conditions too low [MM: Pay Less Now].
   - Free Goodwill: a review request, not an offer model [MM: Free Goodwill Offer].
15. [S] A good lead magnet does four things: engages ideal customers when they see it; gets more people to engage than the core offer alone; is valuable enough to be consumed; makes the right people more likely to buy. Step 0: "If you're struggling to get leads, make an amazing lead magnet." [Leads: Offers and Lead Magnets].

**Decision rules, formulas, thresholds**

- Core offer first; lead magnet when buyers need to know more (higher ticket) [Leads: Offers and Lead Magnets].
- Free/discounted front must not satisfy the core need; core offer must remain the unsatisfied, more expensive "dessert" [T-LeadMagnets 08:59].
- Type selection: worsening problem → reveal; recurring core → trial; complex core → one step [Leads: Offers and Lead Magnets].
- Test order: headline → image → subheadline; 2–10× [Leads: Offers and Lead Magnets]. Testing ladder: small ad tests → story polls with filtered responders → texts / A-or-B comments [T-LeadMagnets 23:56].
- "Two years writing, two days testing the headline" [T-LeadMagnets 24:48].
- Multiple formats: 2–4× take rates [Leads: Offers and Lead Magnets].
- CAC via lead magnet = delivery cost ÷ conversion rate [T-Moats #12]; worked: $25 × 4 = $100 [T-LeadMagnets 19:17]; $100/call ÷ 1-in-10 = $1,000 CAC [Leads: Offers and Lead Magnets].
- Help-first arithmetic: "buy my thing" to 100 → ~1; "let me help" then ask → 3–5× as many buyers on the same traffic [T-LeadMagnets 02:54].
- Splinter discount 80–90%, never 10–20% [T-OffersLM 12:14].
- Giveaway discount = 10–30% of gross margin; 3–7 day entry; 7-day claim; usage window hours to five days [MM: Giveaways].
- Pay-later cancellations >10% → diagnose [MM: Pay Less Now]. WYMB only if existing refund rate <5%; expect ~10% to ask for money back [MM: Win Your Money Back].
- Free services: BANT-style qualification first [T-LeadMagnets 18:42]; qualifying dropdown for everything else [T-LeadMagnets 07:20].
- CTA = clear instruction + exact next action + reason now; repeat within, before, after [T-LeadMagnets 27:46–31:23].
- Scarcity on a free download: 20 per week, notify-me list [Offers p137].
- Cold-outreach lead magnets must be "big fast value": swapping a "game planning session" for "as much free service as we could possibly afford" tripled take rates [Leads: Cold Outreach].

**When / when not, and stage**

- Stage: "If you're struggling to get leads, make an amazing lead magnet" [Leads: Offers and Lead Magnets]; "if you do not have a lead magnet you are making less money than you could" [T-LeadMagnets 07:40].
- Stage: direct offer is fine for smaller revenue targets; lead magnet plus conversion process is required for scale [T-OffersLM 07:30].
- Book-a-call CTA only with an informed, warm audience [T-LeadMagnets 01:00].
- Webinars work but are recognised as pitches by sophisticated buyers; a screen-recorded case study ("consume it on your own time") out-converted [T-LeadMagnets 03:41–05:14].
- Service businesses: use real capacity as scarcity; big businesses: cohorts [T-LeadMagnets 30:11].
- Experienced owners with a $25 delivery budget "win" because they can provide far more value than $0 [Leads: Offers and Lead Magnets].
- Legal: giveaways are "heavily regulated" and "when done wrong, can become illegal lotteries"; advertising laws on "free" tighten over time [MM: Giveaways; MM: Four Types].
- Do not run WYMB unless "you are OK giving money back" [MM: Win Your Money Back]. Do not run Buy X Get Y Free "if you can't manage money" [MM: Buy X Get Y Free].

**Author's examples with numbers**

- [E] April 2016: webinar, $150/day, "$450, 80 leads, 0 sales"; replaced with a 13-minute screen-recorded case study ("213 members and $112,000… San Diego"); calendar booked solid a week out by next morning [Leads: Offers and Lead Magnets; T-LeadMagnets 03:41].
- [E] $100M Offers is split ~¼ each across ebook, physical, audiobook, video: "2-3-4x the amount of leads for the same work" [Leads: Offers and Lead Magnets].
- [E] $100M Leads title tests: Advertising beat Promotion; Leads beat Advertising; Leads beat Marketing; real image beat cartoon; six subheadline variants [Leads: Offers and Lead Magnets; T-LeadMagnets 24:48].
- [E] Website speed test close: 3% conversion lost per second; 9 s → 3 s = +18% [T-LeadMagnets 11:06]. ALAN's best ad ever: "four reasons why you'll never have a million-dollar agency" [T-LeadMagnets 11:52].
- [E] Gym Launch: four months of free agency services bundled with the monetisation system, then "now you can pay for them" [T-LeadMagnets 14:39].
- [E] Local-maps company: rank the business in positions 1–3 free, call at day seven [T-OffersLM 09:31]. Ashley the stylist: colour palette as automated lead magnet instead of "book a call" [T-OffersLM 09:53; T-LeadMagnets 00:53].
- [E] CEO shirt at a conference: $3–4 per shirt, 1 in 20 converts, "$100 for a CEO" [T-LeadMagnets 22:22].
- [E] Free 6 Week Challenge as attraction offer: 20 leads/day at $5, 50% show, 50% close, $680 per customer, "$1 in, $34 out in 48 hours" [MM: Start Here].
- [E] Giveaway retry: event tickets failed; $50,000 equipment bundle plus a year of the core product "crushed" [MM: Giveaways].
- [E] Fitness-certification "full-ride scholarship": everyone else offered a partial scholarship, "most of them join on the spot"; capped to service capacity [MM: Giveaways].
- [E] Prestige Meals test kitchen: "free gym forever" if on the meals did not work; "a bit of a distraction" [T-Moats #12].

**Connections and tensions**

- [I] The lead-magnet definition in Leads, the splinter offer in the workshop, and Money Models' attraction offers are one idea on a price continuum from free to discounted; the author himself links them ("wrap what you currently sell" in an attraction offer) [T-OffersLM 04:03].
- Tension: free gives more leads, discount gives higher show rates [MM: Decoy] vs the workshop's "80–90% off or free" rule for splinters [T-OffersLM 12:14]. [I] Both hold: choose free when volume matters, discount when no-shows are expensive (doctors, lawyers, dentists per the author).
- Tension: "give away the secrets" [Leads; T-Content] vs "don't solve the core problem for free" [T-LeadMagnets 08:59]. The author's own resolution: give everything about the problem that precedes the core problem; keep the core need unsatisfied.
- [I] The Offers "scarcity on a free checklist" [Offers p137] and the Leads "reality scarcity" CTA are the same tactic; Money Models adds giveaway entry caps matched to seven-day call capacity.
- [I] The keynote's "sell the implementation" and Money Models' Decoy (free DIY vs paid done-with-you) are the same funnel; Gym Launch's stage-I money model was exactly this [MM: Section VI].
- Tension: Offers "never discount the main offer" [Offers p148] vs splinter 80–90% discounts and Money Models attraction discounts. Author's resolution: the discount is on a component or wrapper, and prices are raised first (Buy X Get Y Free) so the core price is not what moves.

**Not covered by the sources**

- No data on lead-magnet-to-sale conversion by type or delivery vehicle beyond illustrative ratios (1 in 4, 1 in 10, 1 in 20).
- No guidance on lead-magnet nurture sequences between opt-in and call other than "call every opt-in" and the CTA rules.

### 3. Organic content

**Core statements**

1. [S] Posting is warm 1-to-many: say it once, the whole warm audience can see it, and sharers extend it. Free content grows the warm audience constantly and "makes all other advertising more effective." Trade-offs: less personal, you compete with everyone, standing out gets you copied. "The content you create isn't the compounding asset: the audience is." [Leads: Content I].
2. [S] The content unit is the smallest material that hooks, retains and rewards. Hook levers: topics (far past, recent past "do stuff, then talk about it," present, trending, manufactured), headlines (at least two of recency, relevancy, celebrity, proximity, conflict, unusual, ongoing), format (look like the platform's best content). Retain: curiosity via lists, steps, stories. Reward: "value per second"; "There is no such thing as too long, only too boring"; the test is that the audience grows [Leads: Content I].
3. [S] Long vs short: "not much" difference; long form is linked units and needs more skill; start shorter [Leads: Content I]. Keynote: "shorts give you a lot of breadth, longs give you depth"; long = hook → story → framework → why; short = hook → "hammer" (the validated tweet) → example → explanation. Virality per MrBeast: "CTR × watch time. That's it." [T-Content 15:00–16:30].
4. [S] Give:ask. Vaynerchuk's "jab, jab, jab, right hook." Mature-platform benchmarks: television 47:13 ≈ 3.5:1, Facebook ≈ 4 posts per ad; they optimise money over growth, so they are the minimum sustainable ratio; "If you want to grow, give far more than you ask." Author's tweak: "Give until they ask"; "You give in public, ask in private"; "The moment you start asking for money is the moment you decide to slow down your growth." [Leads: Content II]. Keynote: "goodwill compounds faster than money"; "The longer you can delay the ask, the bigger the ask can be. The longer the runway, the bigger the plane"; against the right hook, "give give give, you don't even have to ask": people hand you things (inbound $10k–$20k offers for ten minutes) "because you're not asking for anything and it makes people feel uncomfortable" [T-Content 47:00–49:00]. Offers' version: "The longer you delay the ask, the bigger the ask you can make" [Offers p128].
5. [S] Asking when you must: asks are commercials that cost trust and growth. Integrated: an ask in every piece; [E] a one-hour podcast with three 30-second ads = 58.5:1.5, "well above the 3:1 ratio"; add one ask, verify growth holds, add the next; [E] a friend's podcast asked too often inside the content, stopped growing, then shrank. Intermittent: 10 gives, ask on the 11th. Short-form platforms → intermittent; long-form → integrated. Ask for the core offer or a lead magnet: "If you're not sure, do the lead magnet. It's lower risk." [Leads: Content II]. Keynote: "Inject" CTAs (other channels, lead magnets, share, review, apply, the book), two versions each, at start, mid-roll and end; the podcast went 20,000 → 400,000 downloads/month and "a big part of that was just having calls to action"; without CTAs "you get all of these impressions… but you don't drive a result" [T-Content 17:00–18:00].
6. [S] The content creation model, Test → Record → Inject → Contextualize → Distribute: every idea as a tweet (~5/day, "net zero time"), traction = validated, threads → long form, tweets → shorts; record short and long; inject CTAs; re-cut per platform ("the exact same asset on the wrong platform hurts it"); distribute 7 → 80 posts/week, "We 10× the inputs and we got 10× the output" [T-Content 13:00–20:00]. His caveat: "a lot of people ascribe that as the reason… I don't think that's why"; the model "worked for me this time," the frame is "the more eternal stuff" [T-Content 20:30–24:00].
7. [S] The frame: every viewer asks "why should I listen to you?", "the frame that the content is consumed in"; "The real reason our audience is growing is that we're talking about stuff that we have evidence to support." Two shifts for a beginner: "Do, then talk" (do, talk about it, do bigger) and "How I" not "how to" ("you should" reads as preaching; "Everyone is unquestionably an expert on your own life") [T-Content 22:00–29:00]. Leads' lesson 1: "How to" → "How I," "the best way" → "my favorite ways," "especially starting out" [Leads: Content II]. Damaging admissions "are the single greatest thing that built trust"; "If you have done nothing, do something first and then talk about it" [T-Content 34:00–39:00, 52:00–55:00].
8. [S] Niche authority sequence: puddles → ponds → lakes → oceans; "If you have a small local business, you probably shouldn't make general business content. Not at first"; plumbing in one town → local-business pond → brick-and-mortar-chains lake → general-business ocean [Leads: Content II]. Keynote: be a niche-specific authority ("ATM businesses," "flip houses in Phoenix"): "in that little pond you can be king because you compete against 20 guys"; "you will not gain a mega following at first, but you will accomplish"; "It's not bad, it's just sequence" [T-Content 30:00–34:00].
9. [S] Give away the secrets, sell the implementation: "1% of people will do it. My business model relies on the 1% who do it"; "If you're not afraid that you're giving away too much, you're not giving away enough"; viewers judge on one piece and "slowly you fade away"; "quality over quantity, but quality-quantity beats just quality"; most people's free stuff is not better than others' paid, "that's why it doesn't work" [T-Content 43:00–47:00].
10. [S] Volume: [E] Leila paid $120,000 for four influencer calls: "anyone telling you there's some secret is trying to sell you something. We just put out as much as we possibly can"; 10× the content → +1.2M audience in six months [Leads: Content I]. Keynote: "we're doing the right stuff, we're just doing way too little of it"; a vacation-shot video beat the studio one, "what's inside the content matters more than the wrapper… it's 80/20"; "volume × time = skill" [T-Content 06:00–08:00, 42:00].
11. [S] Scaling, two "both valid" strategies. Depth then width: one platform, maximise quality and quantity (short form "up to ten times per day per platform," long form "up to five days per week (see soap operas)"), then add a platform while maintaining the first; con: single-channel risk ("platforms… sometimes ban you for no reason. If you only have one way to get customers, it can kill your business"). Width then depth: all platforms first, then maximise; con: "lots of bad content everywhere. Sucky fluff." "If you already have a sizable business, scale up faster." [Leads: Content II].
12. [S] Content even when not the primary method: [E] January 2020 paid ads stopped working because he had stopped making gym content; 78% of clients had consumed at least two long-form pieces before booking a call; content "was nurturing the demand" and warms people going to and from the cold methods, "even if hard to measure" [Leads: Content II]. Best free content makes the best paid ads "nine times out of ten" [Leads: Paid Ads II].
13. [S] Other lessons: "We need to be reminded more than we need to be taught" ([E] 1 in 5 who saw his daily book post did not know he had a book); a "greatest hits" list by problem for the sales team; free content retains paying customers; manual posting beats pre-scheduling [Leads: Content II].
14. [S] Measurement and cost: monthly followers and reach, plus growth rate (500 new on 1,000 = 50%); "We can only control inputs," so fix a posting cadence and an ask cadence, "Do. Not. Stop." [E] Podcast twice a week for four years before the Top 100: "Give time, time." [Leads: Content II]. [E] YouTube 600 → 70,000 subscribers in 12 months at 3 videos/week; after 7 → 80 pieces/week, six months brought ~1M audience, ~100,000 organic clicks/month, ~400 applications/month; at a $25 CPM, "$2 million a month in exposure… we're getting paid to do it" [T-Content 06:00, 10:00–11:30]. Cost: ~7% of his time; $20,000/month per person in editors, ×2 = $40,000/month; "I have to compensate with money. You could probably do this on your own" [T-Content 11:00–13:00]. Acquisition.com: ~30,000 engaged leads/month from a ≈$100,000/month content team ≈ $3.33 per lead [Leads: Employees].
15. [S] Sequencing: after warm outreach, "if I have more time than money, I move to posting content" [Leads: Section III conclusion]; the first post can be an ask; keep doing warm reach outs daily; "With these two methods alone [you] can eventually build a six- or seven-figure business" [Leads: Content II]; L2: "I wish I would have started with posting free content" [Leads: The Roadmap].

**Decision rules, formulas, thresholds**

- Hook + retain + reward; ≥2 headline components [Leads: Content I]. Virality = CTR × watch time [T-Content 15:00].
- Give:ask floor 3.5:1 / 4:1; growing → far more; "give until they ask" [Leads: Content II].
- Integrated (58.5:1.5, one ask at a time) on long form; intermittent (10 then 1) on short form; unsure → lead magnet [Leads: Content II]. CTAs start, mid-roll, end [T-Content 17:00].
- ~5 tweets/day as tests; re-cut per platform [T-Content 13:00–19:00].
- Caps: ≤10 short posts/day/platform, ≤5 long-form days/week; next platform only while maintaining the first [Leads: Content II]. Rule of 100: 100 minutes/day, ≥1 post/day [Leads: More Better New].
- "How I" not "how to"; do then talk; puddle before ocean [Leads: Content II; T-Content 25:00–34:00].
- Measure monthly: followers, reach, growth rate [Leads: Content II]. Organic reach at $25 CPM [T-Content 11:00]; payroll ÷ engaged leads [Leads: Employees].
- More time than money → content [Leads: Section III conclusion]. Best free piece → paid ad, 9/10 [Leads: Paid Ads II].

**When / when not, and stage**

- Stage: after warm outreach, with more time than money; L1 one platform, L2 warm outreach plus content; sizable business → "scale up faster" [Leads: Section III conclusion; Leads: The Roadmap; Leads: Content II].
- Local business: no general business content at first [Leads: Content II; T-Content 30:00].
- Beginner: "How I," damaging admissions, do-then-talk; "Teaching on principles is difficult until you've achieved a material amount in the field" [T-Content 52:00].
- Delaying the ask for years is what he did while other businesses funded him (only monetisation: "you get to three million, then we can partner"), with his own "Himalayan grain of salt" on his organic experience [T-Content 47:30–49:00].
- Works for B2B and services too (Nomad Capitalist 3,000 applications/month) [T-Content 03:00–06:00].

**Author's examples with numbers**

- [E] 10× content → +1.2M audience in six months [Leads: Content I]; 7 → 80 posts/week → ~1M audience; acquisition.com $7M → $13M/month in the period, partly portfolio growth [T-Content 10:00–11:30]. Podcast 20,000 → 400,000 downloads/month after CTAs [T-Content 17:00].
- [E] First content: "stubborn fat on your arms and stomach" for gym members, won on "values-based rapport, not expertise," and "made me enough money to grow the company I had"; Gym Secrets podcast → Gym Launch Secrets book → $100M Offers, which sold in under a year what the former did in four [T-Content 31:00–34:00].

**Connections and tensions**

- Tension (same author): "Give until they ask" [Leads: Content II] vs CTAs at start, middle and end that drove the podcast 20× [T-Content 17:00]. His resolution in Leads: integrated asks after value, added one at a time while growth holds, preferably for a lead magnet; "give in public, ask in private." [I] The keynote's CTAs are mostly non-monetary, the same resolution.
- Tension: delay monetisation for years ("most people don't make money [because] they can't wait 12 months" [T-Content 49:00–52:00]) vs the 30-day payback floor and "Bootstrapped businesses must get customers at a profit" [MM: Section I; MM: Section VI]. [I] The keynote describes a brand asset funded by other businesses; "your first post can be an ask" plus warm outreach is the first-business version. Not reconciled by the author.
- Tension: volume (10/day, 80/week) vs "Sucky fluff" and one-video judgement; resolved as "quality-quantity beats just quality" [T-Content 44:00].
- Depth-then-width vs width-then-depth: left open, as is topic 1's "pick one" vs L5 two methods [Leads: Content II; Leads: The Roadmap].
- [I] Content ROI is "hard to measure" when arguing to keep posting [Leads: Content II] and precisely priced ($25 CPM, $3.33/lead) when arguing it beats ads [T-Content 11:00; Leads: Employees].

**Not covered by the sources**

- No platform mechanics (thumbnails beyond "CTR × watch time," posting times) and no data tying posting volume to leads beyond his own audience counts.
- No rule for when an audience is mature enough to move from intermittent to integrated asks.

### 4. Paid ads

**Core statements**

1. [S] Paid ads are cold 1-to-many, "renting eyeballs or earballs": the fastest reach, riskier, but "more leads than any other method." Reach is guaranteed, payback is not, so ads are "a game of efficiency, not reach"; "If an ad isn't profitable, most of the time, it's because the right people never saw it" [Leads: Paid Ads I]. "Efficiency matters more than creativity. All advertising works. The only thing that differs… is how well they work" [Leads: Paid Ads II]. Method: narrow the haystack (platform → targeting → an ad that repels everyone else → CTA), then widen puddle → pond → lake → ocean while the spend-to-buy ratio falls and total profit rises: "make the audience as big as possible while still turning a profit" [Leads: Paid Ads I].
2. [S] Platform, four conditions: you have used it and gotten value; you can target your buyers; you know its ad format; you have the minimum spend. One platform first [Leads: Paid Ads I]. Targeting: lookalike from an uploaded list, ordered to reach the platform minimum: current and previous customers → warm reach-out list → cold reach-out leads, narrowed by factor targeting (age, income, interests, location). More filters → more efficient, faster "burn"; specific early wins fund broader audiences, "This is how you scale" [Leads: Paid Ads I].
3. [S] Ad = Call Out + Value + CTA. Call out: "People noticing your ad is the most important part… by a lot"; [E] "My advertising became 20x more effective when I focused the majority of my effort on the first five seconds." Verbal callouts: labels (local rule "LOCAL AREA + TYPE OF PERSON," "Americans < Texans < Dallas Residents < Irving Residents"), yes-questions, if-then statements, ridiculous results; nonverbal: contrast, likeness ("Quack like a duck"; mascots; a likeness that is not you makes the business sellable), the scene [Leads: Paid Ads I]. Offers' avatar rule agrees: "Not Baltimore but Towson, MD." [Offers p175].
4. [S] Value = What-Who-When: eight elements (each value driver and its opposite: nightmare, risk, slowness, effort); status-givers (spouse, kids, colleagues, rivals) as the Who; past, present and future consequences as the When. Long and short ads differ only in the number of angles; keep callouts and CTAs identical [Leads: Paid Ads I]. Compliance: a quantified claim with a duration implies a guarantee and "most ad platforms won't approve it" [Offers p175–176]. CTA: "S-P-E-L-L it out," easy numbers, short URLs [Leads: Paid Ads I].
5. [S] Landing page must match the ad in look and language, "a continuous experience from 'click to close'"; consistency framing moves people to the next step; contact info = engaged lead, not yet a sale [Leads: Paid Ads I]. Sales-lecture mirror: "the sale starts at the click"; everything between ad and call changes close rates "independent of what you say" [T-Sales 00:37–00:39].
6. [S] Three phases: Track Money (tracking before the first dollar, "If you don't track, you're gonna get cleaned out"); Lose Money ([E] "I've lost money more times than I've made money running paid ads"; 10 ads × $100, nine lose, one returns $500; then "100x down" on the winner, $10,000 → $50,000; "You might lose nine or ninety-nine times in a row before you win big"); Print Money ("spend as much as you can") [Leads: Paid Ads II].
7. [S] Kill rules: per new ad, budget 2× the cash collected from a customer in the first thirty days (not LTGP) "as long as it's getting leads"; no leads at all → kill before 1×. Running too long and quitting too early both cost him; this is the sweet spot. [E] One business took a year to make ads profitable, then earned the year back in a month: "Measure the returns over a long time horizon, not next week." [Leads: Paid Ads II].
8. [S] Budget: reverse from customers you can handle: 100 × $100 CAC = $10,000, plus 20% "because ads get less efficient as they scale" → $12,000 per 30 days → $400/day; "If the number terrifies you, then you're doing it right." [Leads: Paid Ads II]. Set aside 1%, 5% or 10% for tests with no expected return [Leads: Section V intro].
9. [S] LTGP:CAC: "Every business I invest in that struggles to scale… their LTGP to CAC ratio was less than 3 to 1. As soon as I get it above 3 to 1… they take off. This is a pattern I personally observed, not a rule." Lower CAC or raise LTGP, "I prefer to do both"; "Costs can only approach zero but how much you make can go up to infinity"; owners blame ads when they have "a crappy business model (Low LTGP)," hence the CAC-vs-3×-industry diagnostic (topic 1) [Leads: Paid Ads II]. Variant: CAC "should only rise with CPM" (~15%/year); CAC that doubles or triples fast is a reputation problem [T-CaseSaaS 00:25–00:26].
10. [S] Client-financed acquisition: "if your customer spends more than it costs you to get and fulfill them, in the first 30 days, then you have the funds to scale now and forever" (30 days of interest-free credit-card money). [E] $15/month, $5 to deliver, 10 months → $100 LTGP; CAC $30 = 3.3:1 but cash-negative for two months; "I immediately sell them more stuff": a $100 upsell at 100% margin taken by 1 in 5 adds $20 → $30 in 30 days = CAC → "free customers"; "how I've scaled every company… past $1M/mo in the first twelve months, without outside funding" [Leads: Paid Ads II]. Money Models' floor: 30-day profit per customer > cost to get and service that customer; the $100M standard funds "at least two more customers"; otherwise the cut-ads death spiral [MM: Section I; MM: Section VI; MM: Ten Years].
11. [S] The offer is the engine: a Grand Slam Offer at the same spend gives 2.5× response × 2.5× conversion × 4× price = 22.4× cash, "$10,000 to make $112,000" [Offers p43–44]; lifetime 36:1 [Offers p12]. Front end: direct-to-workshop ads have higher ROAS up front; scaling-roadmap lead-magnet ads have higher ROAS over an expanded horizon and "scale better," since a direct CTA reaches only the two warmest awareness segments; rule: higher long-horizon ROAS plus more reach = "by far the better front end"; if you want less money the direct ask wins "for sure," for "huge higher realms" soften the ask and build the conversion process behind it; the objectors are small businesses ("small is relative," maybe $5M/year) [T-OffersLM 05:42–08:15]. Ads to a "request a quote" page fail [T-OffersLM 08:15]. [E] ALAN's best ad: "four reasons why you'll never have a million-dollar agency" [T-LeadMagnets 11:52].
12. [S] Local reach and fatigue: ~$20 reaches 1,000 people on most platforms, so 200,000 people ≈ $10,000 once; one exposure is not fatigue; fatigue is "a matter of years, not months" if creative, hooks and copy keep changing around the same offer [Offers p172–173]. Variation order, lightest first: creative → copy → headline → duration → enhancer → monetisation [Offers p178–180]. Local is "both easier and harder": in-person trust converts more, but "the smaller the radius, the faster offers fatigue" [Offers p180]; rolling seasonal urgency is "my number one strategy for local businesses" [Offers p144]. Keynote price of attention: $25 CPM [T-Content 11:00].
13. [S] Lessons: "If your engaged leads have the problem you solve and the money to spend, and they're not buying, then your ads work fine, you have a sales problem" ([E] 12 weeks, $150,000, quit "six inches from gold," ~$30M of enterprise value); best free content → best ads "nine times out of ten"; UGC as ads; start by placing one $100 ad, "you're in the game," then "an acceptable amount of money you are willing to lose each month… You won't be earning, you'll be learning"; ads last because the other methods' skills transfer and pay for them [Leads: Paid Ads II]. Working the leads: call an opt-in with three mental cues because two days later "they're in a different universe" [T-Sales 00:43–00:46]; [E] text blasts to un-worked engaged leads took a month to $1.8M [Leads: Section V intro].
14. [S] In the system: Rule of 100 = 100 minutes/day making ads and 100 days at budget, "Aim for Client Financed Acquisition"; one test per week per platform [Leads: More Better New]; L5: new audiences on the best platform, then all placements, then another platform [Leads: The Roadmap].

**Decision rules, formulas, thresholds**

- Platform: 4 conditions; lookalike order customers → warm → cold; more filters = faster burn [Leads: Paid Ads I].
- Ad = callout + value + CTA; first five seconds (20×); local callout = area + person type; landing page matches [Leads: Paid Ads I].

- $20 per 1,000 [Offers p172]; $25 CPM [T-Content 11:00]; fatigue order creative → copy → headline → duration → enhancer → monetisation [Offers p178–180].
- Direct CTA for smaller targets; lead magnet plus conversion process for scale [T-OffersLM 07:30]. Grand Slam Offer 22.4× [Offers p43].
- Not buying with problem and money → sales problem; best free content → ad (9/10); first ad $100; ads last [Leads: Paid Ads II].

**When / when not, and stage**

- Stage: ads last, never without money, when money exceeds time [Leads: Paid Ads II; Leads: Section III conclusion]; L3 media buyer, L5 audiences → placements → platforms [Leads: The Roadmap]. $1M–$3M businesses on one platform assume saturation [Leads: More Better New].

- Local: rotate wrappers fast, seasonal deadlines, hyper-local callouts [Offers p144, p175, p180].
- Banned industries (tobacco, firearms, cannabis, weight loss) → cold outreach instead [Leads: Cold Outreach].
- Ads need content behind them (January 2020) [Leads: Content II]. Enterprise cycles of 6–12 months: the decide-now mechanics do not apply [T-Sales 02:05].

**Author's examples with numbers**

**Connections and tensions**

- [I] "Audience as big as possible while still turning a profit" [Leads: Paid Ads I] vs "under $10M… niching down will make you more money" [Offers p54]: audience breadth on a fixed offer vs who the offer is for.

### 5. Outbound

**Core statements**

Warm outreach

1. [S] One-to-one contact with the warm audience (calls, texts, emails, DMs), advertising the lead magnet or core offer: "the cheapest and easiest way to find people interested in the stuff you sell… most businesses don't do it." Few leads per unit time but "As certain as the sun rises and sets, it works" [Leads: Warm Outreach].
2. [S] Ten steps: list everyone (phone, email accounts, every platform: "your first 1000 leads"); start on the platform with the most contacts; personalise the greeting, "You haven't asked for anything"; 100 people per day, follow up up to three times, once per day for three days or until they respond; A-C-A (Acknowledge what they said, Compliment a trait, Ask a question leading toward the offer); offer after 3–4 exchanges by message or 3–4 minutes in person; first ones free; next platform; start charging; keep the list warm [Leads: Warm Outreach].
3. [S] Ask "do you know anybody," not "buy": "do you know anybody who is (struggle) looking to (dream outcome) in (time delay)? I'm taking on five case studies for free, because that's all I can handle… I even guarantee people get (dream outcome) or I work with them until they do… Does anyone you like come to mind? (Pause)… does anyone you hate come to mind?" "Of the people who say yes, most say they are interested"; interested, refer or both, all wins. Short form: "I help (ideal customer) get (dream outcome) in (time period) without (effort and sacrifice) and (increase perceived likelihood)." [Leads: Warm Outreach].
4. [S] First five free: "free so long as you promise to: 1) Use it 2) Give me feedback and 3) Leave a killer review… Does that sound fair?" Rule: "whenever you launch a new product or service, make the first five free": reps making offers, "you probably suck (for now)," guinea pigs; free customers convert, refer and testify. A refusal of free means the hidden costs are too high, "your 'free' stuff is too expensive"; ask why; "Yeses give me opportunity. Nos give me feedback." [Leads: Warm Outreach].
5. [S] Raise every five: "Once people start referring, start charging"; "80% off for the next five," then 60%, then 40%; "I increase my prices every five" creates real urgency; then +20% every five to the sweet spot. Keep the list warm with regular value and probe it with Dean Jackson's 9-word email, "Are you still looking to [4 word desire]?" (no links, no images), "Among the first things I do when I invest in a new business"; replies are "top priority" [Leads: Warm Outreach].
6. [S] Benchmarks: ~1 in 5 reply (100 → ~20); ~1 in 5 repliers take the free offer (→ 4); ~1 in 4 free takers later pay → ~1 customer per 100; volume guarantees a customer. 1,000 leads = "ten solid days of work. A month… including follow-ups." [E] $104,000: 1% of the list buys a $400 offer; 500 reach outs/week → 5 customers → $2,000/week, "two times the median household income." Starting out, customer-getting takes "four hours per day, minimum… the first thing you do when you get up." Limits: your time and the number of people who know you → content next [Leads: Warm Outreach].

Cold outreach

7. [S] One-to-one to strangers, "the more advanced cousin" that "sits atop the foundation of warm outreach"; use it to grow faster or to make lead flow more predictable; "It worked 100 years ago. It works today." The difference is trust, so three problems in order: no way to contact them (list), they ignore you (what to say), not yet interested (volume and follow-up); "Cold outreach is a numbers game" [Leads: Cold Outreach].
8. [S] List building, in order of accessibility: (1) software: many scraping tools, "test a representative sample (a few hundred) from each," pull everything from the ones whose data is current and responsive; (2) brokers: test samples, keep what works; (3) elbow grease: join the groups holding your audience, take contacts from directories, contact them outside the group. Databases are searchable by everyone; self-built lists are "the freshest." "Put your first 1,000 names together"; "more time than money… start at step three" [Leads: Cold Outreach].
9. [S] Personalise, then give big fast value. Personalise with 1–3 facts a friend might know and compliment them (subject line, first message, first sentence of a call); research and batch. [E] Dog-trainer call: local area code, "<Your name?>" and a pause, "I watched a few of your videos… That peanut butter trick really helped," nearby proof, "Will you be around at 4?"; "wanna buy some marketing services?" gets a hang-up. Big fast value: "blow their minds in under thirty seconds"; "Give away something for free people would normally pay for"; [E] a "game planning session" as magnet "felt like torture" for four months; "as much free service as we could possibly afford" tripled take rates. Scripts "never more than a page or two," emails "rarely more than half a page"; "Get your first 100 conversations or 10,000 emails out of the way before tweaking." [Leads: Cold Outreach].
10. [S] Volume: automate delivery and distribution "when ethical and available"; automation wins on leads per hour even at a lower rate. "The fewer leads you have, the less automation you should use" (1,000 hedge-fund managers → personalise every one). 10–20% of effort on untested technology. Follow up more times in more ways, "Act like you're actually trying to get ahold of these people"; email first, because non-response gives a reason to call. "Expect 2–3 conversations before a higher-ticket sale." Restart the list after 3–6 months: "Everything may be right except the timing." [Leads: Cold Outreach].
11. [S] Benchmarks, tracked "like the back of their hand" (the two untracked attempts failed). Phone: 100 calls, 20% pick up, 25% take the magnet → "four engaged leads" ([I] arithmetic gives 5), ~1 per hour. Email: 100 sent, 30% open, 10% reply → 3%, "Shoot for 3%"; [E] a niche high-ticket campaign hit 4%, ~a third convert → 1 customer per 100. DM: personal video or voice memo → 20% reply. Minimum "at least three times the lifetime profit of a customer compared to what it costs you to get them"; [E] that company "gets over 30:1." Hand off once converting leads out-earn a rep [Leads: Cold Outreach].
12. [S] Cost model: reps at $15/hour + $50 per show; leads $0.10; 200 calls/day → ~2 shows; $120 + $100 + $20 = $240 = $120 per show; 33% close → $360 per client; $3,600 profit → 10:1. "Then, you just add bodies. It is boring and tedious, but brutally effective." Solve for X: 1 customer per 100 emails → 100 customers = 10,000 emails = 333/day = three people at 111/day [Leads: Cold Outreach]. Offers' convergent example: 100 calls/rep/month, 4 per sale → 25 closes; 110 sales → 5 reps [Offers p90–91].
13. [S] Seven benefits: one crafted message, no ad fatigue; invisible to competitors; reliable; rules "hardly changed in thirty years"; FTC only, so banned industries can still advertise; no spokesperson, hence sellable ([E] the outreach team is why Gym Launch could be sold); hard to copy. Sequence: warm reach outs → reps → content → more reps → cold [Leads: Cold Outreach].
14. [S] Ramp: [E] July 2020; a competitor at ~$10M/month on "100% cold outreach" with ~30 reps; his build: September 0, October 2 ($32,000, team asked to stop), December 4, January 6, February 10, March 14, April 20, May 30 ($480,000); "it took almost a year," not the 12 weeks he expected; the third attempt worked because it tracked and used someone who had done it before [Leads: Cold Outreach]. [E] June 2021: a missed target traced to losing a rep every four weeks and 40 interviews per hire; group interviews doubled cold sales within a quarter: "We just didn't have enough people doing cold outreach" [Leads: Employees].
15. [S] Sales-lecture scripts: favourite outbound voicemail or email, "Hey [business], this is Alex, I'm calling in regards to [competitor]. Give me a call back when it's convenient," curiosity beats "calling from 123 Financial"; on callback, "looks like they're kicking your ass" [T-Sales 00:42–00:43]. Ghosted "because you didn't work leads fast or enough": the Kevin Hart "where you at?" meme has their highest response rate; 200 cold contacts → 5–10 re-engage [T-Sales 01:11–01:12]. "No for now ≠ no forever": pulse-check every 3–6 months with memes or one-line emails [T-Sales 02:24–02:26]. "Volume negates luck"; "He who asks the most times gets the most sales" [T-Sales 01:30, 02:08].
16. [S] Speed to lead for what outreach brings in (9-word replies, opt-ins, giveaway entrants): within 60 seconds → +391% close likelihood; after 5 minutes −80%; 50% pick whoever responds first; "lead quality going down" is usually a lead-working problem [T-Sales 00:03–00:07]. Local-lead call: "John?" (pause) "This is Alex." (pause) "I'm calling because you opted in on Facebook for our promotion," then "I've got 2:00 and 4:00 today, which works better?" [T-Sales 00:43–00:46]. Call every opt-in: "how'd you like that? want me to do it at your place? risk-free" [T-LeadMagnets 07:20]; the local-maps company calls at day seven [T-OffersLM 09:31]; giveaway non-winners get a private call and a seven-day claim window [MM: Giveaways].
17. [S] Outreach inside money models and launches: winback videos to 200 past customers offering $4,000 credit, ~20% took it, "~$1,900,000 of annual revenue" from one recording day [MM: Rollover Upsell]; 100 outbound messages/day is the criterion in his B2B Win-Your-Money-Back template [MM: Win Your Money Back]; for the book launch he emails his list twice and prior affiliates 1–2 more times [T-Affiliates 08:22–09:00]; text blasts to un-worked engaged leads made the $1.8M month [Leads: Section V intro].
18. [S] The volume lesson: motivated effort is "10 calls every three days"; clockwork is "200 dials every day before lunch"; the gap is "not 2× or 4×, it's like 50×" [T-Content 08:30–09:00]. "100 people over the last six weeks… one customer" is 1/42 of the work [Leads: Open To Goal]. Cadence by avatar: low-ticket customers "cannot be communicated with too much," affluent high-ticket buyers want minimal reach-out [T-CaseSaaS 00:34].

**Decision rules, formulas, thresholds**

- Warm: 100/day; ≤3 follow-ups, once/day for 3 days; A-C-A; offer after 3–4 exchanges or minutes; "do you know anybody"; first five free; charge when referrals start, 80/60/40% off per five, then +20% per five; yield 20% × 20% × 1 in 4 ≈ 1 per 100; $104,000 = 1% × $400 × 500/week; 9-word email; ≥4 h/day at the start [Leads: Warm Outreach].
- Cold: list via software (test a few hundred) → brokers → elbow grease, first 1,000 names; personalise with 1–3 facts; value in <30 seconds; scripts ≤1–2 pages, email ≤½ page; tweak after 100 conversations or 10,000 emails; fewer leads → less automation; 10–20% effort on new tech; email first; 2–3 conversations for high ticket; restart after 3–6 months [Leads: Cold Outreach].
- Phone 100 → 20% → 25% → ~4; email 3% target (4% sample, ~1/3 convert → 1 per 100); DM 20% reply; minimum 3× lifetime profit; $15/h + $50/show → $120/show → $360/client → 10:1; solve for X; hand off once leads out-earn a rep; ~a year to scale [Leads: Cold Outreach].
- "Calling about [competitor]"; meme to 200 → 5–10; pulse-check every 3–6 months; inbound <60 s +391%, >5 min −80% [T-Sales 00:03–00:05, 00:42, 01:12, 02:25].
- Rule of 100: 100 warm or cold reach outs/day [Leads: More Better New]; 50× effort gap [T-Content 09:00].

**When / when not, and stage**

- Stage: warm outreach is L1 and the "First Five Clients" method for any new product; advanced firms use it for re-engagement and new lines [Leads: Warm Outreach; Leads: The Roadmap]. Never stop it when adding content [Leads: Content II].
- Stage: cold outreach after warm outreach, reps and content; when you run out of people or want predictability; when money exceeds time; it needs full tracking and, in his experience, someone who has done it [Leads: Cold Outreach; Leads: Section III conclusion].
- Small universe → personalise everything; mass market → automate; platform-banned industries → cold outreach [Leads: Cold Outreach].
- Enterprise or government, 6–12-month cycles: decide-now techniques "are not going to apply" [T-Sales 02:05]. Affluent high-ticket buyers: minimal follow-up [T-CaseSaaS 00:34]. Never impersonate a second employee as setter [T-Sales 00:46].

**Author's examples with numbers**

- [E] May 2013: $51,128.13 in savings; free 12-week training for a charity donation and a testimonial; six yeses, then paid; 5–6 more by referral; ~$4,000/month [Leads: Warm Outreach].
- [E] Cold ramp 0 → 30 sales/month ($480,000) in ~nine months; magnet swap tripled take rates; 4% email engagement; 30:1 [Leads: Cold Outreach].
- [E] Winback: 200 videos, ~20% took $4,000 credit, ~$1.9M/year [MM: Rollover Upsell]. Reader: Rule of 100 → 8 meetings in 7 days, 4 closed [MM: Free Goodwill].

### Referrals and word of mouth

**Core statements**

1. [S] A referral is a referrer sending an engaged lead to the business; the best referrers are customers. Referrals are "the lowest cost, highest profit, and best quality leads" [Leads: Customer Referrals]. Customers who buy and then refer are lead getter #1; "ideally, every lead becomes a lead getter" [Leads: Section IV intro].
2. [S] Goodwill: "Price is what you charge. Value is what they get. The difference between price and value is goodwill." Goodwill produces word of mouth, which produces referrals. Build it by lowering price (temporary at best) or by giving more value [Leads: Customer Referrals]. Variants: "if you introduce something valuable to someone, they associate that value with you" [Offers p89]; "goodwill compounds faster than money" [T-Content 47:00].
3. [S] Referred customers buy more expensive things, buy more often and pay cash up front (higher LTGP), and cost nothing to acquire (lower CAC) [Leads: Customer Referrals].
4. [S] Growth = referrals in minus churn out. Above churn the business grows without other advertising; equal, other advertising is needed to grow; below, you advertise to stand still, "a hamster wheel of death". Core-four growth is linear; word of mouth is exponential, "nothing scales like word of mouth" [Leads: Customer Referrals]. Mirror image: selling 100, 200, 300 a year at 100% churn ends year three the same size as selling 100 a year and keeping them, but only the second compounds; direct-response businesses cap out because they lose everyone [T-CaseSaaS 35:58].
5. [S] Two reasons businesses get few referrals: the product is not as good as they think, and they do not ask. "If your product were exceptional, people would already know about it and you'd have more business than you could handle"; ask why customers are "too embarrassed to tell everyone they know". Competitors copied every asset "with little success" because "the best advertising is a happy customer"; products must "not only deliver, but delight" [Leads: Customer Referrals].
6. [S] Six ways to get more referrals by giving more value, mapped onto the parts of an ad and the value equation [Leads: Customer Referrals]: (1) call outs: sell only the customer type that gets the most value; (2) dream outcome: "slowly lower the promises you make when making offers. Keep lowering them until your close rates lower. At that point, stop"; (3) likelihood: find what the best customers did, force new customers to repeat it, match guarantee conditions to those actions; (4) time delay: faster wins, seven small deliveries beat one, "updates are wins", the lasting impression forms in the first 48 hours, BAMFAM, add 50% to timelines and "never late"; (5) effort: a monthly improvement loop (most common problem → fix → test on struggling customers → roll out); (6) CTA: tell them what to buy next, "if you don't, they'll still buy, but from someone else"; customers who fall off do not refer.
7. [S] "Asking for referrals only works when you treat it like an offer": how you give the incentive, what you incentivise with, how you ask [Leads: Customer Referrals].
8. [S] Seven ways to ask [Leads: Customer Referrals]: (1) one-sided, pay your average CAC to referrer or friend, demand a three-way introduction ("not just a name and number") right when they buy, "always ask for the spouse and give a household discount"; (2) two-sided, split the CAC (Dropbox, PayPal); (3) ask at checkout: "People who do our program with someone else tend to get 3x the results. Who else could you do this program with?"; (4) negotiation chip, the discount only for three introductions made now; (5) referral events over one to four weeks (Bring-a-friend, Spouse Challenge, Coach Challenge); (6) an ongoing programme mentioned in all content, outreach and ads; (7) unlockable bonuses for people who both refer and leave a testimonial, cheap enough to be two-sided. "The more insane you make the offer, the more people will refer."
9. [S] The 2025 book embeds the ask inside its offer models: Win Your Money Back criteria should "advertise the business" (posting, tagging, referring, reviews) [MM: Win Your Money Back]; giveaways carry two prizes, "if someone you refer wins, you win too" [MM: Giveaways]; 90%+-off gift cards usable only on other people mean "people pay you to refer their friends" [MM: Rollover Upsell]; a feature downsell is bartered for reviews, a video testimonial, three posts and two introductions [MM: Feature Downsells]; a giftable continuity discount card "becomes a lead magnet" [MM: Continuity Discount Offers]. [I] The 2025 taxonomy has no standalone "customer referral" attraction offer; referral mechanics ride inside the five named ones.
10. [S] Anti-referrals: the real cost of a lost customer is reputation, "you may lose nothing on this sale but lose three (or five) customers you would have had"; "everyone believes they're good enough to get referrals; no one believes they're bad enough to get anti-referrals" [T-CaseSaaS 25:04]. Tracking only refunds misses the mass who "tell everyone you suck" [T-CaseSaaS 31:00]. Customers proactively downgraded to a cheaper tier "never cancel" and become a source of referrals and testimonials [T-CaseSaaS 39:41]. Investor's first metric: how many of last year's customers still pay; "if we're not keeping anyone, we're just letting more people know we suck" [T-CaseSaaS 37:16].
11. [S] Referrals signal when to charge: the first five customers of a new product are free for use, feedback and a review; "once people start referring, start charging". Free customers pay three ways: they convert, they send paying referrals, their testimonials bring buyers [Leads: Warm Outreach].
12. [S] "Referrals are a way of doing business", not a tactic; referring risks the customer's goodwill with a friend, so add incentives and deliver: "I am compensated tomorrow for the value I provide today" [Leads: Customer Referrals].

**Decision rules, formulas, thresholds**

- Goodwill = value minus price; growth = referrals minus churn, as monthly percentages [Leads: Customer Referrals].
- Referral math: at LTGP:CAC 4:1 a customer costs 25% of one LTGP; each bringing two more makes it 12:1, "three customers for the price of one" [Leads: Customer Referrals].
- Baseline first: "figure out your referral percentages and churn percentages", then the six giving steps, then the asks [Leads: Customer Referrals].
- Incentive = average CAC, whole or split; CAC $200 → $200 cheque or $200 off; $500 programme → $100 cash plus $100 off, up to three friends [Leads: Customer Referrals].
- Gift cards, two variants: (a) one-third of programme cost, expires in 7-14 days, texted as a picture with the friend's name on it, plus purchasable cards at 90% off only for friends of customers [Leads: Customer Referrals]; (b) 90%+ off ($200 for $20), limit two, only for others, value = 20% of the next offer's price (a $200 card needs a $1,000+ offer) [MM: Rollover Upsell].
- Barter: $100 off for reviews, a video testimonial, three posts, two introductions [MM: Feature Downsells].
- Lower promises until close rate drops [Leads: Customer Referrals]; honest re-scripting left close rates unchanged [T-CaseSaaS 16:32].
- First impression: 48 hours [Leads: Customer Referrals]; 24-48 hours, worth "two, three months" of LTV [T-CaseSaaS 03:22].
- Stage targets: Level 4 = 25%+ of customers from referrals; $100M machine = a third [Leads: The Roadmap].
- CAC should rise only with CPMs (about 15%/year); a doubled CAC signals ([I] implied) anti-referrals [T-CaseSaaS 25:19].
- Bounty used: $2,000 cash per referred gym [Leads: How I Got Here].
- Win Your Money Back with referral criteria only if refund rate is under 5% [MM: Win Your Money Back].

**When / when not, and stage**

- Referrals are first in the natural order (referrals → employees → agencies → affiliates) [Leads: Section IV conclusion]. Roadmap Level 4 follows employees and precedes more channels; "this is where most people mess up. They let their product slip and never recover" [Leads: The Roadmap].
- Early stage: free-first-five exists to produce referrals and testimonials [Leads: Warm Outreach].
- Two-sided cash "worked really well for my local businesses"; unlockable bonuses are for owners who "don't like paying cash" [Leads: Customer Referrals].
- Spouse variant in sales: bring the partner onto the call, often close both, "better results together" [T-Sales 01:53].
- Not for an "unremarkable" product [Leads: Customer Referrals]. Low-ticket customers "cannot be communicated with too much"; affluent buyers want little contact [T-CaseSaaS 34:48].

**Connections and tensions**

[I] Stage-dependent: advertising buys chances early; compounding needs product quality.

### Affiliates and partners

**Core statements**

1. [S] An affiliate is a lead getter that is an independent business telling its audience to buy your stuff; unlike referrers they own businesses, advertise themselves, and agree to offer your product for money, free stuff, or both. You get them by advertising "a unique type of offer": a fast, simple, easy way to earn commissions. "One of the highest-leverage lead-getters out there" [Leads: Affiliates and Partners; T-Affiliates 00:36]. "Referrals" is the brick-and-mortar name; influencer, UGC, centre of influence and JV are the same thing [T-Affiliates 15:39].
2. [S] Leverage: 10 customers a month at $10,000 caps at $100,000/month ($1.2M in year one, then plateau); 10 affiliates a month each bringing one such customer monthly adds $100,000/month every month ($7.8M in twelve months). "In theory, once you build an affiliate army, you never need to advertise again" [Leads: Affiliates and Partners]. "Selling through other people's audiences is free, with the caveat that you give them some strong incentive"; do the core four to get affiliates, then hand them the core-four tools [T-Affiliates 17:10, 10:56; Leads: Section IV intro].
3. [S] Three hard parts: convince them to advertise someone else's stuff; make it yours; keep them advertising [Leads: Affiliates and Partners]. If any of "worth it, they know about it, easy" is missing, "you could just stop right there"; businesses also fail by not following up [T-Affiliates 11:31].
4. [S] Step 1, find: "a business with a warm audience full of people like your customers"; ask what your best customers buy, where they go, what they do, who employs them; categories: software, products, equipment, services, groups, events [Leads: Affiliates and Partners].
5. [S] Step 2, offer: callout, value, CTA with the affiliate as the customer. Value template: more money from current customers and more leads, high chance of working because their customers already want it, no building or support, selling tomorrow [Leads: Affiliates and Partners]. The programme starts with the offer to the affiliate; "if you make the offer good enough, you can move mountains" [T-Affiliates 00:53, 05:49].
6. [S] Step 3, qualify by investment: terms that force a fast first win by making them invest time, money and product use; "nine times out of ten, if they pay, they'll pay attention." Routes: make them a customer ("if they don't believe in your stuff enough to buy it, they probably shouldn't sell it") and/or a paid, certified expert, which funds onboarding of "every. single. affiliate." Favourite: both [Leads: Affiliates and Partners].
7. [S] Step 4, pay from max allowable CAC in tiers. Pay for what you want done (new and repeat customers; with tracking, earlier steps). Tier 1 = 25% of CAC on sign-up; tier 2 = 50% on activation (certification, set outreach, a launch); tier 3 = 100% on sustained performance. Leftover funds contests and recruitment, "or just plain pocket it" [Leads: Affiliates and Partners]. Launch variant: pay in free stuff, because 1099s for "a gazillion people" are a nightmare and non-monetary value can beat a 10% kickback on a cheap product [T-Affiliates 01:12].
8. [S] Prize architecture: always two tiers, "killers" with big audiences and "the everyman" bringing 3, 5 or 10 friends. Top prize unscalable ("a fireside with 1,000 people would work for nobody"), "big and sexy", with "varying levels of approximation" below. Prizes must benefit the affiliate and their audience (win-win-win), otherwise affiliates feel they are spending goodwill on a hard close; both ends must be able to win [T-Affiliates 01:40, 02:14, 05:04, 19:00].
9. [S] Step 5, launch: "Treat them like customers. Give them something good, fast. And nothing does that for affiliates like big launches and lots of cash." Do the work ahead so they plug and play. Whisper-Tease-Shout, "this is how you launch anything": whisper = curiosity, behind the scenes, can begin years out ("the longer something appears to take, the more an audience will value it"); tease = product, date, value elements; shout = actions, bonuses, scarcity, urgency, guarantees for "the first ones" [Leads: Affiliates and Partners]; reused for giveaway countdowns [MM: Giveaways].
10. [S] Collateral and compliance: never ask affiliates to "go make a campaign"; give pre-written, pre-approved emails, posts and ads that already worked, "blackbooks" per channel including texts for Slack threads, then "the winners" as data arrives. Writing it yourself keeps messaging honest and expectations uniform; compliance = "don't lie, don't exaggerate, state the facts" [T-Affiliates 07:12, 13:29, 14:13]. Speak each type's language: posts for Instagram and YouTube, text assets for email, X, LinkedIn and blogs, pre-approved ads for pure paid-ads operators. "As soon as there's friction, they'll stop" [T-Affiliates 15:39, 16:50].
11. [S] Activation: opt in → extra data → custom link at once (no belief in attribution, no traffic) → real-time leaderboard → collateral → regular communication ("cool stuff" plus the leaderboard "as frequently as possible") → personal help for top movers [T-Affiliates 11:58, 14:53, 15:10, 18:58]. Sharing email and ad performance lets affiliates learn the launch "from the outside in" [T-Affiliates 07:12].
12. [S] Step 6, integration, "sell an affiliate once and they send engaged leads for life": (1) affiliates give your lead magnet away with every purchase of theirs; (2) affiliates sell your lead magnet and keep the cash, "all profit and no work for them"; (3) affiliates sell your core offer for a higher LTGP share while you deliver. "I prefer to pay forever so my affiliates stay motivated to keep my customers forever. And, I never cap payouts" [Leads: Affiliates and Partners].
13. [S] Returns: compare the cost of an affiliate with the gross profit of all customers they send; "at least 3:1", want 5:1 to 10:1+; below 3:1 lower affiliate CAC, raise activation with a launch process, or raise LTGP with better integration. "'Affiliates can't work for my business,' the loser said. 'I have to make affiliates work for my business,' the winner said" [Leads: Affiliates and Partners].
14. [S] Partners inside the offer (2021): non-competing businesses give products or services free as bonuses for exposure to your customers; gains are goodwill, "future referral IOUs" and a stronger offer, "you can literally justify your entire price in the savings." "Jedi" level: a group discount for clients plus a commission to yourself, "negotiating with the purchasing power of all your customers at once" [Offers p151-153].
15. [S] Affiliate products fill money-model gaps at any stage: no offer, one offer, or $100M [MM: Section VI]; the storage mentor's free month included a moving-company kickback [MM: Start Here].
16. [S] Distribution and licensing: exclusive distribution is "fundamentally a B2B or B2B2C strategy"; a smaller business wins a distributor with lower cost, a retail kit, free staff training, "perks and incentives that more established players probably aren't willing to do or don't need to do" [T-Moats #8]. Owning the channel beats contracts; small versions are dominating a small area or owning a segment [T-Moats #13]. Licensing others' brands "piggybacks on 20 years of branding" for a royalty (Oreo at 8% of revenue; a supplement line on Swedish Fish flavours) [T-Moats #10].
17. [S] Agencies: lead-getting service businesses whose real cycle is a senior rep moving to the newest client while results decay. Use them for two things only, new methods and new platforms; "hiring an agency is all about investing in important skills you can't really learn anywhere else." The hire-to-learn opener: six months, "I'll pay extra for you to break down why you make the decisions you do", then train your team, then a lower-cost consulting arrangement; "compare your team's results to the agency's until you beat them. Then, cancel". Good agencies show word-of-mouth results, prominent clients, a waiting list, realistic expectations, clear tracking; "all good agencies are expensive, but not all expensive agencies are good" [Leads: Agencies].
18. [S] Employees, briefly: people trained to do the core four for you; 40 hours of doing → 4 hours of managing → a manager leading 200 hours. A business that runs without you is an asset ("$2,000,000 in profit... could easily be worth $10,000,000+"). Train by Document, Demonstrate, Duplicate; judge direction-following over results; retrain on any dip; "hire and train anyone willing" for ground-level roles [Leads: Employees]. Sales variant: group interviews, hire for the lowest skill deficiency [T-Sales 02:11].

**Decision rules, formulas, thresholds**

- Qualification price: 10-20% of the average active affiliate's first-twelve-month earnings ($40,000 → $4,000-$8,000) [Leads: Affiliates and Partners].
- Tuning: not enough start → lower the commitment; not enough follow through → raise it; physical products, raise the minimum every five sign-ups [Leads: Affiliates and Partners].
- Payout ceiling = max allowable CAC: $200 price, $40 fulfilment, 3:1 target → $120 business, $40 affiliate; tiers $10 / $20 / $40; blended 20/20/60 → $30 average, LTGP:CAC 3:1 → 4:1, "cutting marketing costs by 33% can translate into 10% to 20% more net profit" [Leads: Affiliates and Partners].
- Affiliate LTGP:CAC ≥ 3:1, aim 5:1 to 10:1+ [Leads: Affiliates and Partners].
- Launch cadence: whisper every 4-6 weeks until 60 days out, every 2-3 weeks until 30; tease weekly until 14 days, twice a week until 3; shout twice a day from 3 days, every few hours on launch day until 2 hours out, then every 30 minutes [Leads: Affiliates and Partners].
- Bottom-tier activation by friction: in-person ≈ 2, text intro ≈ 4-5, opt-in ≈ 10+ [T-Affiliates 03:50].
- Promotion: email own list only twice ("promote the book, not promote promoting the book"); prior affiliates 1-2 extra; a button on every buyer's thank-you page; no paid ads for recruitment [T-Affiliates 08:38, 09:29, 10:56]. Affiliate page = prizes, what to do, how you help [T-Affiliates 06:39].
- Integration mix used: strategy 1 twice a year as an event plus strategy 3 ongoing; give the affiliate 100% of workshop money, "they'll want to do it more" [Leads: Affiliates and Partners].
- Start with ten to twenty affiliates, fix the offer on their feedback, then scale on their results [Leads: Affiliates and Partners].
- Agencies: no money → no agency; six-month term, then discounted consulting; run both teams until yours wins; "don't be cheap" [Leads: Agencies].
- Employees: cost per engaged lead = payroll ÷ engaged leads ($100,000 / 1,000 = $100; 1 in 10 convert → $1,000 CAC; $4,000 LTGP → 4:1). Diagnostic: leads lack problem or money → advertising problem; have both and do not buy → sales problem [Leads: Employees].

**When / when not, and stage**

- Affiliates come last in the natural order because owners manage outsiders "only when [they] have a handle on managing people inside"; expect three to six months to crack a new source [Leads: Section IV conclusion]. Roadmap Level 5 extended paid ads to potential affiliates [Leads: The Roadmap].
- Free-stuff pay is stated for a cheap product with very many affiliates [T-Affiliates 01:12]; cash tiers for a product with real margin [Leads: Affiliates and Partners].
- Distribution deals: B2B or B2B2C; ownership is the large-capital version [T-Moats #8, #13].
- Agencies only with money; expect to pay agency and own team at once for a period [Leads: Agencies]. Employees at Roadmap Level 3, after personal inputs are maxed [Leads: The Roadmap].

**Author's examples with numbers**

- [E] Prestige Labs, 1 Dec 2018: $1,000,000+ on affiliate software and training, $3,000,000 inventory before sales; weeks 1-3 $150,000 total; week four $450,000+, then $429k, $383k, $411k, $452k weekly; 300+ orders/day from 400+ affiliates; "no paid ads, no sales team" [Leads: Affiliates and Partners]; "almost 100% affiliate sales" [T-Affiliates 19:15]; $1.7M/month [T-Content 01:00].

ALAN plus Prestige Labs: $75,000,000+ from 5,000+ affiliates [Leads: Affiliates and Partners]. The programme added $1.5M/month to Gym Launch [Leads: Section V intro].
- [E] Return: $4,000 per affiliate; $120,000 sales; 75% margin; 40% payout; $54,000 left → 12.5:1 [Leads: Affiliates and Partners].
- [E] Case studies: a $50M tax-prep business gives every affiliate's customer a free LLC, then sells bookkeeping, zero paid ads; Prestige Labs gyms run a free 28-day challenge, then sell $50-$1,000 of supplements at a nutrition orientation, 20 clients × 70% = 14 buyers per gym per month; chiropractors run a workshop through gyms at $29-$99 (30 × $99 = $2,970 to the gym), then one or two adjustments in every membership [Leads: Affiliates and Partners].
- [E] $100M Leads launch: 500,000 registrations, a third referred, "30-something thousand" affiliates. 2025 plan: top 10 fireside and dinner; top 50 three-hour HQ Q&A; 10+ opt-ins get an audiobook, a raffle for 50 seats, the recording; two days on prize permutations [T-Affiliates 00:00, 02:03, 05:43, 05:49, 08:59].
- [E] Pain-clinic partner stack (massages, $100 adjustments, $50 food, $150 orthotics, $100 club, $100/month pharmacy) turns a $400 offer into +$350 "pure profit" in commissions; Prestige Labs athletes: 30% off, paid 40% of net sales [Offers p151-152].

### Authority and proof

**Core statements**

1. [S] The unspoken question is "why should I listen to you?"; it is "the frame that the content is consumed in". Most people create "from a weak frame"; "the real reason our audience is growing is that we're talking about stuff that we have evidence to support." The content mechanics are "contextual"; the frame is "the more eternal stuff" [T-Content 22:00].
2. [S] Evidence over confidence: "you have to do things so that you have evidence that you can support why you are good"; "confidence is self-delusion, trying to make up for what you don't have evidence for." People seek truth through the most extreme example because "most people cannot separate information from source"; outside your authority say "I don't have authority to speak on that" [T-Content 34:00].
3. [S] Damaging admissions "are the single greatest thing that built trust": what follows an admitted deficiency is believed more [T-Content 34:00]. The anti-guarantee is framed the same way [Offers p167]. "Very specific on the negatives" lets you be "vaguer on the positives"; if you nail their problems "they believe you" [T-LeadMagnets 12:22]. Lower promises raise believability; promising the moon "can make people doubt you" [T-CaseSaaS 16:32].
4. [S] Niche authority and sequence: you "probably won't be respected as an authority on business until you've achieved mega success"; be a niche authority where "you compete against 20 guys", but "walk the talk". Do, then talk, then do bigger. "How I" not "how to": "everyone is unquestionably an expert on your own life." Trade-off: "you will not gain a mega following at first, but you will accomplish, which will set you on the path to be broader later" [T-Content 25:00, 30:00, 52:00]. Book version: "how to" → "how I", "the best way" → "my favorite ways", especially starting out; a small local business "probably shouldn't make general business content. Not at first" (puddle → pond → lake → ocean) [Leads: Post Free Content II].
5. [S] Proof in the offer: perceived likelihood of achievement is a value driver, "people pay for certainty", communicated through messaging, proof, what the offer includes or excludes, and guarantees [Offers p80-81]. The warm-outreach script raises it with results, reviews, awards, certifications, guarantees and results from people like the prospect [Leads: Warm Outreach]; ads use explained-away past failures, success of people like them, authority assurances, guarantees [Leads: Paid Ads I]; each bonus gets proof, a stat, a past client or personal experience [Offers p149]. Caveat: "larger-than-life claims are the easiest to establish (and therefore less unique). Anyone can make a promise" [Offers p76].
6. [S] Proof as the sales frame: "the best way to get someone to expect value in the future is to provide value today"; how dialled the business is before the ask is "a huge boost to perceived likelihood" [T-CaseSaaS 07:45]. Free content does the same: "How do I know it's going to work? It already has" [T-Content 43:00]; "99% of people aren't gonna buy, but they will create (or destroy) your reputation based on the value of your free stuff" [Leads: Offers and Lead Magnets].
7. [S] Testimonials for closer conviction: "sales is passing conviction from one person to another; an education process over a bridge of trust." In a slump, read testimonials and give kudos to the rep who closed that customer; a convicted salesperson "will always have the correct tonality" [T-Sales 00:34]. Results witnessed: on weigh-out days, after watching 20 people lose 20-30 lb, close rates were "absurd, like 100%" [T-Sales 00:36]. Conviction is "one of the strongest predictors of sales close rate"; routing complaints to reps destroys it [T-CaseSaaS 20:18].
8. [S] Proof-Promise-Plan: set the agenda in 15-20 seconds with proof ("we've done this with lots of people like you"), promise and plan, the same structure as his YouTube openers "because attention works the same way" [T-Sales 00:50]; customer success opens onboarding with a personalised proof-promise-plan [T-Sales 01:48].
9. [S] Edification of the closer: the setter transfers authority, "I'm going to put you on with Shawn, he's helped 400 people just like you get [what you said you wanted]", and the closer keeps the frame [T-Sales 00:39]. A rep without personal authority uses foils, someone like the prospect who succeeded or an authority inside the business [T-Sales 01:39]; the secretary and mechanic closes borrow the business's authority the same way [T-Sales 01:22]. Posing as a second employee to edify himself is labelled deception, "you cannot do that" [T-Sales 00:46].
10. [S] "Best of" lists: the list will not get business; telling people you are on it will ("did you hear about us from [city]'s best boot camp list?"); even a "no" answer frames you. You usually "just pay five grand to the magazine" [T-Sales 00:37, 00:39]. Related signals: announce sell-outs and honest capacity, "scarcity implies social proof" [Offers p136, p138]; authorities charge egregious rates on implied demand [Offers p132]; a good agency shows word-of-mouth results and a waiting list [Leads: Agencies].
11. [S] Brand as a moat: "my personal favorite" because "you can make [it] with skill" without capital; a brand becomes synonymous with the product and lets a commodity convert higher, cost less per customer and price at a premium. Built by keeping promises and making "associations clearly and deliberately over a long period of time with what their customers value", and disassociating from what they do not [T-Moats #6]. Copying your offer or content is not IP theft; "pretending" to be you is [T-Moats #10]. "The content isn't the compounding asset, the audience is" [Leads: Post Free Content I]; goodwill compounds, so delay the ask [T-Content 47:00; Offers p128]. A big brand still needs lead magnets: a direct "buy" CTA reaches only the two warmest awareness segments [T-OffersLM 05:42].
12. [S] "We have evidence" content: "give away the secrets, sell the implementation"; "if you're not afraid that you're giving away too much, you're not giving away enough"; make free material better than others' paid; speak on "experience that no one can challenge"; be a "mini researcher" of your own business [T-Content 43:00, 52:00]. # Cluster D. Converting leads: qualification, sales, follow-up, conversion mechanics

Scope: what the sources say about turning an engaged lead into a paying customer: who gets a sales slot, what happens in the conversation, what happens between touches, and which levers outside the conversation move show and close rates. Backbone: the 2.5 h sales lecture [T-Sales]; the three books add scripts, thresholds and the offer-side view; the SaaS case supplies the customer-success mirror. Affiliate qualification is only pointed to (cluster C). Tags: [S] stated rule, [E] author's example or number, [I] inference.

### Qualification

**Core statements**

1. [S] BANT (from an IBM study): a lead is qualified for a closer once the setter has confirmed Budget, Authority, Need and Timing. Where: setting call, pre-call application, restated on the closing call, always before asking for money. Warning: people say yes to all four and change their mind when money is asked [T-Sales 00:41].
2. [S] "Kill zombies early": the zombies are the BANT items. "Anyone else who needs to be involved in this decision?"; labelling supplies need; "good to start now?" supplies timing; budget via the application, or best of all inside the pain cycle, where what they tried before reveals need, timing, budget and "was your husband involved in that decision?". Rule: whatever consistently blows up in the close gets loaded earlier, before money is on the table; after the ask the same questions feel like extraction [T-Sales 01:37].
3. [S] Lead scoring (his companies): approximate probability of closing as a score and/or colour: green / yellow (slightly qualified, lower quality) / red (unqualified). [E] 800 credit score plus $500k income outscores six jobs and 19 kids for a timeshare [T-Sales 02:26].
4. [S] "Feed the killers": best leads to the best closers, worst to the worst, lowest net waste. Side effects: a top rep at $500k-$3M attracts people who work below market for the shot; the bottom tier is a "churn factory" where newcomers learn on the hardest leads and qualify themselves for better ones [T-Sales 00:12]. For new reps "yellow = gold": take every call allowed. Condition: if someone literally cannot benefit, don't sell; if merely less qualified, practise closing [T-Sales 02:27].
5. [S] Qualify before "call now" and before any full sales slot: put the call-now option after qualification [T-Sales 00:07]; reps pulling appointments forward qualify first, "which you should do anyway for anything taking a full sales slot" [T-Sales 00:09].
6. [S] Lead-magnet takers: to "I don't want freebie seekers", add a dropdown that qualifies; route qualified people to the good thing, the rest to something they might qualify for [T-LeadMagnets 07:20]. Free service work only for the perfect lead: budget, authority to decide, clear need, wants to act now; agreed up front; anything else is "a terrible waste of your time" [T-LeadMagnets 18:42]. [I] BANT on the lead-magnet side.
7. [S] Self-qualifying capture: a "CEO" shirt only for people who prove they are CEOs yields "an incredibly qualified list" [T-LeadMagnets 22:22]; yes-questions and if-then callouts in ads ("Do you have a home worth over $400,000?") [Leads: Paid Ads I]; giveaway eligibility questions and qualifying actions: "the more work to enter, the fewer enter, but the more qualified; find your sweet spot" [MM: Giveaways].
8. [S] The avatar as gate. [E] Gym Launch: microgym owner with ~100 members, a signed lease, at least one employee, wanting to help clients lose weight; personal trainers and online coaches turned down even though they could be helped, for product focus and high-converting messaging [Offers p54]. Variant: find what the most successful customers share and "sell only" people meeting those criteria; [E] a PR company's lowest-churn niche was 15% of the business; narrowing the callouts to it broke a years-long plateau [Leads: Customer Referrals, way 1].
9. [S] Lead = "the problem to solve and the money to spend" [Leads: Section I intro]. Diagnostic: "Do my engaged leads have the problem I solve and the money to spend?" No: advertising problem. Yes but too few: advertising problem. Yes but not buying: sales problem [Leads: Employees; Leads: Paid Ads II].
10. [S] Commitment gates inside the money model: "are you here for free stuff or lasting results?" [MM: Decoy Offer]; "on a scale from 1-10 how bad do you wanna do this?", 8+ continue, 7 or below → "why not a 10?" → feature downsell [MM: Payment Plan Downsells]; "if someone doesn't agree to put their card down and do the work, I won't sell them. They complain more and they convert less"; "if this program got you the result, will you stay long-term?", if no, no trial [MM: Trial With Penalty]; refund asked = give it, then "get better customers" [MM: Four Types of Offers].
11. [S] Price, name and guarantee as filters: raising price attracts "the best clients, easiest to satisfy" [Offers p65-66]; $25,000 tickets yielded "more-qualified donors" [Offers p124]; the anti-guarantee script "if you're the type of customer who needs a guarantee before taking a jump, you are not the type of person we want to work with" [Offers p167]; the name attracts the right avatar and repels the wrong one [Offers p175, p181]; segment "to sell to the right wallet" [T-Moats #7]; if a speed premium will not sell, go up-market [T-OffersLM 01:40].
12. [S] Decision-maker prevention: if the purchase materially affects a household or business, involve the partner in the setting process and bring both on the call [T-Sales 01:54].
13. [S] Enterprise/committee exception: government or large enterprise with 6-12-month cycles and multiple stakeholders, "much of the stuff I covered here is not going to apply"; the techniques force a decision now and committees cannot decide now. Instead stay in the pocket and multi-thread: three on their side times two on yours creates many ties [T-Sales 02:05].
14. [S] "Qualify, never lie": turning away prospects raises close rate because you know you are helping; trying to close everybody means "either you become a sociopath or your sales go down" [T-Sales 02:07]. Ethical test: if they knew everything you know they should buy [T-Sales 00:37].
15. Pointer: affiliates are qualified by investment, buying the product and/or paying 10-20% of an average affiliate's first-year earnings for certification [Leads: Affiliates and Partners, Step 3]. Cluster C.

**Decision rules, formulas, thresholds**
- Qualified for a closer = all four BANT items confirmed; restate on the close; always before the ask [T-Sales 00:41]. Whatever kills deals at the close is asked before the ask [T-Sales 01:37].

**When / when not, and stage**

**Author's examples with numbers**
- [E] #1 of 3,000 timeshare reps five of six years; scored allocation replaced round robin; income $250k → $3M; company "5x'd" nationwide [T-Sales 00:12].

- [E] CEO shirt at $3-4; 1 in 20 converting = "$100 for a CEO feels like a good idea" [T-LeadMagnets 22:22]. Service magnet: $25 cost, $250+ value, 1 in 4 buys → $100 CAC, qualified recipients only [T-LeadMagnets 19:17].

**Connections and tensions**
- [I] Three forms of one gate: BANT [T-Sales 00:41]; "budget, authority, need, act now" [T-LeadMagnets 18:42]; "problem and money" [Leads: Employees]. Leads drops authority and timing, consistent with an advertising-side test.
- Resolved by the author: best leads to best closers versus new reps on the hardest leads; one system, the bottom tier trains and filters [T-Sales 00:12, 02:27].
- Open: tight qualification ("terrible waste", "turn away prospects") [T-LeadMagnets 18:42; T-Sales 02:07] versus "you can close everyone" [MM: Decoy Offer], "everybody buys something" [MM: Downsell Offers Conclusion], "no means no for this thing, not for everything" [MM: Rules of Downselling]. His boundary is benefit, not means [T-Sales 02:27], plus the card-and-work commitment [MM: Trial With Penalty].
- [I] "Lead quality going down is usually a lead-working problem" [T-Sales 00:05] pulls against Leads' diagnostic that treats unqualified engaged leads as an advertising problem [Leads: Employees]; they agree only once response speed is ruled out.

**Not covered by the sources**

### Sales

**Core statements** (the lecture's six elements, then the books)

1. [S] Element 1, multipliers: "things outside the sale affect sales more than anything inside the sale"; closing skill cannot give 400%, the surroundings can [T-Sales 00:06]. The eight: sell seven days a week (104 extra days, +29% minimum; weekend bookings otherwise stack onto Monday and no-show) [T-Sales 00:00]; respond in under a minute (391%; test every other activity with "does it have an immediate 4X?") [T-Sales 00:03]; a "call now" option after qualification, and hire a rep when one rep can 2-3x revenue for far less than the onboarding cost [T-Sales 00:07]; 15-minute start increments plus an off-the-call SOP for pulling appointments forward; faked-busy calendars "don't do anything", tested [T-Sales 00:09]; feed the killers [T-Sales 00:12]; reward show-up rates [T-Sales 00:15]; the reminder sequence [T-Sales 00:16]; same-day/next-day availability only, and if that means "I can't book all my appointments", hire more salespeople [T-Sales 00:19]. Numbers in Follow-up and Conversion mechanics.
2. [S] Element 2, training. Most sales orgs "run churn factories"; if you can train, you can pay below market: the owner's arbitrage [T-Sales 00:20]. Role-play is "the single greatest skill" for a trainer; set the no-ego frame; never let a rep run the whole script and then get 20-30 corrections, because people take one piece of feedback at a time and trainability is inversely correlated with feedback delay: announce the one thing, reinforce in real time, on a miss "say it like this, try again" and repeat the correct line 2-6 times; pre-frame "I'll stop you 30 or 40 times"; one skill for a week or two; huddle for the team issue, one-on-ones for the individual [T-Sales 00:21]. The manager listens to recordings, notes the ~28 faults, works only the one with the highest expected close-rate gain [T-Sales 00:25]. The best closer is usually not the best manager (hunters vs coaches) [T-Sales 00:26]. Compete against an external rival, not rep-vs-rep [T-Sales 00:27]. Record every customer call; sales and CS review sales game tape and an onboarding call together weekly: CS flags phrasings that create bad expectations, sales finally sees what happens after the card runs; this cuts the 5-10% of closed sales that back out [T-Sales 00:29]. "No mysticism": record yourself when hot; "good closers try and get hot; great closers never get cold" [T-Sales 00:32]. Slumps: read testimonials and give the closer kudos; a convicted rep "will always have the correct tonality" [T-Sales 00:34]. Definition: "Sales is passing conviction from one person to another; an education process over a bridge of trust"; fill the fewest gaps needed to decide [T-Sales 00:37]. Leads' 3Ds for any trained role: document, demonstrate, duplicate; judge direction-following over results; retrain on any dip [Leads: Employees]. Conviction is "one of the strongest predictors of close rate"; never make reps field unhappy customers [T-CaseSaaS 20:18].
3. [S] Element 3, before the sale. "The sale starts at the click": everything between click and call changes close rates independent of what is said [T-Sales 00:38]. Edify the closer: "I'm going to put you on with Shawn, he's helped 400 people just like you... he has an opening this afternoon"; a "best of" list (~$5k to be on) does nothing until you tell people [T-Sales 00:39]. BANT [T-Sales 00:41]. Favourite outbound voicemail: "I'm calling in regards to [competitor], give me a call back" [T-Sales 00:42]. Favourite local-lead call: "John?" (tone up, pause) → "who's this?" → "This is Alex." (as if he should know) → "you opted in on Facebook for our promotion, wanted to make sure you weren't a crazy person on the internet..." with three memory cues, then the alternative close: "I've got 2:00 and 4:00 today, which works better?" Never ask them to pick a time [T-Sales 00:44]. Prep five minutes per call; setter notes in the CRM; the closer opens by restating; making the customer repeat themselves is strongly negative, repeating back strongly positive; notes pass to CS [T-Sales 00:47].
4. [S] Element 4, the sale. Agenda in 15-20 seconds with Proof → Promise → Plan; "100 golden BBs", treat every variable as under your control; in B2B get to the point [T-Sales 00:50]. Script adherence first: he would rather the whole team be word-perfect and closing no one, because then the script can be fixed centrally; the first two-thirds, up to the ask, is word for word; ~12 questions with one or two follow-ups [T-Sales 00:53]. Then pacing (faster toward the close, slower and lower at the ask), emphasis, tone; a four-cue tone guide in the script (underline = slow, CAPS = emphasise, italics = space, bold) [T-Sales 00:56]. CLOSER, for transactional closes: C, clarify why they are here (nobody calls "for more information"; remind them of the action they took) [T-Sales 00:58]; L, label the gap [T-Sales 00:59]; O, overview past pain: "deprivation creates motivation", "the pain is the pitch", quantify time, money, status and cost of inaction; everything tried maps onto a three-pillar pitch, "highlight the good and name the missing pillar" [T-Sales 01:00, 01:04]; S, sell the vacation, not the flight: under two minutes on the product, ~90% of learning time on the prospect's pains, one metaphor per pillar; "details get you into the weeds, and that's where you die" [T-Sales 01:06]; E, explain away concerns: on a yes, "let me get your ID" [T-Sales 01:12]; R, reinforce the decision [T-Sales 01:49].
5. [S] Element 5, closing. Train back to front: payment processing, then the ask, then the top [T-Sales 01:13]. "Closing begins once someone says no"; the one time to shut up is when they say yes [T-Sales 01:14]. "Prep = smooth": know in one breath what you will say to every objection [T-Sales 01:16]. Stall sequence: "what's your main concern?" → "what are you afraid of having happen?" → "what variables are you using to decide?" → "what makes this a yes?" → "what makes this a no?" [T-Sales 01:17]. All-purpose closes: "do you think the reason you're on the phone today is because you've struggled to make this decision in the past?" and the 1-10 close ("what would make it a 10?", "why not a one?") [T-Sales 01:20]. Details are death traps: never answer a question you do not already know the answer to; ask about their question; the secretary and mechanic closes appeal to the authority of the business; a detail objection is a preference objection, "change the variables, change the outcome" [T-Sales 01:22]. AAA: acknowledge (repeat their last phrase), associate (with something positive or a foil: a customer like them, an authority in the business), ask; "he who asks the most times gets the most sales", rapport gives "unlimited shots on goal" [T-Sales 01:28]. "The person asking is the person closing"; "no one can disagree with a question, be like smoke"; never disagree; ask what answer they want, then chunk up: "if we got to the same result, would you care how?" [T-Sales 01:32]. Kill zombies early; same side of the table; "when in doubt, say it back" [T-Sales 01:37]. Get permission before getting real; without authority, use foils [T-Sales 01:40]. Stack 2-3 closes on one obstacle [T-Sales 01:42]. Know the fallacy per category: time = priorities; decision maker = "support not permission"; details = preferences; money = "nothing is ever too expensive, it's just not worth it"; stalls = fear of a wrong decision or not knowing how to decide; 2-3 closes per category in your own words [T-Sales 01:44]. Lost? Best case / worst case, "especially strong with any guarantee" [T-Sales 01:47]. R: a "handshake" with CS, not a handoff; BAMFAM [T-Sales 01:49]. FAQ closes: cheap competitor ("if ours were the same as theirs, which would you do? Why?... that's why we're more") [T-Sales 01:51]; the ID close (wallet on the table, "I'll trade you for whichever card"; on Zoom "hold your ID up, we try to be PCI compliant", a reason he admits inventing) [T-Sales 01:52]; decision maker, "the hardest of all closes": "what would happen if they said no?" (1 in 3 would do it anyway), "what would their main concern be?", past agreements, support not permission [T-Sales 01:54]; "circle back in a couple quarters": "what will you be doing instead? does that get better returns?", put math to it, stop the moment the prospect is better served not buying [T-Sales 01:59]; no guarantee: "if you need a guarantee this is not for you; is it fair to guarantee work I have no input in?" [T-Sales 02:03]; "let me do your free stuff first": "absolutely, do that before we kick you off; either way, let's get started" [T-Sales 02:04]; enterprise exception [T-Sales 02:05].
6. [S] Element 6, behaviours, hiring, comp. Whoever cares most about the prospect wins; turn away those it no longer serves [T-Sales 02:07]. Volume negates luck: do double your best closer's effort; skill rises in stages (~2-3 months, ~1 year, ~2-3 years), income spikes at the end [T-Sales 02:08]. Résumés do not matter in transactional selling: group interviews of 1-2 minutes each; hire for the lowest skill deficiency (a script takes 2-3 weeks to teach; "kindness" hides ~20 sub-skills); reverse it for rare expertise [T-Sales 02:12]. Trust is a continuum, "a bigger plane needs a longer runway"; many 2-4-call closes mean insufficient pre-education [T-Sales 02:20]. Prospect talks two-thirds of the time [T-Sales 02:22]. "Don't be cute" [T-Sales 02:23]. No for now ≠ no forever [T-Sales 02:25]. Trash man [T-Sales 02:26]. 7. [S] Offers: persuasion last. "Always hit your guarantee hard, even if you don't have one" [Offers p156-157]; the best-case/worst-case close on a $500-of-value guarantee, "the only thing guaranteed not to help you is walking out of here today" [Offers p161-162]. Group selling deferred to a "Volume IV $100M Sales" [Offers p125, p148].
8. [S] Leads: conversation scripts. The book ends at the raised hand [Leads: Offers and Lead Magnets]. Warm: A-C-A, acknowledge what they said, compliment a trait, ask a question leading toward the offer; after 3-4 exchanges (3-4 minutes in person) ask "do you know anybody who is (struggle) looking to (dream outcome) in (time)? I'm taking on five case studies for free because that's all I can handle... I guarantee (outcome) or I work with them until they do... anyone you like come to mind? ...anyone you hate?" You never ask them to buy; "most say they are interested." Short form: "I help (ideal customer) get (dream outcome) in (time) without (effort) and (likelihood)." Free offer: "free so long as you promise to 1) use it 2) give me feedback 3) leave a killer review. Does that sound fair?" On a no: "why?" and "what would I have to do to make it worth it?" [Leads: Warm Outreach, Steps 5-7]. Content ask: "I'm looking for 5 (avatar)... if you don't get (outcome) I will 1) hand you your money back 2) work with you until you do" [Leads: Post Free Content II]. Cold: trust must be won in seconds, so personalise (1-3 facts a friend would know) and lead with big fast value; the dog-trainer call: local area code, "<name?>" plus pause, "it's Alex, I watched your videos, that peanut butter trick helped", company intro, nearby proof, "do you offer similar services?", a joke, "walk you through it later today, will you be around at 4?"; "wanna buy some marketing services?" gets a hang-up. Scripts one to two pages, emails half a page; tweak after 100 conversations or 10,000 emails; "expect 2-3 conversations before a higher-ticket sale" [Leads: Cold Outreach]. Reps send "greatest hits" content before and after calls [Leads: Post Free Content II]. Referral asks in the sale: "people who do our program with someone else get 3x the results. Who else could you do this with?"; the chip: "I can't do less than $500 down, but if you make a 3-way text introduction to a few friends right now, I'll cut the initiation fee" [Leads: Customer Referrals, ways 3-4].
9. [S] Money Models: offer-sequence scripts. "Hard selling is for weak products"; make the offer available when the customer has the problem [MM: Four Types of Offers]. Decoy: premium first, decoy "in your back pocket"; "free stuff or lasting results?"; if free, contrast and ask "which will get you to your goal faster?"; assumed close, "this is what everyone does, just a formality, let me get your ID and credit card", almost bored [MM: Decoy Offer]. Classic upsell: "you don't want anything else, do you?"; bonuses one at a time for fence-sitters, all of them for early yeses; put the thing in their hands first [MM: Classic Upsell]. Menu: unsell (cross out), prescribe ("explain how to use it as if they already have"), A/B ("chocolate or vanilla?"), "use the card on file?" (first purchase: "what card do you wanna use?") [MM: Menu Upsell]. Anchor: premium at 5-10x first; on the gasp, "do you care about the designer much?"; main = same primary features at about one fifth; "which card do you prefer?"; treat the anchor as real [MM: Anchor Upsell]. Rollover: "I wanted to give you your money back... $500 as credit towards staying pain-free for good"; one-time urgency [MM: Rollover Upsell]. Rules of downselling: no means no to this offer; downsells are trades; personalise, don't pressure; same things in new ways; never drop price to get a buy; customers talk, test prices deliberately [MM: Rules of Downselling]. Payment plans, seven steps, up to nine offers: "it's $15, but $10 if you prepay, that's what most people do"; financing / "would you rather I decide your payment terms or you decide?" / layaway; "when's the next time you get paid? half today and the rest then?"; the 1-10 check; thirds; even spread; free trial last; seesaw for newer reps ("giant monthly payments or tiny ones?"); if stuck, "pull your chair to their side of the table" [MM: Payment Plan Downsells]. Trial with penalty: offer it last ("how about we just get ya started for free... let me get your ID"); always the card ("that's just how we've always done it"); sell staying and paying; fees explained after the card, initialled [MM: Trial With Penalty]. Feature downsell: "if you don't want the option to get your money back, you can pay less. Or keep your guarantee, which would you prefer?"; remove highest to lowest value; "deal?" or "fair enough?" after each; the cheapest is "The Minimum", "so nothing more than the minimum package then?"; temperature-check after two, alternate with payment plans; stay "a helpful guide" [MM: Feature Downsells]. Continuity bonus: anchor the bonus, then "do you want to know how you can get this for free? Become a VIP member today, or buy it for $XXX, which would you prefer?" [MM: Continuity Bonus Offers]. Waived fee: "if you only wanna test us, you cover the start-up costs; if you commit longer, I'll cover them" [MM: Waived Fee Offer]. Hint at the next offer early ("everyone wants to know when my next intensive starts, I'll get to it at the end") [MM: Pay Less Now or Pay More Later].
10. [S] SaaS case: rescript to lower the promise. Sales calls contained phrasings that made the product sound easier and faster than it was; after rescripting the company "sold the same amount", close rates "didn't change at all"; a lower promise raises perceived likelihood because it is believable [T-CaseSaaS 16:00-17:08]. Leads' rule: "slowly lower the promises you make... until your close rates lower. At that point, stop" [Leads: Customer Referrals, way 2]. R applied after purchase: restate C, L, O and re-sell the vacation; tying next steps to the stated goal "doubled or tripled" follow-through at ALAN [T-CaseSaaS 04:31, 05:39].
11. [S] The frame set by free value: a prospect who grew $1M → $3-5M on your free content asks no "how do I know it will work": "it already has" [T-Content 45:30]; "specific on the negatives, vaguer on the positives" makes promises believable [T-LeadMagnets 12:22]; damaging admissions build trust in negotiation [T-Content 37:00].

### Follow-up

**Core statements**

1. [S] Speed to lead: contact within 60 seconds of opt-in gives +391% close likelihood, "you could 4X your business tomorrow"; 50% of prospects go with whoever responds first; over 5 minutes the likelihood drops 80% ("four out of five times you would have closed now goes to 20%"). "People have huge motivation for a tiny period of time." Follow-up should be personal, fast, multiple times over multiple days, but the first acknowledgement matters most; customers judge how "buttoned up" you are by response speed [T-Sales 00:03-00:07]. Same premise in the CTA rule: motivation is huge "for a tiny time" [Leads: Paid Ads I].
2. [S] Off-the-call follow-up: reps call new leads within minutes, qualify, and offer "I had an opening later today"; even if the lead cannot move, they were contacted fast and are pre-qualified [T-Sales 00:09].
3. [S] Reminders: automated ones are mandatory and should look automated; layer three manual touches, the highest-show ones in ALAN's data: 24 hours before, morning of, one hour before. The 24-hour touch shows prep so the prospect feels worse about not showing ("I set aside a t-shirt for you, what size?"; "I looked over your site, talked to my team, three things we can help with"). Five minutes of prep per prospect gives ~30% higher show-up; "in 60 minutes a day you can increase throughput by 30%." iMessage over SMS; voice memos and video [T-Sales 00:16-00:19]. [E] First ad test: called every lead, texted an hour before [Leads: Paid Ads I].
4. [S] Warm cadence: 100 reach-outs a day; follow up "up to three times: once per day for three days, or until they respond"; physical mail weekly [Leads: Warm Outreach, Step 4]. Keep the list warm with regular value, then the nine-word email "Are you still looking to [4 word desire]?", no links; replies are "top priority" [Leads: Warm Outreach, Step 10].
5. [S] Cold cadence: "more times in more ways"; "act like you're actually trying to get ahold of these people"; different people answer different channels; email first because non-response gives a reason to call ("following up about my email"); expect 2-3 conversations before a higher-ticket sale; restart the list after three to six months, because they may not have seen it, the moment was bad, or circumstances changed, "everything may be right except the timing" [Leads: Cold Outreach, Problem #3].
6. [S] Dormant pipeline: "no for now ≠ no forever"; some objections are legitimate; pulse-check every 3-6 months without shame with memes or one-line emails; reps skip it because it is work and they fear rejection [T-Sales 02:25]. Ghosted anywhere: send a meme; the Kevin Hart "where you at?" meme has their highest response rate; 200 cold pipeline contacts → 5-10 re-engage [T-Sales 01:12].
7. [S] Nurture versus escalation, the bow tie. Lead nurture decreases: 3 calls the first day, 3 the next, a week later once a day, then once every two weeks. Escalated customers increase: email + SMS + call once today, twice tomorrow, three times the next day. Reach customers as fast as leads; "treat the customers as important as you do your leads" [T-CaseSaaS 28:12, 39:41]. With a 10-15% save commission, CS went from one attempt to 3-5 texts, emails and calls [T-CaseSaaS 27:37].
8. [S] Affluence warning: follow-up is "a double-edged sword"; low-ticket, high-unit customers "cannot be communicated with too much"; the more affluent the buyer or expensive the product, the less they want reach-out, they want it to work; unless it is a lot of money to them, then they want to talk all the time [T-CaseSaaS 34:48].
9. [S] Follow-up on takers of free things: call every opt-in, "how'd you like that? want me to do it at your place? risk-free" [T-LeadMagnets 07:20]; free maps ranking, then a call at day 7 [T-OffersLM 09:31]; giveaway non-winners called with a second-place prize [T-OffersLM 04:47]. Money Models: daily updates to giveaway entrants across every channel for 3-7 days (value, countdown, social proof); non-winners contacted privately, calls same or next day, a second seven-day claim deadline [MM: Giveaways]; trial non-users reached multiple times, meeting required, fee waived for attending [MM: Trial With Penalty]; payment-plan customers periodically re-offered the paid-in-full discount [MM: Payment Plan Downsells]; continuity customers reminded of the lifetime discount as the churn month approaches [MM: Continuity Discount Offers]; winback at 6+ months since last purchase, [E] 200 personalised videos offering $4,000 credit, ~20% took it, ~$1.9M annual revenue [MM: Rollover Upsell].
10. [S] BAMFAM: after every interaction the customer knows when the next contact is and with whom [T-Sales 01:49]; impressions form in the first 48 hours after purchase, "even 'no progress' updates beat silence" [Leads: Customer Referrals, way 4]; schedule the next appointment at the end of each [MM: Classic Upsell]; never "we'll coordinate", both pull up calendars; one point of contact at a time [T-CaseSaaS 13:36].
11. [S] Offer-driven pipeline touches: tell the pipeline before every price rise, "Never raise your prices without letting people know" [Offers p144]; sold-out lead magnets feed a notify list [Offers p137-138]; unmet demand returns with more urgency [Offers p127-128].
12. [S] Ask cadence in marketing: "ask as many times as you can", optimise ability to ask × number of asks with value between asks; CTAs within, before and after [T-LeadMagnets 26:45, 31:23]; injected CTAs took a podcast 20,000 → 400,000 downloads a month [T-Content 17:00]. Affiliates need follow-up too: link immediately, leaderboard "as frequently as possible", personal outreach to top movers; "you cannot expect affiliates to promote by magic" [T-Affiliates 11:31-15:24] (cluster C).
13. [E] The cost of none: at $1.5M/month they were not following up engaged leads at all; email nothing, calls nothing, text blasts → $1.8M the next month [Leads: Section V intro].

**Decision rules, formulas, thresholds**
- Under 60 s (+391%); over 5 min (−80%); 50% pick the first responder [T-Sales 00:03-00:05].
- Reminders 24 h / morning / 1 h; 5 min prep → ~+30% show; automation visibly automated [T-Sales 00:17].
- Warm: 3 touches, once a day for 3 days or until reply; mail weekly; nine-word re-probe [Leads: Warm Outreach].
- Cold: email first; 2-3 conversations for high ticket; restart the list after 3-6 months [Leads: Cold Outreach].
- Dormant: pulse every 3-6 months; meme to 200 → 5-10 [T-Sales 01:12, 02:25].
- Nurture 3 / 3 / 1 per day / 1 per two weeks; escalation 1 / 2 / 3 per day; 3-5 attempts [T-CaseSaaS 27:37-28:44].
- Giveaways: daily updates 3-7 days, 7-day claim, calls same or next day [MM: Giveaways]; winback at 6+ months [MM: Rollover Upsell].
- Next contact booked before this one ends [T-Sales 01:49].

**When / when not, and stage**
- The sub-minute rule is for inbound opt-ins with a live process; same-day slots need reps with an off-the-call SOP [T-Sales 00:03, 00:09].
- Three touches for permission-granted contacts; unlimited, multi-channel, restarting for strangers; "the fewer leads you have, the less automation" [Leads: Cold Outreach].
- Intensity by avatar: heavy for low-ticket, high-volume; light for affluent or expensive; heavy again if the sum is large to that buyer [T-CaseSaaS 34:48].
- Decreasing cadence for leads, increasing for escalated customers [T-CaseSaaS 28:12].
- Many 2-4-call closes → add pre-education, not more touches [T-Sales 02:20]; 78% consumed two long-form pieces before booking [Leads: Post Free Content II].

**Author's examples with numbers**
- [E] Leila calling salons: two said "appointment only", the third took her immediately and won the business [T-Sales 00:04].
- [E] ALAN, 4,000+ appointments a day: opening calendars was "the single strongest correlate to overall throughput" [T-Sales 00:06].
- [E] Cold-outreach ramp with tracked follow-up: 0 → 2 → 4 → 6 → 10 → 14 → 20 → 30 sales a month over about a year [Leads: Cold Outreach].
- [E] Text blasts → $1.8M month [Leads: Section V intro]; winback videos → ~$1.9M/yr [MM: Rollover Upsell]; meme 200 → 5-10 [T-Sales 01:12].

**Connections and tensions**
- "Personal, fast, multiple times over multiple days" [T-Sales 00:06] versus "affluent customers do not want reach-out" [T-CaseSaaS 34:48]: leads versus customers, conditioned on ticket relative to the buyer. [I] Open for high-ticket leads.
- Variants side by side: three touches (warm) [Leads: Warm Outreach]; "more times, more ways" (cold) [Leads: Cold Outreach]; 3/3/1/biweekly nurture [T-CaseSaaS 28:12]; 3-6-month pulses [T-Sales 02:25]. Restart after 3-6 months [Leads] and pulse every 3-6 months [T-Sales] are one rule in two sources.
- Automation should look automated [T-Sales 00:16] and cold outreach should look warm [Leads: Cold Outreach]: different steps (reminder versus first contact); Leads adds that automation trades response rate for volume.
- "Give until they ask" [Leads: Post Free Content II] versus "ask as many times as you can" [T-LeadMagnets 26:45] and "he who asks the most times gets the most sales" [T-Sales 01:30]: resolved as give in public, ask in private, value between asks.
- [I] The escalation cadence is the customer-side mirror of the sub-minute rule: "be as fast or faster reaching out to customers as to leads" [T-CaseSaaS 39:41].

**Not covered by the sources**
- No templates for the nurture sequence beyond the three reminders and the nine-word email; no touch count for inbound leads beyond "multiple days"; no tooling; no data on where in a sequence replies concentrate.

### Conversion mechanics

**Core statements**

1. [S] Work the constraint: the step where the most leads drop off; the smallest improvements there give the biggest boost. Funnel 30% opt-in, 5% apply (constraint), 50% schedule; +5 points each alone: 30 → 35% = 1.16x; 5 → 10% = 2x; 50 → 55% = 1.1x. One test per week per platform, launched Monday; next Monday pick winners, log every test, design the next to beat the control; if four tries (a month) cannot beat it, move to the next constraint; do "more" until it breaks, then "better", then "new" [Leads: More Better New].
2. [S] Headline and packaging move take rates by multiples: improving the headline, name or display "can 2x, 3x, or 10x your engagement"; test headline → image → subheadline; polls, "respond 1 or 2", or DMs when you lack a following; multiple formats lift take rates 2-4x [Leads: Offers and Lead Magnets, Steps 4-5]. Naming the same offer gives "2x, 3x, or 10x the response"; run two or three names, keep the winner as control; "I honestly have no idea why some names win" [Offers p178, p180]. Test packaging over contents; the name determines engagement "more than anything else"; ask the audience; ladder: small ad tests → filtered story polls → texts or A/B comments; [E] six titles, cover and sub-headlines split-tested for $100M Leads, "two years writing, two days testing" [T-LeadMagnets 05:14, 22:30-25:12]. First five seconds: "my advertising became 20x more effective" [Leads: Paid Ads I]. Conventions: number + outcome + time; how to X without Y even if Z; adjective + thing; X mistakes [T-LeadMagnets 25:35].
3. [S] CTA rules: what to do plus reasons to do it now; "call now", not "don't delay"; real scarcity ("if you sold 1000x the customers tomorrow, could you handle it?"), urgency, and any "because"; "Don't be clever, be clear"; give it often [Leads: Offers and Lead Magnets, Step 7]. "S-P-E-L-L it out": click this button, reply YES; easy numbers, short addresses ($370,000 for Acquisition.com) [Leads: Paid Ads I]. Formula: clear not clever + exact next action + reason to act now; scarcity/urgency optional; any reason beats none (the line-cutting study); CTAs within, before and after; small businesses use real capacity limits, big ones rolling cohorts [T-LeadMagnets 27:46-30:47]. Inject CTAs start, middle and end in two versions [T-Content 17:00]. "No one wants to request a quote" [T-OffersLM 08:15]; book-a-call "only works if you already have an informed audience" [T-LeadMagnets 00:53]. "Come buy my thing" to 100 → ~1; "I just want to help", then ask → 3-5x as many buyers on the same traffic [T-LeadMagnets 02:54].
4. [S] Path consistency: the landing page matches the ad in look and language and delivers what was promised; no "Frankenstein experience"; "a continuous experience from click to close"; then "now that you just did A, you need to do B" [Leads: Paid Ads I, Step 4]. "The sale starts at the click" [T-Sales 00:38]. Run emails, ads, sales and onboarding scripts through a reading-level grader; at sixth- to eighth-grade level "30% of people are automatically not going to understand"; lowering the page's reading level raised use [T-CaseSaaS 18:50].
5. [S] Show-rate levers: reward show-up rates, weekly kudos, optionally $500/week; +20% show equals +20% close in revenue and is far easier; "can raise sales more than sales training can" [T-Sales 00:15]. 15-minute start increments raised schedule and show rates at ALAN; a booking consumes three blocks; faked-busy calendars "don't do anything" [T-Sales 00:09]. Same-day and next-day only; weekend selling avoids the stacked Monday [T-Sales 00:00, 00:19]. Opening calendars beat 10-20% close-rate gains as a throughput correlate [T-Sales 00:06]. Discount offers show more than free: free brings more leads, discount fewer leads with a higher show share; "if you have low show-up rates, try a Discount Offer", especially where no-shows are costly (doctors, lawyers, dentists) [MM: Decoy Offer].
6. [S] "Put math to it": "if we improve conversion 30%, revenue is +30%; at 90% gross margin and 30% net margin that doubles profit. Are the things you're working on doubling profit? Over what period?" Seek-to-understand, not combative [T-Sales 02:01]. Same move: "3% per second of load; 9 to 3 seconds is +18%; versus what I charge, nothing" [T-LeadMagnets 11:06]; "if I made you $239,000 extra this year, would you pay me $42,000?" [Offers p60]; guarantee arithmetic, "don't be emotional, just do the math", 130 sales at 10% refunds beats 100 at 5% by 23% [Offers p157]; "$200 to save a $2,000 sale, I'd rather have $1,800 than zero" [T-CaseSaaS 26:28].
7. [S] Price presentation as lever. "It costs too much" means "too much up front"; a payment plan gets more buyers and keeps full price; present the price with "interest" built in and prepayment as the discount; schedule off paychecks; rerun declined cards the same day (about a third recovered); diagnostic: close rate rises but paid-in-fulls must not fall; [E] 10 leads, 3 → 6 sales [MM: Payment Plan Downsells]. Billing cadence vs monthly churn (Profitwell, 14,000 businesses): monthly 10.7%, quarterly 5%, annual 2%; "start high and work your way down" [MM: Payment Plan Downsells]. Four framings of one discount ($1,200 → $900): 25% off, $300 off, 3 months free, "one year for $900 ($1,200)" [MM: Decoy Offer]. Reframe rather than discount: 1 shirt for $30 plus 2 free; "more people know the value of free than the value of one shirt"; raise prices first; buy 6 get 12 beats buy 12 get 6 at the same $1,800 [MM: Buy X Get Y Free]. Premium at 5-10x first, main at about one fifth [MM: Anchor Upsell]. The Economist play: price the bundle equal to the pricier single option [MM: Menu Upsell]. Standalone at 1.33x the first month → 50% choose continuity; 2x → 70%; 2.66x → 90%; "people pay 33% more to avoid continuity" [MM: Continuity Bonus Offers]. Four-week billing, +8.3% revenue, +41% profit at 20% margins; "+3% processing fee" never lost a buyer [MM: Continuity Discount Offers]. "$5,000 in value for $1,800" turns 10% off into "a 64% difference in cost-to-value" [MM: Giveaways]. Offers: anchor the price to the core, let bonuses widen the gap "until it's too big to bear"; [E] $4,351 of stack "for only $599" [Offers p115-116, p148-150]. Discounts of 10-20% have "no point"; 80-90% changes behaviour, on a cheap component that starts the upsell [T-OffersLM 12:14].
8. [S] Never change price; change terms or components. "We don't negotiate with terrorists": customers talk, price moves once and every price looks negotiable; tests only premeditated, never mid-sale; to "can you do it for less?" say "we could do it for more", a counter-anchor with a "perfect batting average"; a plan changes what they pay today, a feature downsell changes the components [T-Sales 02:30]. "You can offer something different for less; you just can't offer the same thing for less" (the insurance salesman who dropped $5,000 to $400 lost trust and the sale); "don't drop your price just to get somebody to buy... tough cookies"; test prices with a set number of people, planned [MM: Rules of Downselling]; "business terrorists"; pay less now → plan; pay less overall → feature downsell; never "just because" [MM: Feature Downsells]. "Never discount the main offer. It teaches your customers that your prices are negotiable"; add bonuses [Offers p148]; raise price only after value [Offers p62]. Allowed trades: a discount for three-way introductions, "you changed the terms of the sale" [Leads: Customer Referrals, way 4]; $100 off for reviews, a video testimonial, three posts, two introductions [MM: Feature Downsells]. "That's expensive" is a reaction, not an objection [T-Sales 02:29].
9. [S] Offer-side multipliers as claimed: Grand Slam Offer 2.5x response × 2.5x close × 4x price on the same eyeballs [Offers p43]; guarantee quality 2-4x [Offers p156]; one unsolved obstacle loses sales [Offers p111-112]; up to 50-60% of a launch's sales in the last four hours [Offers p142]; local in-person converts "a much higher share" [Offers p180]; guarantee removed as a feature downsell, 25% → 75% [MM: Feature Downsells]; decoy ~8 of 10 on $399 [MM: Decoy Offer]; continuity bonus 34 → 17 retained became 15 + 40 + 8 per 100 [MM: Continuity Bonus Offers]; one-on-one onboarding restating the goal, ascension 20% → 48% [T-CaseSaaS 07:27].
10. [S] Baselines: warm ~20% reply, ~20% of those take the free offer, ~1 in 4 pay → ~1 customer per 100 [Leads: Warm Outreach]; cold 3% engaged, about a third convert → ~1 per 100; 33% of shows close at $120 per show → $360 per client [Leads: Cold Outreach]; 1 in 3 calls close on a core-offer ad versus 1 in 10 magnet takers at a third of the CAC [Leads: Offers and Lead Magnets]; gym opening 50% show × 50% close = 25% of leads, two-thirds later to membership [MM: Start Here]; freemium CAC = cost of the free thing ÷ free-to-paid conversion [T-Moats #12].

### 1. Onboarding

**Core statements**

1. [S] Onboarding is "what a customer does immediately after they buy." If a business has none, adding it makes customers happier, keeps them longer and makes them pay more; "if you're not doing it, start there" [T-CaseSaaS 00:00:38]. For a service or a software (treated as "very similar") onboarding is the first real touch point [T-CaseSaaS 00:03:10].
2. [S] The first-impression window. Variants side by side: customers form their first impression in the 24–48 hours post-purchase, and that frames the rest of the relationship [T-CaseSaaS 00:03:22]; customers form their lasting impression "within the first 48 hours after they buy," so force as many wins as possible in that window: "Set many expectations. Meet many expectations. Repeat" [Leads: Customer Referrals, faster wins]; the client should have "a big emotional win early (as close as possible to their purchase)" [Offers p82]. Claimed payoff: nailing the 24–48 hours raises upsell take-up and tenure, and onboarding "can add two, three months to your LTV if you have any kind of recurring service" [T-CaseSaaS 00:03:40]. Sending only an email with lists is "missing a massive revenue opportunity" [T-CaseSaaS 00:03:45].
3. [E] The SaaS case's five failure modes: (1) one-to-many, 25 new customers per onboarding call; (2) company-centred content (mission, founders, story) that "customers don't care about"; (3) about a third of each call spent resetting passwords for a 55+, low-tech avatar; (4) a hard upsell pitch at the end of the group call; (5) no next steps for anyone who declined the upsell, "good luck" [T-CaseSaaS 00:00:38–00:03:10]. Context the author adds: a new product launched to a large base floods infrastructure (one rep, 6–8 calls/day × 25), and hiring 100 reps for three days makes no sense, so some of the problems were scale-driven [T-CaseSaaS 00:02:30].
4. [S] Fix 1: one-to-one. [E] 15% of new customers were routed through a new process to test it before scaling headcount [T-CaseSaaS 00:03:57]. Fix 2: apply the CLOSER framework's last letter, R = reinforce the decision, to onboarding: restate Clarify (why they came), Label (the problem), Overview (past pain), then re-sell "the vacation" [T-CaseSaaS 00:04:41]. [E] At ALAN, changing only the language ("you said you wanted $10,000 a month extra, why?") and tying the next three steps to that reason "doubled or tripled" follow-through [T-CaseSaaS 00:05:39]. Fix 3: a quick win, concierge log-in set-up and product tour. Fix 4: route any issue live to support so every customer leaves the call logged in and understanding the basics for the goal they signed up for [T-CaseSaaS 00:06:00–00:06:45].
5. [S] The sales lecture states the same R from the sales side: post-purchase is a "handshake" with customer success, not a "handoff"; information travels like a baton through the lifecycle; BAMFAM = book a meeting from a meeting: after every interaction the customer knows exactly when the next meeting is and with whom, and that person knows the whole conversation (goals, fears, pains) and speaks in those terms [T-Sales 01:48:45]. Script: "John, this is Sarah, she'll help you with XYZ so you can [goal]... she's cured cancer six times," after which Sarah opens with proof-promise-plan personalised to the prospect [T-Sales 01:49:30]. Setter and closer notes go in the CRM; CS opens with "you talked to Steve and Jim, you decided on X, we'll get you [goal] by step one, two, three"; making the customer repeat themselves is "strongly negative" [T-Sales 00:46:49]. [E] A portfolio company switched handoff to handshake: fewer drop-offs, back-outs, cold feet and first-30-day refunds [T-Sales 01:50:30].
6. [S] Fix for "no next steps," the end-of-call trio: (1) a call summary, announced at the start so they stop taking notes and stay present; (2) links to the three tutorials matched to what they said in Clarify/Label; (3) the point of contact introduced live on the call [T-CaseSaaS 00:12:13–00:13:20]. Baton-pass chain: sales rep → onboarding person (with a set time) → account rep; three people touch the account but the customer never has multiple points of contact at once; never end with "we'll coordinate," both pull up calendars [T-CaseSaaS 00:13:36]. BAMFAM restated in Money Models: end every appointment by scheduling the next; "a customer should know the next time they see you, and why, before they leave" (attributed to Sharran) [MM: Classic Upsell]. In Leads it is the fourth "faster wins" practice: they should always know the next time they will hear from you [Leads: Customer Referrals, faster wins].
7. [S] Expectations management. Three complaints ("too techy," "not enough support," "not getting the result soon enough") map to effort and sacrifice, perceived likelihood, and time delay, "the nightmare version" of the value equation [T-CaseSaaS 00:14:33–00:16:00]. Two ways to close a gap: add to the back end (costs money, "and that's okay") or set better expectations up front [T-CaseSaaS 00:16:00]. [E] Re-scripting the sales calls to stop making the product sound easier and faster than it was: sales volume and close rate "didn't change at all" [T-CaseSaaS 00:16:32]. [S] Lowering expectations can raise perceived likelihood because the promise becomes believable [T-CaseSaaS 00:16:50]. Same rule in Leads: "Slowly lower the promises you make when making offers. Keep lowering them until your close rates lower. At that point, stop"; "I add fifty percent to my timelines so I always deliver early"; "deliver early, but never late" [Leads: Customer Referrals, set better expectations; faster wins]. Related: be specific on the negatives and vaguer on the positives in the lead magnet, which yields more realistic expectations [T-LeadMagnets 12:22]; write affiliate collateral yourself so every customer arrives with the same expectations you can deliver on [T-Affiliates 14:13].
8. [S] Sales + CS game-tape review: record every customer call across the lifecycle; put sales and customer success on the same weekly call reviewing a sales call and an onboarding call. CS tells sales which phrasings create bad expectations; sales sees what happens after the card runs (conviction); CS discovers cheap extras "everyone asks for." Reduces the 5–10% of closed sales that back out, "increase sales by decreasing a negative" [T-Sales 00:28:54–00:32:16].
9. [S] Support reachability: support existed but was hard to reach; an in-product chat bubble fixed a perceived problem with no new solution. Two support types: a dedicated account rep (known from onboarding) plus general support for daily issues. Advanced: the bubble logs the page where help was requested, which drives roadmap priority (illustrative: "if 82% of complaints come from this one point," fix that page first) [T-CaseSaaS 00:17:08–00:18:50].
10. [S] Reading level: lower the grade level of pages, emails, ads, sales and onboarding scripts through a reading-level grader; as heard, "if you're talking to people at a sixth-grade or eighth-grade level then 30% of people are automatically not going to understand" (ambiguous whether threshold or target; the direction is: go lower) [T-CaseSaaS 00:18:50–00:19:55].
11. [S] Best-customer actions in the first days. [E] Gym Launch tracked activities and found gym owners who ran paid ads and made a sale in the first seven days had 3× LTGP, so they forced everyone to do both in the first seven days; average results "skyrocketed." Six-step process: survey best results → interview for what they did differently → find common actions → force new customers to repeat them → measure → match the guarantee's conditions to those actions [Leads: Customer Referrals, get more people better results]. The Offers book gives the same fast-win target: ads live and the first $2,000 sale within seven days [Offers p82]. Guarantees that institutionalise it: delayed second payment until the first outcome, which "focuses the team on activation (the metric/action that predicts long-term retention)"; first-outcome guarantee ("first sale in 14 days or we pay your ad spend") [Offers p166]. Win Your Money Back criteria should "make everyone do what the best customers do" [MM: Win Your Money Back].
12. [S] Money Models' onboarding devices. "$1 first month" beats a free month: a super-low price justifies asking for a card and proves the card works [MM: Trial With Penalty]. Trial With Penalty makes onboarding the trial's terms: the customer pays only if they skip the tasks (attend all consulting calls, post progress, journal daily, attend feedback sessions); [E] HR software: "$500 onboarding, then $99 per month," the $500 waived if you attend three 60-minute Zoom calls, do the homework and set employees up [MM: Trial With Penalty]. Check-ins are required and each is tied to a benefit; "we charge if you miss these because it's the only way we can get you results" [MM: Trial With Penalty]. Onboarding you must do anyway can be a continuity bonus: "slap a price on it" [MM: Continuity Bonus Offers]; a setup fee is presented as "it costs us money to get you started" [MM: Waived Fee Offer]. After every DFY option is refused: "How about you just come to a free orientation on X tomorrow?" and offer a DIY product at the end; [E] about half of fitness refusers showed and "almost all of them bought supplements" [MM: Feature Downsells]. Same mechanism in the moats video: free gym use with a mandatory onboarding consultation where personal training is sold [T-Moats #12].
13. [S] Onboarding as offer enhancer in 2021: cohort starts produce "a choreographed onboarding experience," which matters more as you scale [Offers p141]; speedy personalised onboarding can be a bonus for signing today [Offers p143]; free onboarding is a swappable urgency enhancer [Offers p144]. Ease vector in 2025: prepare intake and onboarding ahead of time so the experience is seamless; so few businesses do it that customers "almost always appreciate and recognise it" [T-OffersLM 03:10].
14. [S] Every customer conversation has a perfect way it should go: if you script sales, lead nurture and onboarding, script CS too; 90% of issues are the same few ("can't log in," "don't know step one") [T-CaseSaaS 00:24:02].
15. [I] The affiliate programme uses the same onboarding logic on lead getters: opt in → collect data → immediate custom link (fast reward, because without believing attribution they will not send traffic) → real-time leaderboard → plug-and-play collateral [T-Affiliates 11:58–13:29].

**Decision rules, formulas, thresholds**

### 2. Retention

**Core statements**

1. [S] In 2025 retention is continuity: offers that "provide ongoing value that customers make ongoing payments for, until they cancel"; "you sell once, but get paid again and again" [MM: Section V intro]. The sources' mechanics are offer mechanics (billing cadence, discounts, fees, credits) plus the SaaS case's customer-success operation.
2. [E] Billing cadence sets churn. Profitwell, 14,000 businesses: monthly billing 10.7% monthly cancellation; quarterly 5%; annual 2%. [S] "So start high (fewer bigger payments) and work your way down" [MM: Payment Plan Downsells].
3. [S] Continuity Bonus Offer: "an awesome thing if they sign up today," typically worth more than the first payment; "anything you offer for free you can also offer as a discount" [MM: Continuity Bonus Offers]. Tested table ($199/month baseline): standalone at 1.33× → 50% choose continuity; 1.66× → 60%; 2× → 70%; 2.33× → 80%; 2.66× → 90%; "the exact numbers matter less than the principle"; "people pay 33% more to avoid continuity... half will buy it" [same]. Bulk prepay ("buy five months get one free"): "only one out of every eight people has to take the upsell to raise 30-day profits by 50%" [same]. Trade bonuses for 3-6-12+ month commitments; bonuses members-only; bonuses from what you already do ("onboarding... slap a price on it") [same]. [E] Origin: 34 of 100 took the challenge, 17 stayed; after, ~15 take it, 40 go straight into continuity, ~8 prepay six months [same]. [S] "No successful continuity business I've seen has a standalone membership offer"; "nobody wants to make a recurring commitment to something they haven't tried" [same].
4. [S] Continuity Discount Offer ("Buy X Get Y Free done continuity-style"). Four ways to apply the discount: (1) up front, best where contracts are historically enforced (cell phones, storage, collateral), skip with high churn, "it gets customers, but delays cash"; (2) at the end, if every payment is on time, "they earn their free time"; (3) spread, $600 of free months on a $200/month year → $50/month off for 12 months, optionally for life if never late; (4) after the first one or two payments (activation fee, "first and last month") [MM: Continuity Discount Offers]. Billing: four-week cycles, 13 versus 12 = +8.3% revenue for the same buyers, "literally made me millions"; extend the term (pay 12 get 3 = 15 months), never eat into it; "plus a 3% processing fee," "never lost a buyer to it"; two forms of payment (waive the fee for the second, prefer ACH) because recurring businesses lose "mountains of cash" to failed cards [same]. Lifetime discount at the most common churn point: a lower rate for life for staying past month X, X = the average drop-off month; [E] rice company: 5% off subscription, 15% off after five straight months, "just beyond where most people canceled" [same].
5. [S] Cancellation policy, decided ahead of time. Favourite: cancellation fee = the discount received by committing. Make sure customers know how to cancel: no internal channel means external complaints and 1-star reviews; a clear path gives "a real chance to save it." Exit interview: let them vent, "get more angry about the problem than them," fix what is fixable, rollover-upsell if they wanted more; "I'll waive your cancellation fee if you come in and tell me what I could do better"; [E] "I routinely save a third of customers that agree to exit interviews." Trade-offs: frontloaded discounts convert more and churn more, backloaded the reverse; "light cancellation terms get more people to sign up but more people leave. Harsher terms get fewer sign-ups but fewer leave" [MM: Continuity Discount Offers].
6. [S] Waived Fee Offer: a setup fee for month-to-month, "typically 3–5× the monthly rate," waived for a longer commitment; quit early and the fee is due; "customers will stay longer if leaving costs more than staying" [MM: Waived Fee Offer]. Script: "It costs us money to get you started. If you only wanna test us out, you cover those costs. If you commit longer, I'll cover them." Commitment "at minimum" one year; best for slow-to-work services (SEO, investing, weight loss); more cash wanted → 1.5–3× fee; drop the fee once the term is met. "If more than 5% want to cancel early, look into it," "nudge them, not handcuff people"; pricing "can't (and shouldn't) overcome a terrible product" [same]. [E] $1,000/month, $5,000 fee, 12 months [same].
7. [S] Sequence: as an attraction offer continuity "leave[s] you strapped for cash today," so it comes last; on acceptance upsell a bulk block at a discount and auto-continue after it [MM: Section V intro; Continuity Offers Conclusion]. Other devices: WYMB winnings spread over 12 months because "people fall off if they don't pay something" [MM: Win Your Money Back]; rollover to rescue upset customers before refunding [MM: Rollover Upsell]; feature-downsell current customers who do not use what they pay for, and downsold customers "have the second highest value of all my customers" [MM: Feature Downsells].
8. [S] Ground rules: "if a customer asks for their money back, give it back... Next time, spend the time and resources to get better customers" [MM: Four Types, Important Notes]. Do not bill trial non-starters, "a small fee isn't worth a 1-star review": require a meeting, waive the fee for attendance; one free goof [MM: Trial With Penalty].
9. [E] SaaS case, problem 3: everyone paid to sell, nobody to keep customers; reps fielding support, which destroys conviction ("one of the strongest predictors of sales close rate"); CS "didn't try," unscripted [T-CaseSaaS 00:19:55–00:24:05]. Fixes: a dedicated escalations team, triage "as in an emergency room"; 10–15% commission, "real money," for saved customers; the call scripted and practised [T-CaseSaaS 00:24:05–00:27:37]. With the commission CS reached out three to five times [T-CaseSaaS 00:27:37]. [S] Escalation outreach is the inverse of lead nurture (the bow tie): lead cadence decreases (3/day, 3 next day, then daily, then fortnightly); escalated-customer cadence increases (email + SMS + call once today, twice tomorrow, three times the next day) [T-CaseSaaS 00:28:12–00:28:44]. Results: −61% cancellations; 25% of escalation calls ascended [T-CaseSaaS 00:29:27–00:30:02].
10. [S] Why pay to save: a lost customer costs the refund plus "three (or five) customers you would have had"; "no one believes they're bad enough to get anti-referrals" [T-CaseSaaS 00:25:04–00:26:00]. "If I have to pay $200 to save a $2,000 sale, I'd rather have $1,800 than zero... just do the math" [T-CaseSaaS 00:26:28]. Takeaways: "If you don't track, you don't care": a low refund rate is not CSAT or NPS. "You're never too big for good CS." Follow-up by avatar: low-ticket customers "cannot be communicated with too much"; the affluent buying expensive products want less, "they just want it to work" [T-CaseSaaS 00:34:48]. Proactive CS: [E] another portfolio company's second-highest LTV avatar was customers it had proactively downgraded ("you're only using three of our ten things; we have a service level at half the price"), who "never cancel" and refer [T-CaseSaaS 00:39:41].
11. [S] Revenue retention as the engine. [E] Business 1 sells 100, 200, 300 a year and loses everyone; Business 2 sells 100 a year and keeps them: same size at year three, "but you want Business 2," because without a compounding vehicle growth caps ("the marketing team of a $300M business" doing $30M) [T-CaseSaaS 00:35:58–00:36:48]. ### 3. Ascension and monetization

**Core statements**

1. [S] "A Money Model is a sequence of offers"; "if you offer the right thing when customers realize they need it, you can make as many offers as you like" [MM: Section I]. Four types: Attraction turns strangers into customers, Upsell gets people to spend more cash, Downsell gets yeses where there would have been nos, Continuity keeps people buying; "making more offers works better than making one" [MM: Four Types of Offers]. Three stages: Get Cash (attraction), Get More Cash (upsell and downsell), Get The Most Cash (continuity); evolution: customers reliably → they pay for themselves → they pay for other customers → maximise long-term value → maximise ad spend [MM: Section VI]. The 2021 growth formula was split into three levers "to understand the money models that will come in Volume III" [Offers p37].
2. [S] Upsell = "whatever we offer next"; "you upsell the solution to the problem your offer reveals"; "often, upsells make the majority of the profit" [MM: Section III intro]. [E] Burger: $0.25 profit; fries +$0.75; drink +$1.75 → $2.00 (8×); supersize → $3.00 (11.6× as printed) [same]. [S] Upsells fail on the wrong thing, the wrong time ("before they've experienced the problem") or the wrong way [same].
3. [S] Classic Upsell, "You can't have X without Y": solve the next problem the moment the customer becomes aware of it; timed right, "customers upsell themselves" [MM: Classic Upsell]. Tactics: most profitable upsell first; "say no to say yes" ("you don't want anything besides what I just offered, do you?"); free bonuses that create the problem the upsell solves (free earmuffs → $30 storage); BAMFAM; "upsell as many times as makes sense... The worst thing is if they would've said yes but you never asked" [same]. Small/medium/large: $5/$8/$9 pushes Large, $6/$7/$9 pushes Medium; if most buy large, raise all prices [same]. Guarantees, warranties and insurance as paid upsells: "add 5–50% onto the price"; [E] an art studio asked +10% for what it used to replace free, 30% now pay [same]. [E] One-product businesses "barely have a business, you have a front end"; those that added an upsell "actually 5×'d their business" [same].
4. [S] Menu Upsell, four tactics in order: Unselling (cross out what they do not need), Prescription (tell them what they need and how to use it "as if they already have it"), A/B ("chocolate or vanilla?"), Card On File ("use the card on file?") [MM: Menu Upsell]. Nudge ("this is my favorite"); sold out → take payment, delay delivery. Economist play: A digital $59, B print $125, C both $125 → customers take C; price the bundle equal to the dearer option "so long as you price the options to preserve your margins." "Works best when you have multiple offers available" [same]. [E] 19 consults with zero supplement sales, then "chocolate or vanilla?" and 20 in a row bought [same].
5. [S] Anchor Upsell: present a premium at 5–10× first; get "the gasp"; "come to the rescue" by asking whether they care about what makes it premium; present the main offer, same primary features at "a fifth of the price" [MM: Anchor Upsell]. "If you treat the anchor like a fake, so will the customer"; make a premium you want people to buy ([E] a friend's fake anchor, fixed, tripled profits); "the bigger the gasp, the more they bought" [same]. [E] $500 suit budget → $16,000 anchor → $2,200 suit, 5× the budget [same].
6. [S] Rollover Upsell: credit previous purchases toward the next offer; who (lapsed, upset instead of refunded, competitors' upset customers, regulars), what (more, better, new), how (all or part, up front or spread); next offer at least 4× the credit so a full credit discounts at most 25%; one-time urgency; rollover before refunding [MM: Rollover Upsell]. [E] Origin: $600 WYMB winners churned before their first out-of-pocket payment until a friend rolled winnings into a year at $50/month off, "the missing link in my Money Model." Winback: videos to 200 past customers offering $4,000 credit, ~20% took it, "~$1,900,000 of annual revenue" [same]. Gift-card play: $200 card for $20, limit two, only for other people; card value = 20% of the next offer; "people pay you to refer their friends" [same].
7. [S] Two universal upsells: "more of the thing they just bought, or more help with the thing they just bought"; uptake is highest immediately after a first purchase; presenting the offer is "an ethical duty," made known rather than hard-closed [T-CaseSaaS 00:08:47]. Buying is not periodic: a person with a problem buys everything in a short window; [E] the shop that "only sells shoes" loses "Susie" to "above-average Andrew" who sells everything in her "hyper buying frenzy"; "average Adam" offering the case 30 days later is too late [T-CaseSaaS 00:09:30–00:11:00]. Purchases carry an identity shift ("I'm a runner / author / investor"); stock what the identity needs, "back it up" [T-CaseSaaS 00:11:00–00:11:55]; Money Models' hyper-buying cycle says the same [MM: Classic Upsell].
8. [E] SaaS ascension: paid-tier take-up 20% → 48% (2.4× company-wide) after one-to-one onboarding that restated goals; 25% of escalation calls ascended; a higher software tier costs "pennies" so it drops to the bottom line [T-CaseSaaS 00:07:27, 00:29:27–00:30:40]. [S] "How dialled and tight the business is before you ask for money" raises perceived likelihood; "the best way to get someone to expect value in the future is to provide value today" [T-CaseSaaS 00:07:45–00:08:30].
9. [S] Downsells: "any offer you make after someone says 'no'"; levers: how they pay, what they get [MM: Section IV intro]. Rules: (1) "no means no for this thing, not no for everything"; (2) "if you're gonna give something, get something"; (3) personalise, don't pressure; (4) "a hundred ways to offer the stuff you already have"; (5) don't drop price to get a buy, a payment plan is allowed; (6) customers talk about price, test prices deliberately, never in-the-moment [MM: Rules of Downselling]. [E] Insurance dropped from $5,000 to $400 for the same policy: trust gone, no sale; "you can offer something different for less. You just can't offer the same thing for less" [same]. Sales lecture: never change price to close; counter a discount request with "we could do it for more"; change terms or components [T-Sales 02:30:05–02:31:53].
10. [S] Payment Plan Downsell, seven steps, stop when they buy: (1) reward paying in full rather than punish paying over time ("it's $15... but $10 if you prepay"); (2) financing, credit card, layaway; (3) half now, half on payday, "what's the most you can put down today?"; (4) "on a scale from 1–10 how bad do you wanna do this?": 8+ → keep going, ≤ 7 → "why not a 10?" then feature downsells; (5) three payments; (6) spread evenly ([E] Gym Launch, 16 weekly charges); (7) free trial = Trial With Penalty; "up to nine offers" [MM: Payment Plan Downsells]. "'It costs too much' really means 'this costs too much up front'"; charge on paydays; re-run declined cards the same day, "recoup a third"; periodically offer the prepay discount on the balance [same].
11. [S] Trial With Penalty as a downsell, offered last, "only changes what they pay today, not how much they pay in total"; [E] 3 of 10 close, 4 more take the trial, 3 of those are upsold → 6 of 10 [MM: Trial With Penalty]. After the trial: like it → longer term or higher tier; hate it → "only one person can be angry, and it needs to be you," position them for the higher-level thing, "about half of these people" buy; unused → require a meeting, waive the fee for attendance [same].
12. [S] Feature Downsells: change what they get; remove features from highest to lowest value so "people will see the value in the thing you removed after they see the difference in price" and "re-upsell themselves" [MM: Feature Downsells]. [E] Removing the money-back guarantee tripled a high-ticket close rate 25% → 75%: 35 full-price + 40 downsell per 100 calls [same]. Name the cheapest "The Minimum" ("so nothing more than the minimum package then?"); temperature check after two downsells (8+ → payment plan, ≤ 7 → "what would a 10 look like?"); alternate feature and payment downsells, "you become very difficult to refuse"; close with "Deal?"; DFY → DIY; free orientation → DIY product [same]. "Everybody buys something"; "a hundred different offers for the same product... the offer is never the same stuff for cheaper" [MM: Downsell Offers Conclusion].
13. [S] Monetising free value: "Give away the secrets, sell the implementation"; "you can give the whole farm away, people still won't do it. 1% of people will do it. My business model relies on the 1% who do it"; his only ask at the time: "you get to three million, then we can partner"; "the longer you can delay the ask, the bigger the ask can be" [T-Content 43:00–49:00]. Freemium: "find what everyone wants, make it better, give it away for free, and monetize another way"; the effort goes into ascending free → paid; [E] free gym use with a mandatory onboarding consultation selling personal training, fine if 1 in 5 buy, provided PT profit covers facility cost [T-Moats #12]. Lead-magnet ladders: sell at the point of greatest deprivation, the "dessert" dearer than the free "entrée" [T-LeadMagnets 08:59–09:56]; a trial then removed creates the deprivation that converts ([E] four months of free agency services, then "now you can pay for them") [T-LeadMagnets 14:39]; the splinter offer, one component at 80–90% off, after which "they then want to buy A, C, D" [T-OffersLM 11:17–12:14].
14. [S] Client-financed acquisition as an ascension rule: when first-purchase profit is below CAC, "I immediately sell them more stuff"; [E] a $100 upsell at 100% margin taken by 1 in 5 closes the 30-day gap [Leads: Paid Ads II]. Referrals way 6: "tell them what to buy next. If you don't, they'll still buy, but from someone else"; [E] customers referred friends to the dearer product bought later [Leads: Customer Referrals]. WYMB: "the real money comes from people who succeed with it and you have something else to offer them" [MM: Win Your Money Back].
15. [E] Gym Launch's money model as built ("it only looks clean looking back"): Stage I decoy, free DIY courses with a free call versus "$16,000 over 16 weeks" → $476,000/month within three months. Stage II: "Gym Lords" classic upsell at $42,000/year with a $6,000 prepay discount ("many paid up front with money I had just made them"); payment-plan downsells ($10,000 down, then ~$800/week × 52); for the final no, "start for free," a continuity discount front-loading free time until the first offer was paid off → ~$1,500,000/month. Stage III: a menu of service levels (full $800/week; DFY advertising $300; sales training $200; monthly releases $500; original materials + support $100 = "Minimum package") with feature downsells, "almost everyone stayed for something" → $2,300,000/month within 14 months; Prestige Labs added → $4,400,000/month by month 20 [MM: Section VI]. The 2021 telling: $16,000, then 35% upsold into three years at $42,000/year [Offers p68]. [I] The features sum to $1,100/week against the $800 bundle, the Economist pattern.
16. [S] "It's less about having 100 products to offer, and more about having 100 ways to offer your product" ([E] personal training at 1, 2, 3 or 4 sessions/week) [MM: Section VI; Downsell Offers Conclusion]. Affiliate products fill money-model gaps at any stage [MM: Section VI]; partner commissions add $350 "pure profit" to a $400 offer [Offers p152]; the credit-based guarantee is "best in an upsell" [Offers p164].

**Decision rules, formulas, thresholds**

- Attraction → Upsell → Downsell → Continuity; one offer at a time; measure in quarters [MM: Four Types; Section VI]. Upsell = more / better / different; failure = wrong thing / time / way [MM: Section III intro].

Rollover: next offer ≥ 4× credit; gift card = 20% of next offer [MM: Rollover Upsell].

### 4. Unit economics

**Core statements**

1. [S] Growth arithmetic: three ways to grow (more customers, higher purchase value, more purchases); "New clients/month × lifetime value = max monthly revenue" ([E] 10 × $1,000 = $10,000/month cap) [Offers p37]. Leads: "when you double your leads, you double your business" [Leads: The Problem This Book Solves]. Money Models: twice as valuable × twice as many × twice as fast = "8× faster"; triple each = 27× [MM: Section I].
2. [S] Definitions. Gross profit = revenue minus the direct cost of servicing an additional customer ([E] $10 lotion, $2 cost = 80%; $1,000/month agency, $100 labour = 90%) [Offers p38]. LTV = gross profit × purchases ([E] $1,000 × 90% × 5 months = $4,500), indirect costs excluded; also called LTGP [Offers p38]. Leads: LTGP = "all the money a customer ever spends on your stuff minus all the money it takes to deliver it" ([E] $15 price, $5 delivery, ten purchases → $100); gross profit "is the actual money you use to acquire customers, pay rent, cover payroll" [Leads: Paid Ads II].
3. [S] The 3:1 pattern: "Every business I invest in that struggles to scale has at least one thing in common: their LTGP to CAC ratio was less than 3 to 1. As soon as I get it above 3 to 1... they take off. This is a pattern I personally observed, not a rule" [Leads: Paid Ads II]. Restated: cold-outreach hand-off at "at least three times the lifetime profit of a customer compared to what it costs you to get them," portfolio example 30:1 [Leads: Cold Outreach]; acquisition cost "at least one-third of the profit you make over the lifetime" ([I] read: no more than) [Leads: Employees]; affiliates "at least 3:1," aim 5:1, 10:1+ [Leads: Affiliates and Partners]; referral variant: at 4:1 a customer costs 25% of one LTGP; if each brings two more, 12:1, "three customers for the price of one" [Leads: Customer Referrals]. Levers: lower CAC or raise LTGP; "costs can only approach zero but how much you make can go up to infinity" [Leads: Paid Ads II].
4. [S] Diagnostic: CAC below 3× the industry average → fix the business model (LTGP); above 3× → fix advertising; competitors' CACs "are much closer than you'd think. The difference between the winners and the losers is how much they make off each customer" [Leads: Paid Ads II; Employees]. Leads with the problem and the money who do not buy → "a sales problem" [Leads: Paid Ads II].
5. [S] Cost per engaged lead = payroll ÷ engaged leads; [E] $100,000 / 1,000 = $100; 1 in 10 buy → $1,000 CAC; $4,000 LTGP → 4:1; Acquisition.com ~30,000 engaged leads/month on ~$100,000 of content payroll → ~$3.33 per lead [Leads: Employees]. Keynote content cost: $20,000/month per person, ×2 = $40,000/month; ~7% of his time; "you could probably do this on your own"; exposure at a $25 CPM = "$2 million a month," "we're getting paid to do it" [T-Content 11:00–12:00].
6. [S] Cold-outreach cost model: $15/hour + $50 per show; leads $0.10; 200 dials/day → ~2 shows; $240/day → $120 per show; 33% close → $360 per client; $3,600 profit → 10:1; "then, you just add bodies"; "for every X people contacted, you get Y customers. Then, you simply solve for X" [Leads: Cold Outreach].
7. [S] Lead-magnet CAC math: $10,000 profit per customer; $1,000 of ads per call; 1 in 3 close → $3,000 CAC. A $25 lead magnet: $75 of ads per call → $100 all-in; 1 in 10 buy → $1,000 CAC, "cut our cost to get a customer by 3×," 10:1, "triple our business"; "even if your lead magnet costs money to deliver, it should still lower your cost to get a new customer" [Leads: Offers and Lead Magnets]. Service magnet: $25 hard cost, $250+ value, 1 in 4 buy → $100 CAC [T-LeadMagnets 19:17–20:04]. Freemium: CAC = cost to deliver the free thing ÷ conversion ([E] 1 cent per free user, 1 in 100 → $1) [T-Moats #12]. Splinter offers put the 80–90% discount on what costs little [T-OffersLM 12:14].
8. [S] Max allowable CAC and affiliate payouts: [E] $200 price, $40 cost → $160; at 3:1, $120 to the business and $40 to the affiliate = max allowable CAC. Tiers: 25% ($10) on sign-up, 50% ($20) on activation, 100% ($40) on sustained performance; at 20/20/60 of sales the blended payout is $30, moving 3:1 to 4:1; "cutting marketing costs by 33% can translate into 10% to 20% more net profit" [Leads: Affiliates and Partners]. Affiliate return: $4,000 per affiliate, $120,000 sales, 75% margin, 40% paid out → 12.5:1 [same].
9. [S] The 30-day rule, both books. Leads: "if your customer spends more than it costs you to get and fulfill them, in the first 30 days, then you have the funds to scale now and forever," because a credit card is interest-free for thirty days; [E] $15/month, $5 delivery, 10 months → $100 LTGP; CAC $30 → 3.3:1 but two months to break even; a $100 upsell at 100% margin taken by 1 in 5 → $30 in 30 days = CAC → "free customers" [Leads: Paid Ads II]. Money Models: minimum, "more profit from a customer than it costs to get and service them in the first 30 days. That's the bare minimum"; $100M standard, "more profit from one customer than it costs to get and service many customers," i.e. "at least two more customers in less than 30 days" [MM: Ten Years In Ten Minutes; Section VI]; the attraction offer "ideally" covers "the cost of the customer and the cost to deliver our thing multiple times over" [MM: Attraction Offers Conclusion]. The failure loop: ads cost more than the sale profits → cut ads → float on personal cash → "lose it all"; "what if it takes you two years to make your cash back?" [MM: Section I]. Bootstrapped businesses "must get customers at a profit" [MM: Section VI].
10. [S] Pricing arithmetic. "Raise price in stages": new offers cheap, then "keep raising the price until you cannot make up for the nos with the extra cash you make from the yeses... until you make less money" [MM: Section VI]. Offers: "charge as much money... as humanly possible," "up to a hundred times more" than fulfilment cost [Offers p73–74]; raise price only after raising value [Offers p62]; "99 percent of businesses need to raise their prices to grow" [Offers p69]. [E] Gym Launch entered at 3× the highest competitor ($5,000) and 32× the lowest: $16,000 for 16 weeks, against an average owner take-home of $35,280/year [Offers p68]. Giveaway discount = "10%–30% of your gross margins" [MM: Giveaways].
11. [S] Continuity arithmetic. [E] $1,000 to 100 people, 10 buy → $10,000 now, $0 later; at $50/month, 40 buy and stay 20 months → $2,000 now and $40,000 later, plus 4× the customers to upsell [MM: Section V intro]. Four-week billing +8.3%, "at 20% margins that skyrockets annual profit by 41%"; "+3% for five extra words," in a 10% profit business +30% profit [MM: Continuity Discount Offers]. 1 in 8 prepay → +50% 30-day profit; standalone at 1.33×–2.66× → 50–90% continuity [MM: Continuity Bonus Offers]. Waived fee 3–5× monthly, 1.5–3× for cash [MM: Waived Fee Offer].
12. [S] Guarantee and refund arithmetic. 130% as many sales with refunds doubling 5% → 10%: 95 net versus 117 net = 1.23×, "all to the bottom line"; "don't be emotional, just do the math" [Offers p157]. High fulfilment cost → conditional or anti-guarantee [Offers p157–158]. [E] Fladlien: $2,997 course, $25,000 buy-back, +$3M sales, 10 payouts → $2.75M net [Offers p163]. WYMB only if refund rate < 5%; expect ~10% to ask [MM: Win Your Money Back]. Pay-later cancellations > 10% → promised too much [MM: Pay Less Now or Pay More Later].
13. [S] CS arithmetic (SaaS case): a rep at $70k–$100k/year saving $500k–$2M/year is "at least 5:1"; "if something can get you a 5× or a 10× within the company, you make that bet every time"; the bigger the business, the more the owner is an investor allocating to the highest internal return [T-CaseSaaS 00:38:41]. Pilot on 15% because 1:25 → 1:1 is a large headcount add [T-CaseSaaS 00:37:50]. $200 to keep $2,000 → $1,800 rather than zero [T-CaseSaaS 00:26:28]. CAC should rise only with CPM: "if CPMs go up 15% a year, that's the amount your CAC should go up"; 10:1 becoming 5:1 or 3:1 within a year is a reputation signal; counter by raising LTV [T-CaseSaaS 00:25:19–00:26:00].
14. [S] Sales multipliers as economics: 104 extra selling days = +29% "minimum" [T-Sales 00:01:15]; contact within 60 seconds → +391% close likelihood, "you could 4× your business tomorrow"; > 5 minutes → −80% [T-Sales 00:03:23, 00:05:05]; 5 minutes of prep → ~+30% show-up [T-Sales 00:17:53]; hire a rep when it can 2–3× revenue and the gain is "significantly greater than the onboarding cost" [T-Sales 00:07:45]. "If we improve conversion 30%, revenue +30%; at 90% gross margin and 30% net margin that doubles profit" [T-Sales 02:01:25].
15. [E] Whole-model numbers. Gym Launch survey (158 gyms, 11 months): +$19,932/month top line (+$239,000/year); +$13,339/month recurring; profit $2,943 → $8,940/month (3.1×); churn 10.7% → 6.8%; price $129 → $167/month; the pitch: "$239,000 extra this year" for $42,000 [Offers p60, p68–69]. Agency Grand Slam Offer: 2.5× response × 2.5× close × 4× price = 22.4× cash up front, $10,000 → $112,000 [Offers p43–44]. Gym opening: $5 leads, 50% show, 50% close, $680 per customer, "one dollar in, 34 dollars out, in 48 hours" [MM: Start Here].

**Decision rules, formulas, thresholds**

- Max monthly revenue = new clients/month × LTV; gross profit = revenue − direct cost of one more customer; LTV = LTGP = gross profit per period × periods [Offers p37–38]; LTGP = lifetime revenue − delivery cost [Leads: Paid Ads II].
- LTGP:CAC ≥ 3:1 (observed); 4:1 → 12:1 with two referrals each; acquisition cost ≤ 1/3 of lifetime profit; CAC < 3× industry average → fix LTGP, > 3× → fix ads [Leads: Paid Ads II; Customer Referrals; Employees].
- Cost per engaged lead = payroll ÷ engaged leads [Leads: Employees]; cold outreach $120/show, $360/client, 10:1 [Leads: Cold Outreach]; lead magnet $3,000 → $1,000 CAC [Leads: Offers and Lead Magnets]; $25 × 4 = $100 [T-LeadMagnets 19:17]; freemium CAC = delivery cost ÷ conversion [T-Moats #12].
- Max allowable CAC = LTGP ÷ 3; affiliate tiers 25 / 50 / 100% of it [Leads: Affiliates and Partners].
- 30 days: cash ≥ cost to get + fulfil (CFA) [Leads: Paid Ads II]; MM minimum = 30-day profit > cost to get and service one customer; $100M = funds ≥ 2 more; attraction offer covers cost "multiple times over" [MM: Ten Years; Section VI; Attraction Offers Conclusion].
- 2 × 2 × 2 = 8×; 3 × 3 × 3 = 27× [MM: Section I]. Raise price until revenue falls [MM: Section VI]; up to 100× cost; 3× the top competitor [Offers p74, p68].
- Continuity: $10,000 now vs $2,000 + $40,000; four-week billing +8.3% (+41% profit at 20% margin); +3% fee (+30% profit at 10% margin); 1 in 8 prepay → +50% 30-day profit [MM: Section V intro; Continuity Discount; Continuity Bonus].
- Guarantee math 1.23× [Offers p157]; WYMB refund rate < 5%, expect 10% claims [MM: Win Your Money Back].
- Internal bets ≥ 5:1 [T-CaseSaaS 00:38:41]; CAC may rise with CPM (~15%/year) [T-CaseSaaS 00:25:19]; $200 per $2,000 saved [T-CaseSaaS 00:26:28].
- +29% (7-day selling); +391% (60-second response); +30% show-up (5-minute prep); +30% conversion at 90% GM / 30% net = 2× profit [T-Sales 00:01:15, 00:03:23, 00:17:53, 02:01:25].
- Content: $20,000/month per person; $25 CPM equivalence [T-Content 11:00–12:00]; ~$3.33 per engaged lead [Leads: Employees].

**When / when not, and stage**

- 3:1 is "a pattern I personally observed, not a rule" [Leads: Paid Ads II]. The 30-day rule is for bootstrapped businesses; funded companies "have the money to burn" [MM: Section VI; Section I].
- Two clocks: LTGP:CAC is lifetime efficiency; CFA and the Money Models rule are 30-day cash; Leads' 3.3:1 example only breaks even after two months until an upsell is added [Leads: Paid Ads II].
- Content costs are the author's own setup, "you can scale this down" [T-Content 12:00]; internal-ROI math is for a bigger business where "the owner is an investor" [T-CaseSaaS 00:38:41].
- Broad guarantees for low-ticket B2C, specific ones for high-ticket B2B [Offers p162].
- Price low and raise while an offer is new [MM: Section VI]; premium from entry once value is proven, "your product must deliver" [Offers p62, p67–68]. [I] A 2021-vs-2025 stage distinction the author does not state as such.

**Author's examples with numbers**

- [E] $1,000 × 90% × 5 = $4,500 LTV [Offers p38]; $15/$5 × 10 = $100 LTGP, CAC $30, $100 upsell at 1 in 5 [Leads: Paid Ads II]; $100,000 / 1,000 / 1 in 10 / $4,000 = 4:1 [Leads: Employees]; $360 per client, 10:1 [Leads: Cold Outreach]; $40 max CAC, tiers $10 / $20 / $40, 12.5:1 [Leads: Affiliates and Partners].
- [E] 22.4×, $10,000 → $112,000 [Offers p43–44]; $1 in → $34 out in 48 hours [MM: Start Here]; 1.23×, Fladlien $2.75M net [Offers p157, p163]; $70–100k rep vs $500k–2M saved [T-CaseSaaS 00:38:41]; $40,000/month content, "$2M/month exposure" [T-Content 11:00–12:00].

**Connections and tensions**

- Tension: lifetime (LTGP:CAC) versus 30-day cash. Leads' example passes the first and fails the second until an upsell is added [Leads: Paid Ads II]; Money Models makes 30 days primary and never states a lifetime ratio [MM: Ten Years In Ten Minutes]. Resolution: pass 30 days first, then maximise LTV [MM: Section VI].
- Tension: 3:1 stated as an observation [Leads: Paid Ads II] but applied as a hard threshold for hand-offs, payouts and hiring [Leads: Cold Outreach; Affiliates and Partners; Employees]. Both readings are the author's.
- Tension: "raise until you make less" for new offers (2025) [MM: Section VI] versus "as much as humanly possible" and 3× the top competitor from entry (2021) [Offers p73, p68]. [I] A value-first premium versus a test procedure; not reconciled explicitly.
- [I] The +41% and +30% profit multipliers assume costs do not move with the extra revenue; stated as outcomes, not as a model [MM: Continuity Discount Offers].

**Not covered by the sources**

- No industry CAC or LTGP benchmarks; no treatment of overhead, net margin or payback beyond the 30-day float; delivery cost left at "direct cost of an additional customer"; no cohort or discount-rate treatment of LTV; profit multipliers given without cost assumptions.

### Scaling

**Core statements**

1. [S] Only two ways to grow: more customers, or make each worth more; Offers splits the second into purchase value and frequency "because it will be easier to understand the money models in Volume III" [Offers p37; Leads: The Problem This Book Solves]. Money Models: 2× value × 2× customers × 2× speed = 8× growth; 3× each = 27× [MM: Section I]. "Maintenance is a myth": 9 %/yr is maintenance; growing markets need 20–30 %/yr [Offers p36–37].
2. [S] **More, Better, New**, used "every time I want to increase the engaged lead flow in a portfolio company" [Leads: More Better New]. **More** = Rule of 100: 100 primary actions/day for 100 days (100 reach-outs; 100 minutes of content plus ≥1 post; 100 minutes on ads plus 100 days at budget); "the biggest increases often come from advertising more." **Better** = test at the constraint (see Measurement). **New** = new placements → new platforms → new core-four activity, in that order "nine times out of ten", only "when the returns from more and better are lower than what you could get from a new placement"; then cycle back. Stage flag: businesses get "enough customers from one platform to get them to $1M–$3M per year" and hit a wall via the **Size of the Pie Fallacy** (one method, one platform, one audience, 3–4 visible competitors read as the whole market): "saying 'I'm as big as I can get' is much easier than saying 'I'm not as good at advertising as I thought.'" [E] Chiropractor-niche agency at $2M/yr on one platform calling the niche saturated; another owner in the same niche spent $30k across four platforms in a week. First question before investing: "What's stopping them from doing ten times what they're currently doing?"
3. [S] **The Roadmap, Levels 1–7** [Leads: The Roadmap]: L1 friends know (one offer, one avatar, one platform; warm outreach). L2 everyone you know, consistently; exact inputs per engaged lead known; warm outreach plus content ("I wish I would have started with posting free content"). L3 employees advertise for you (you maxed personal inputs, not the platform). L4 product good enough for **≥25 % of customers from referrals**, then re-ramp ads ([E] product updated from feedback every two weeks; "this is where most people mess up. They let their product slip and never recover"). L5 new audiences → all placements → another platform, lead-getter or method; target ≥2 methods on multiple platforms. L6 "hire killers": experienced executives per method, "you're not looking for potential" ([E] "we capped here" at Gym Launch). L7 after a billion. Target state: a third of customers referred, executives drive growth without you. Timeline: "five to ten years" if you know what to do; >10 years for his own first $100M.
4. [S] **Money model stages**: I Get Cash (attraction), II Get More Cash (upsell, downsell), III Get The Most Cash (continuity). "None of my businesses started with a fully forged Money Model. They all start at Stage I. Even Acquisition.com!" A finished model from zero "will collapse on top of you." Evolution: customers reliably → they pay for themselves → they pay for other customers → maximise LTV → max ad spend; "each stage pays for the next"; reliability is financial and operational. "When your Money Model starts working, your business starts breaking" → find someone to build and lead the team. Build rules: **perfect one offer at a time** ("do it so many times it gets automatic, then go to the next stage"); **measure in quarters, not weeks**; **price new offers cheap, then raise until you cannot make up for the nos with the extra cash from the yeses**; "simple scales, fancy fails"; affiliate products fill gaps at any stage [MM: Section VI].
5. [S] **Niche until $10M**: "if you are under $10M per year, niching down will make you more money"; past $10M growth is bounded by TAM, then broaden up, down or adjacent; $10M/yr is a 0.4 % achievement; single-niche companies reached $30M+; "If you are at $1M or $3M, thinking you have capped and must expand, you are wrong. You just need to be better." [Offers p31, p54]. **First Grand Slam Offer → first $100,000** [Offers p187].
6. [S] **Volume × time = skill**, "the equation of life, or at least business" [T-Content 42:00]; the effort gap is ~50×, not 2–4× [T-Content 09:00]. [E] 7 → 80 posts/week: "We 10× the inputs and we got 10× the output" [T-Content 19:00]; ~1M audience in six months [10:00]. His setup ($20k/month per person, 2 days/month + 4 h/week) is explicitly "you can scale this down" [12:00]. Sales version: "the best salespeople do the most volume"; do double your best closer's effort; skill rises in ~2–3 month, ~1 year, ~2–3 year stages and income spikes at the end [T-Sales 02:08–02:10].
7. [S] **Sales multipliers are throughput levers outside the call**: "you cannot get 400 % increases from closing skill" but can from these [T-Sales 00:06]. Sell 7 days (+29 % minimum) [00:01]; respond <60 s (+391 %; >5 min = −80 %) [00:03–00:05]; "call now" [00:07]; 15-minute slots plus an off-the-call SOP [00:08]; best leads to best closers ([E] company 5×'d on scored allocation) [00:11]; reward show-up rates (+20 % show = +20 % close, far easier) [00:14]; manual reminders (+~30 % show) [00:16]; same-day/next-day only, and "then I can't book all my appointments" → hire more reps [00:19].
8. [S] **Sales-floor hiring and comp** [T-Sales]: hire another rep if it can 2–3× revenue and the gain "is significantly greater than the onboarding cost" [00:07:45]; train so you can pay below market [00:20]; group interviews, "one of the easiest ways to scale a sales team" [02:12]; hire for the lowest skill deficiency (script = 2–3 weeks; "kindness" ≈ 20 sub-skills), flipped for rare expertise [02:14]; comp = recruiting, not performance: the only comp change that changed behaviour was paying on cash collected [02:15]; sales and marketing under one CRO [02:16]; enterprise/government 6–12-month cycles: "much of this is not going to apply", multi-thread [02:05].
9. [S] **Employees are the lever**: 40 h of doing → 4 h of managing; 200 → 20; a manager at 4 h/week → "four hours of work for 200 hours of lead-getting." $2M profit that runs without you "could easily be worth $10,000,000+"; with you working around the clock it is "a high paying job." "Replace yourself as soon as you can." "Hire and train anyone willing" for ground-level jobs, picky only for "hyper-specific-multiple-six-figure-C-suite employees" [Leads: #2 Employees]. [E] Cold-outreach growth was capped by hiring (40 interviews per hire); group interviews fixed it and outreach sales doubled in a quarter.
10. [S] **Agencies** are for learning new methods and platforms: "if you have no money, then agencies are out of the question"; work with one for 6 months, pay extra for explanations, run your team in parallel, cancel once it beats them. [E] Time to beat: ~1 year → <6 months [Leads: #3 Agencies].
11. [S] **Lead getters**: a lead getter who gets lead getters is "the makings of a $100M Leads machine" [Leads: Section IV intro]; natural order referrals → employees → agencies → affiliates [Leads: Section IV Conclusion]; 10 customers/month caps at $1.2M/yr, 10 affiliates/month each bringing one $10k customer/month → $7.8M in 12 months [Leads: #4 Affiliates and Partners]; affiliates are "one of the most scalable ways to advertise in any niche", with a deliberately unscalable top prize and a scalable long tail [T-Affiliates 05:04, 18:00].
12. [S] **Delivery format is the constraint**: DFY → DWY moved Gym Launch from eight gyms/month to "hundreds" at "maybe a third" of the fee [Offers p67–68, p106–107]. "Create flow. Monetize flow. Then add friction": over-deliver first, then use cash to build systems that deliver the same value cheaper, "this is how businesses beat one another" [Offers p106–107]. One-to-many assets are "the bedrock" ("this is exactly why software becomes so valuable") [Offers p113–114]. Information lead magnets are "infinitely scalable, basically no operational drag"; service lead magnets need qualification [T-LeadMagnets 16:00–18:42].
13. [S] **Most businesses degrade with scale**; a woven-in advantage "drives your competitors away as you get bigger" [T-Moats intro, #4]. The 1:1 service business "provides value one-on-one, one-on-two provides less; most businesses are structured to deteriorate as more customers come in" [T-Moats #1].
14. [S] **Retention is the compounding vehicle at scale**: acquisition "is necessary especially when you're starting out", but "what it took you to get here isn't what it takes to create a really big business"; direct-response cap: "the marketing team of a $300M business" doing $30M because it loses everyone yearly [T-CaseSaaS 35:40–37:00]. The bigger the business, the more the owner is an investor making 5–10× internal bets [38:41]. Launch inflow "breaks infrastructure"; don't hire 100 reps for three days; pilot before scaling headcount [02:30–04:35].
15. [S] **Acquisitions and consolidation** (PE-scale, flagged): "small businesses get small and stay small through competition; big businesses think about consolidation"; "I'd rather own 20 % of a billion-dollar company than 100 % of a million-dollar company"; scaled down to "own one city" for home-maintenance businesses [T-Moats #11]. [E] Roofing company (~$60M, capped by its lead source): buy 1–3 pay-per-lead agencies, then the financing company.
16. [S] Content has two valid scaling orders, depth-then-width (single-channel risk) or width-then-depth ("sucky fluff" risk); "if you already have a sizable business, scale up faster" [Leads: Content II]. Paid ads Phase Three: budget = customers you can handle × CAC, +20 % "because ads get less efficient as they scale", then commit ("if the number terrifies you, you're doing it right") [Leads: Paid Ads II]; Client Financed Acquisition is how he "scaled every company past $1M/mo in the first twelve months without outside funding." A lead-magnet front end has lower ROAS up front but higher over a longer horizon "and scales better" [T-OffersLM 06:18].

**Decision rules, formulas, thresholds**

- Max monthly revenue = new clients/month × LTV; 9 %/yr = maintenance [Offers p36–37].
- Rule of 100; Open To Goal = work until N outcomes, not N attempts [Leads: More Better New; Open To Goal].
- New only when More/Better returns < a new placement; placements → platforms → core four [Leads: More Better New].
- After warm outreach: more time than money → content; more money than time → cold outreach or ads [Leads: Section III conclusion].
- Cold outreach: "for every X contacted you get Y customers, solve for X"; hand off once leads convert to more than a rep costs, at ≥3× lifetime profit; "then you just add bodies" [Leads: #3 Cold Outreach].
- One-page plan: do the daily action until you can pay someone, then make employees the target lead type [Leads: One Page Advertising Checklist].
- Cost per engaged lead = payroll ÷ engaged leads ([E] Acquisition.com ≈ $3.33) [Leads: #2 Employees].
- Raise price 20 % every five customers [Leads: #1 Warm Outreach]; raise until revenue falls [MM: Section VI]; "99 percent of businesses need to raise their prices to grow" [Offers p69].
- Capacity caps: raise 10–20 %, re-cap; "always have less spots available than you think you can sell" [Offers p136–137].
- Ad budget = customers × CAC × 1.2 [Leads: Paid Ads II]. Content caps: short ≤10/day/platform, long ≤5 days/week [Leads: Content II].
- Affiliates: 10–20 first; pay forever, never cap [Leads: #4 Affiliates]. Freemium CAC = cost of the free thing ÷ conversion [T-Moats #12].

**When / when not, and stage**

- Under $10M niche; at $1M–$3M "you just need to be better" [Offers p54] or apply More Better New [Leads: More Better New]. [I] Same wall, two levers: offer quality (2021), advertising breadth (2023).
- First offer: over-deliver, even fly out; friction only after flow [Offers p105–106]. "Once you've monetized an offer, rarely should you change it" [Offers p179–180].
- No money → no agency; paid ads last; affiliates only after you can manage people inside [Leads: #3 Agencies; Paid Ads II; Section IV Conclusion].
- 1:1 service deteriorates with scale; the author's answers elsewhere are DWY, one-to-many, productising [T-Moats #1; Offers p106–113] [I].
- Consolidation and vertical integration: PE and capital; scaled down only to "own one city" [T-Moats #5, #11].
- Enterprise/government: multipliers and CLOSER don't apply [T-Sales 02:05]. Big business: rolling cohorts; small: real capacity limits [T-LeadMagnets 30:11–30:47].

**Author's examples with numbers** [E]

- Gym Launch: $476k/month in 3 months (decoy) → ~$1.5M/month (upsell + payment plans) → $2.3M/month within 14 months (menu + feature downsells) → $4.4M/month by month 20 with Prestige Labs [MM: Section VI].
- Cold outreach ramp 0 → 2 → 4 → 6 → 10 → 14 → 20 → 30 sales/month over ~a year [Leads: #3 Cold Outreach].

**Connections and tensions**

- [I] Three named walls, all diagnosed as skill, breadth or product deficits, never market size: $1M–$3M single-platform (Leads), $1M/$3M "need to be better" (Offers), L6 "we capped here" (Leads Roadmap).

Partial resolution by the author: his content asset was funded by other businesses and the only ask was "get to $3M then we partner" [T-Content 49:00]; bootstrapped businesses "must get customers at a profit" [MM: Section VI]. Otherwise open.
- Tension: "advertising lets you have a terrible product and still make money" [Leads: Section I intro] vs L4 "product good enough for consistent referrals… most people mess up here" [Leads: The Roadmap] and "most businesses degrade with scale" [T-Moats]. Resolved by stage: ads buy chances early; referrals and retention compound later.
- Tension on comp: "no comp change ever changed behaviour except paying on cash collected" [T-Sales 02:15] vs a 10–15 % save commission that made CS reach out 3–5 times [T-CaseSaaS 26:28–27:37]. Not reconciled; [I] the second paired comp with a script and a dedicated team.

**Not covered by the sources**

### Moats

**Core statements**

1. [S] Umbrella: **monopoly** ("mono = one, poly = to sell"), via supply, demand, media that talks to those people, technology, capital, or licence. "You don't need all 13… just do one right, all the way." Small version: "create a small network of a very small niche that you own" [T-Moats zoom-out].
2. [S] **The 13 ways with the author's small-business translations** [T-Moats]:
   - **#1 Network effects**: customer base or product ecosystem. Diagnostic: "if more people enter my network does it add value to every other customer?" **Warning: the 1:1 service business is the opposite and deteriorates**; counter-example: a dentist buying group gains power per member.
   - **#2 Exclusive control of a resource or technology**: physical (De Beers) or **data and proprietary tech** (Google's 20 years of search data; "the best AI will win, not the first").
   - **#3 Government regulation and licensing**: casino licence "a licence to print money." Small: knowing how to navigate regulation (a liquor licence as a profit centre).
   - **#4 Economies of scale**: low-cost leadership; "no advantage to being the second cheapest." [E] ALAN's message volume → lower unit cost plus data no later entrant has, "a compounding advantage."
   - **#5 Vertical integration**: "sweetheart of private equity"; control supply and distribution "click to close"; "control is the opposite of risk." [E] His self-publishing chain.
   - **#6 Brand and loyalty**, "my personal favorite": the others need time, complexity or capital; brand "you can make with skill; anyone can build one." Effect: higher conversion, lower CAC, premium prices on a commodity. How: "keep their promises and make associations clearly and deliberately over a long period of time with what their customers value, and strongly disassociate with the things and people their customers don't value."
   - **#7 Strategic pricing**: predatory pricing and feature bundling (Office; Instagram Stories); **price discrimination** by segment. Small: "be very clear on the one or two customer segments… so you can sell to the right wallet"; never the same thing at two prices, tweak the offer ("add a free cocktail"), "especially works well with services." Pricing is "one of the easiest levers."
   - **#8 Exclusive contracts**: with supply, customers (incumbency compounds) or distribution; "fundamentally a B2B or B2B2C strategy." Small: "make it worth it for the distributor" with lower cost, a retail kit, free staff training, "perks established players aren't willing to do."
   - **#9 Huge capital requirements**: knock out 99.9 % of owners. Small: with saved or raised capital, "pick vehicles that have fewer people competing against you."
   - **#10 Intellectual property**: patents, copyright, trademarks vs trade secrets (hard to enforce). **Limit: "someone's using my offer, my ad, my content, that's not considered intellectual property."** Test word: *pretending*. Royalty play: licence big brands to piggyback their branding (Oreo 8 %).
   - **#11 Acquisitions and mergers**: "assimilate your competition"; PE darling; sits on top of the others; lens "how do I eat up things that would cause risk to my business" (distribution, customer bases, media assets).
   - **#12 Innovative business model**: "find what everyone wants, make it better, give it away for free, monetize another way"; the marketing budget becomes the cost of the free thing; "free gets 10× the interest of not free." [E] Failed Prestige test kitchen ("a distraction"); hypothetical free gym → mandatory onboarding → PT sale, fine if 1 in 5 buy.
   - **#13 Control over distribution**: stronger than contracts because contracts can be broken (Amazon). Small: **local distribution** (dominate a tiny area, "in that tiny area you're the best", expand outward) or **distribution on a segment** ("if I know every CEO in America on a first-name basis I can sell private jets").
   - Small-business menu he names: brand (#6), pricing/segmentation (#7), local or segment distribution (#13), local consolidation (#11), free-plus-paid (#12), licensing others' brands (#10), distributor perks (#8).
3. [S] **Goodwill compounds**: "play games where if you wait you win, because goodwill compounds faster than money"; "the longer you can delay the ask, the bigger the ask"; a pitch "dramatically decreases the goodwill" [T-Content 47:00–49:00]. Same law: "the longer you delay the ask, the bigger the ask you can make" [Offers p128]. Goodwill = value − price → word of mouth → referrals [Leads: #1 Customer Referrals].
4. [S] **The happy customer is the uncopyable asset**: competitors copied offers, ads, lead magnets, pages, emails, scripts "with little success… the best advertising is a happy customer." Referral growth is exponential, core-four growth linear and "eventually hits a wall"; monthly referral % > churn % → compounding without ads [Leads: Referrals]. Milestones: ≥25 % referred (L4); a third in the target state [Leads: The Roadmap].
5. [S] "The audience is the compounding asset," not the content [Leads: Content I]. An affiliate army means "in theory you never need to advertise again" [Leads: #4 Affiliates].
6. [S] **Cold outreach as a private moat**: competitors cannot see it; invisible steps (scraping, personalisation, distribution, hiring, training) compound; no spokesperson → sellable ([E] the outreach team is why Gym Launch could be sold); platform rules "hardly changed in thirty years" [Leads: #3 Cold Outreach]. A mascot or non-founder likeness makes the business "less dependent on you and more sellable" [Leads: Paid Ads I].
7. [S] **Category of one**: a Grand Slam Offer "cannot be compared"; the bundle is "impossible to compare or confuse with the business down the street" [Offers p39–40, p117]; niche specificity lets you "charge 100× more for the exact same product" [p54–55]; one-to-many proprietary assets ([E] ~100-hour meal-plan app used by 4,000+ gyms) [p113–114]; scarcity is "a compounding strategy that increases in effectiveness over time. One of the few in the marketing arsenal." [p137].
8. [S] Data as operating advantage: chat-bubble page data drove roadmap priority ("82 % of complaints from one page") [T-CaseSaaS 18:00]; ALAN click-to-close data on 4,000 appointments/day underpins the multipliers [T-Sales 00:06]; affiliates "use your data to win for their audience" via the winners collateral [T-Affiliates 14:20].

**Decision rules, formulas, thresholds**

- Pick the one of the 13 you can "weave into the fabric" so the position strengthens with size [T-Moats intro].
- Freemium CAC = delivery cost ÷ conversion; give away what others charge for if buyers take the higher-profit stream [T-Moats #12].
- Price to the wallet by tweaking the offer, never the same thing twice [T-Moats #7]. Win distribution with perks incumbents won't give [T-Moats #8].
- Referrals − churn > 0 → compounding; L4 ≥25 %; machine ⅓ [Leads: Referrals; The Roadmap].
- Brand = promise-keeping and deliberate association over a long period [T-Moats #6].

**When / when not, and stage**

- Network effects, regulation, capital, vertical integration, M&A: capital or PE scale, each scaled down explicitly [T-Moats #1, #3, #5, #9, #11].
- Brand is the only moat available with skill alone [T-Moats #6]; niche authority first, "in that little pond you can be king" [T-Content 30:00–34:00].
- 1:1 service: no network effect by default; restructure or productise [T-Moats #1]. Exclusive contracts: B2B/B2B2C only [#8].
- Referral moat only once the product delights: "if your product were exceptional… you'd have more business than you could handle" [Leads: Referrals].
- [I] Local business: "the smaller the radius, the faster offers fatigue" [Offers p180], so local distribution (#13) is a moat whose wrapper must rotate.

**Author's examples with numbers** [E]

- ALAN 100 → 1,000 → 4–5,000 appointments/day [T-Moats #4]. Oreo 8 %; book rights ~$100M [#10]. Roofing ~$60M/yr [#11].
- Gym Launch $500k/week from word of mouth with ads shut off; "$30M in customers say someone referred them"; Dropbox 39× in 15 months [Leads: Referrals].
- A year of free training → testimonials → almost all converted to paying [T-LeadMagnets 20:11].

**Connections and tensions**

- Tension: "your offer, your ad, your content is not IP" [T-Moats #10] vs "category of one" [Offers p39]. [I] Resolution in the sources: incomparability is a perception effect at point of sale; the durable versions are the happy customer, the audience, the invisible outreach machine, the affiliate army [Leads].
- [I] #12 (free thing funded as marketing) = lead-magnet money math ($25 magnet, 1 in 4 → $100 CAC) [Leads: Offers and Lead Magnets; T-LeadMagnets 19:17] = Money Models' attraction offers. One arithmetic, three vocabularies.
- [I] #6 brand = "goodwill compounds" (T-Content), which adds sequencing: do, then talk; niche before broad.
- [I] Money Models supplies lock-in without naming moats: learned complexity as switching cost [MM: Trial With Penalty], waived-fee commitments [MM: Waived Fee Offer], stealing competitors' upset customers [MM: Rollover Upsell].
- #4 low-cost leadership vs "only compete on price with costs at 1/10th of competitors" [Offers p62]: consistent; cost is a moat only when the gap is structural.

**Not covered by the sources**
No way to measure a moat's strength or decay; nothing on defending against a better-funded copier beyond "do one all the way"; the brand-building method is deferred to a keynote outside the corpus.

### Measurement and testing

**Core statements**

1. [S] **Track → Lose → Print** [Leads: Paid Ads II]: set up accurate return tracking before spending ("if you don't track, you're gonna get cleaned out"); expect to lose ("losses frequent but small, wins rare but large"; then "100× down" on the winner); then spend as much as constraints allow. **Per-ad test budget: 2× the cash collected from a new customer in the first 30 days** (not LTGP) as long as it gets leads; **no leads at all → stop before 1×**. "Measure the returns over a long time horizon, not next week"; [E] one business took a year to make ads profitable, then earned the year back the next month. **Set-aside: 1 %, 5 % or 10 % of the ad budget for tests "without expecting a return… an investment in your education"** [Leads: Section V intro].
2. [S] **Better = test at the constraint** [Leads: More Better New]: the constraint is the step with the biggest drop-off; +5 points there beats +5 elsewhere ([E] 30 % opt-in / 5 % apply / 50 % schedule: 5→10 % = 2×, 30→35 % = 1.16×, 50→55 % = 1.1×). **One test per week per platform**, launched Monday; next Monday pick winners, **log every test in a running log** ("start a zillion improvements later, not at square one"), design the next to beat the control; **four failed tries (one month) → next constraint**. Why one: simultaneous tests confound, steps interact, it forces prioritisation, a week is enough data for his team and spend.
3. [S] **Packaging over contents**: "test the wrapping more than the contents"; a headline alone changes take-up 2×, 3×, 10× [T-LeadMagnets 05:20]; test order headline → image → subheadline [Leads: Offers and Lead Magnets]. [E] $100M Leads: **six titles**, cover image and sub-headlines split-tested; "two years writing, two days testing" [T-LeadMagnets 24:48]. **Ladder without budget**: small ad tests → story polls (filter responders) → texts or A/B comment posts [T-LeadMagnets 23:56]. Names: run 2–3, winner becomes control; "I honestly have no idea why some names win… don't be emotional" [Offers p178]. Fatigue change order: creative → copy → headline → duration → enhancer → monetization [Offers p178–179].
4. [S] **Pilots as scientific method** [T-CaseSaaS]: route **15 %** of new customers through the new process "until we've proven it out" [03:57]; a test is a hypothesis: did we do the thing, did the result happen; **define ahead of time what the stats must look like for the bet to be worth it**, then roll out; a marginal result means "this isn't the way we fix it", not "we shouldn't fix it" [38:00]; a $70–100k rep saving $500k–$2M = ≥5:1, "you make that bet every time" [38:41].
5. [S] **"If you don't track, you don't care."** "Our refund rate is low" is not CSAT/NPS; the silent majority dislike you and tell others; metrics are an intervention ("weigh yourself every morning and you start to lose weight"); if unsure how to fix something, step one is track it [T-CaseSaaS 32:00]. Chat-bubble data on which page users asked for help → fix that page before new features [17:08–18:50].
6. [S] **Monthly content metrics**: followers and reach (how big), growth rate = new ÷ start (how fast); "we can only control inputs" → fix posting and ask cadence, "Do. Not. Stop."; "the more ways you measure, the more ways you can win" [Leads: Content II]. Reward test: "your audience grows; if not, your stuff isn't that good" [Leads: Content I]. Add one CTA, verify growth doesn't slow, add another [Content II]. **Tweets as idea tests** (~5/day; traction = validated idea; threads → long form, tweets → shorts; "net zero time") [T-Content 13:00–14:30]. Virality = **CTR × watch time** [T-Content 15:00].
7. [S] **Baselines and cohorts**: "figure out your referral percentages and churn percentages to set a baseline" [Leads: Referrals]; the investor's number-one metric is "how many of last year's customers are still paying" ([E] 100/200/300 with full churn vs 100 kept yearly, same size at year three, "you want business 2") [T-CaseSaaS 36:00–37:16].
8. [S] **CAC diagnostics**: CAC should rise only with CPMs ("if CPMs go up 15 % a year, that's the amount your CAC should go up"); 10:1 → 5:1 or 3:1 in a year signals reputation (anti-referrals) [T-CaseSaaS 25:19–26:00]. CAC below 3× industry average → fix the business model (LTGP); above → fix advertising; LTGP:CAC < 3:1 in every struggling portfolio company, "a pattern I personally observed, not a rule"; engaged leads with the problem and money who don't buy = sales problem [Leads: Paid Ads II; #2 Employees].
9. [S] **Sales measurement** [T-Sales]: record every customer call across the lifecycle ("stop the video, go do this now"); weekly sales + CS game tape closes the 5–10 % post-close back-out gap [00:28–00:31]; record yourself when hot, "great closers never get cold" [00:32]; ALAN data: opening calendars was "the single strongest correlate to overall throughput" [00:06]; fake-busy calendars "tested, doesn't do anything" [00:08]; Zap CRM "processed sale" → Slack so kudos fire only on real sales [02:15]; price tests premeditated, never mid-sale [02:30]; many 2–4-call closes = insufficient pre-education [02:20]. **Lead scoring** as measurement: close probability as score or green/yellow/red, used to allocate leads [00:11, 02:27].
10. [S] **Money-model diagnostics** [MM]: refund rate <5 % before Win Your Money Back; expect ~10 % money-back requests; store credit converted the same as cash [WYMB]; >10 % pay-later cancels = promised too much, conditions too low, or price too high [Pay Less Now]; payment plans: close rate up while paid-in-full count does not fall [Payment Plan Downsells]; >5 % early cancels → investigate [Waived Fee Offer]; test four framings of one discount, and "buy 1 get 1" vs "buy 1 get 3 socks" [Decoy; BXGY]; price tests planned with a specific price, count and date [Rules of Downselling]; **measure in quarters, not weeks** [Section VI].
11. [S] **Customer-result loop**: survey best customers → interview → common actions → force new customers to repeat → measure → match guarantee conditions to those actions ([E] gym owners who launched ads and sold in 7 days had 3× LTGP); monthly: fix the most common problem, test on a small group of struggling customers, roll out if solved [Leads: Referrals]. Retrain on any major dip [Leads: #2 Employees].
12. [S] **Volume before judgement**: 5,000 flyers then 5,000/day for a month; 0.5 % decent, 1 % winner; 300 flyers = 1/1500 of the effort [Leads: Open To Goal; T-Content 08:30]. Tweak outreach only after 100 conversations or 10,000 emails; test a few hundred names per list source; 10–20 % of effort on new tech; "the person who runs it has to know every single stat" (two untracked attempts failed) [Leads: #3 Cold Outreach].
13. [S] Offer math: "don't be emotional, just do the math" (130 sales at 10 % refunds beats 100 at 5 %) [Offers p157]; "if you try one hundred offers, I promise you will succeed" [p56]. Direct-offer ads: higher ROAS up front; lead magnet: higher when the horizon is expanded, "the part people miss" [T-OffersLM 06:18]. Affiliates: real-time leaderboard proves attribution; update "the winners" collateral from data [T-Affiliates 12:38–14:53].

**Decision rules, formulas, thresholds**

- Per-ad: 2× 30-day cash if getting leads; stop <1× if none; set-aside 1/5/10 % [Leads: Paid Ads II; Section V intro].
- One test/week/platform; log; 4 fails → next constraint; constraint = biggest drop-off [Leads: More Better New].
- Pilot 15 %; pre-defined success stats; ≥5:1 internal bets [T-CaseSaaS].
- Monthly: followers, reach, growth %; referral % and churn % baseline; last-year cohort still paying [Leads: Content II; Referrals; T-CaseSaaS].
- CAC growth ≤ CPM growth [T-CaseSaaS]; CAC vs 3× industry average; LTGP:CAC ≥ 3:1 [Leads].
- Refund <5 % before WYMB; >10 % pay-later cancels; >5 % early cancels; paid-in-full count flat [MM].
- Flyers 0.5 % / 1 % at 5,000 [Leads: Open To Goal]; names 2–3 then control [Offers p178]; virality = CTR × watch time [T-Content 15:00].
- [I] Cadence by object: funnel tests weekly; money-model stages quarterly; ad payback over a year.

**When / when not, and stage**

- Track before spending, any size [Leads: Paid Ads II]. No ad budget → polls; no audience → texts and A/B comments [T-LeadMagnets 23:56].
- Pilot when the intervention is expensive (1:25 → 1:1 headcount) [T-CaseSaaS 38:00]. "You're never too big for good CS" and metrics [33:00].
- Early outreach: no tweaks before 100 conversations / 10,000 emails [Leads: Cold Outreach]. Record every call from day one; joint game tape once a CS team exists [T-Sales 00:28].

**Author's examples with numbers** [E]

- 15 % pilot: ascension 20 % → 48 %; 2.4× company-wide; −61 % cancellations; $2M bottom line in six months [T-CaseSaaS 07:27, 29:27–30:02].
- Six-title test: Advertising beat Promotion; Leads beat Advertising and Marketing; real image beat cartoon [Leads: Offers and Lead Magnets].
- 78 % of clients consumed ≥2 long-form pieces before booking; 1 in 5 who saw a daily post didn't know about the book [Leads: Content II].
- Weigh-out day: close rates "absurd, like 100 %" after reps watched 5–20 results [T-Sales 00:36]. 7-day ad-and-sale cohort: 3× LTGP [Leads: Referrals].

**Connections and tensions**

- Tension on cadence: one test per week per platform [Leads] vs measure in quarters [MM] vs ad returns over a long horizon [Leads] vs "4× your business tomorrow" with <60 s response [T-Sales 00:03]. [I] Different objects (funnel step, offer stage, ad payback, throughput multiplier); the author never states the hierarchy.
- Tension: Rule of 100 says do more without judging; the test cadence says change one variable weekly [Leads]. Resolved in the same chapter: More is volume, Better is one controlled variable.
- [I] "If you don't track, you don't care" (CS), "you're gonna get cleaned out" (ads), "know every stat" (outreach) are one rule.
- [I] Lead scoring (T-Sales) and the affiliate leaderboard (T-Affiliates) turn measurement into allocation and motivation, the "metrics as intervention" claim in practice.
- "I honestly have no idea why some names win" [Offers p178] alongside four naming conventions [T-LeadMagnets 25:35]: conventions generate candidates, tests pick winners.

**Not covered by the sources**
