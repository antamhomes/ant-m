4. **[SOURCE]** [T-Sales 02:31] never change the price to close, change the terms or the components. Your discount instrument is the launch fee and the feature set, not the percentage.

**What must change is not the number but where it appears in the conversation.** The owner's decision variable is their net crown amount and their floor. The percentage is an accounting detail that you disclose plainly when asked and never lead with. **[SOURCE]** [Offers p39-40] sell in a category of one, avoid the commodity comparison; [T-Moats #7] segment by what the buyer actually values.

## B4. The guarantee: right instrument, wrong exposure control

**[ANTAM]** Written annual minimum, at least long-term rent plus energy, Deloitte Rent Index basis, on every flat Antam accepts, live from day one, 12-month measurement window from the first guest, reduced pro rata for owner-blocked nights (2/365, 3/365 in season), shortfall settled from Antam's fee.

**Keep it. It is the strongest thing you have built.** It answers the owner's real alternative in one sentence, it is falsifiable, and it is the reason the 30 % holds.

Three changes:

1. **Condition it on owner actions, explicitly and in writing.** **[SOURCE]** [Leads: Customer Referrals] set guarantee conditions equal to the actions that best customers take; [Offers p157-158] high fulfilment cost means conditional rather than unconditional; [T-Sales 02:02] only guarantee what you control. You control pricing, listing, guests, cleaning. You do not control: availability, the flat's condition, owner-imposed price floors, furnishing decisions, or SVJ intervention. The floor must therefore be conditional on a named list: minimum available nights, agreed fit-out and photo standard delivered by the agreed go-live date, no owner-imposed minimum prices, no mid-term calendar withdrawal beyond the pro-rata rule. This is not weakening the guarantee. It converts it into a behaviour contract that produces the result it promises.
2. **Aggregate exposure: unresolved by decision, with the method fixed now.** **[DECIDED]** no cap figure is invented today; intake discipline is the primary control and the aggregate cap stays an explicit open risk-control item.

   That is the right call on an unmeasured business, and it does not dispose of the risk. Intake discipline controls *idiosyncratic* risk, which is that this flat is bad. It does not control *correlated* risk, which is that this year is bad. A flat that clears its floor in nine years out of ten fails in the same year as every other flat. **[SOURCE]** [MM: Win Your Money Back] the offer is run only where the failure rate is low **and** the downside can be stomached; the library never authorises an uncapped promise.

   **The calculation, to be run the week the economics exist:**

   *Step 1, headroom per flat.* At underwriting, record h = (expected annual owner net ÷ contracted floor) − 1. The floor is long-term rent plus energy, so a flat underwritten at 1.3× long-term rent carries roughly 20 % headroom once energy is added to the floor, and a flat at 1.7× carries roughly 55 %. **[INFER]** This single number decides whether the guarantee is cheap or expensive, and nobody is recording it today.

   *Step 2, portfolio shock.* For a market-wide revenue shock s, claim(i, s) = max(0, floor(i) − expected(i) × (1 − s)). Total claim C(s) = the sum of claim(i, s) across all guaranteed flats. Run s at 10 %, 20 % and 30 % simultaneously across the whole portfolio. Do not model flats independently; moving together is the entire point.

   *Step 3, stressed capacity.* The trap in a naive cap is that Antam's fee income falls in the same year the claims rise. Absorbable(s) = annual gross profit **at that same shock** − fixed costs − cash reserve. Compute gross profit at the shock, never at today's run rate.

   *Step 4, the cap.* Cap = α × Absorbable(s), where α is the share of a bad year's surplus the founder is willing to put at risk. **[UNKNOWN]** α and the shock severity to underwrite against are founder inputs, not calculable ones. Everything else is arithmetic once step 2 of the seven-day plan is done.

   *Step 5, the intake rule that falls out of it.* Before signing flat n+1, recompute C(s) including it. If C(s) crosses the cap, either decline, or accept only flats whose headroom raises the portfolio average.

   *Step 6, monitoring.* Monthly per flat: actual owner net year to date against floor-to-date pace, flagged as soon as a flat falls behind. Monthly per portfolio: forecast C at the current run rate.

   **One mitigation that costs nothing and should start immediately: stagger contract anniversaries.** The floor is measured over 12 months from the first guest. If most flats go live in the same season, every measurement window closes at the same time and one bad winter produces simultaneous claims. Spreading go-live dates across the year does not reduce the chance of a bad market, it reduces the chance that every claim lands in the same quarter. **[INFER]** For a company with no capital buffer this is the cheapest correlated-risk control available, and it is free.
3. **Never quote a floor before underwriting.** The floor is a contractual number. It appears at the deck, after the address, the photos, the SVJ check and the blocked-days agreement (Phase C, gate list). Nothing on the public site or in cold outreach should state a number the owner can treat as a promise. **[ANTAM]** the site already says only that a written annual minimum exists for suitable flats; keep it exactly that vague publicly.

## B7.1 The anonymised owner case format

**[DECIDED]** cases can be built from real statements without naming the owner; a name is a bonus, not a requirement.

An anonymous claim is worth nothing unless the reader can see how it could be checked. The format therefore separates identity, which is removed, from verifiability, which is kept.

**Identify the flat, not the person.** Čtvrť, disposition, square metres, floor, and the month it went live. "2+kk, 62 m², Vinohrady, live since 3/2026" is specific enough for a reader to compare against their own flat and anonymous enough to protect the owner.

**Before.** Their actual long-term rent and the energy they were paying, from the lease or their own figures, not from a model. Label it as the owner's figure, not Antam's.

**After.** Real owner net from Antam statements, for the number of months that actually exist. **Never annualise a partial year into a claim.** A four-month case is a four-month case, stated as such, with the season named. **[SOURCE]** [T-CaseSaaS] a lower and precise promise raises belief more than a higher and vaguer one; [Leads: Customer Referrals] lower the promises until close rates drop.

**Evidence.** One real monthly statement, redacted to remove the owner's name, address and account details, with the Antam header, the nights, the platform split, the 30 % line and the totals intact.

**The verification path is the whole point.** State that the owner has agreed to take one phone call from a serious prospect before signing. That converts anonymity from a weakness into a controlled process: public enough to publish, checkable enough to close, and it costs the owner one call a quarter at most. **[SOURCE]** [T-Content ~34:00] evidence beats confidence, and what follows an admitted limitation is believed.

**Label the boundaries.** What is measured, what is estimated, and the plain statement that another flat will do something else. **[ANTAM]** the same rule the calculator already follows.

## J0. What 8 to 10 hours a week actually buys

**[DECIDED]** 8 to 10 hours a week for acquisition. Held against the plan:

| Activity | Weekly hours |
|---|---|
| 100 outreach touches (connects at roughly 7 minutes, non-connects at roughly 1) | 4.0 |
| List building and weekly refresh | 1.0 |
| Follow-up touches | 1.0 |
| Agent calls and visits | 1.5 |
| Underwriting, visits and decks at month-one volume | 2.0 |
| **Total** | **9.5** |

The plan fits, at the top of the range, with nothing spare. Two consequences worth stating now rather than discovering later:

1. **The funnel throttles itself precisely when it starts working.** Underwriting and decks are the variable cost. At roughly eight assessments a month, underwriting passes three hours a week and begins eating outreach time, which reduces the following month's leads. This is the first real automation or delegation trigger in the business and it arrives long before the VA-at-30-properties trigger. When it arrives the fix is a templated assessment, not more hours.
2. **The block is non-negotiable and it is in the morning.** 20 dials before noon, five days a week. **[SOURCE]** [Leads: Advertising in Real Life] the habit is the mechanism, not the intention; [T-Content ~09:00] the gap between working when motivated and working like clockwork is roughly fiftyfold, not double.
