## A1. What business are we really in?

**[ANTAM]** The public offer is 30 % of accommodation revenue, cleaning paid separately by the guest and retained by Antam, energy paid by the owner, a written annual minimum for the flat of at least long-term rent plus energy (Deloitte Rent Index basis, decided 30. 8.), damage cover up to 25 000 Kč/year by flat size, a 25 000 Kč launch fee, 12 months' commitment then indefinite with 4 months' notice.

**[INFER]** You are not selling property management. You are selling **a floor with upside on an asset the owner already owns**, and the competitor is not Ruterra or Seven Keys, it is the owner's long-term lease. The guarantee is not a marketing enhancer bolted onto a service. It is the product. Everything else (listings, pricing, guests, cleaning, reporting) is the machinery that lets you write the floor without going bankrupt.

**[SOURCE]** [Offers p39-40] a Grand Slam Offer is one that cannot be compared; [Offers p49-51] the four market indicators are pain, purchasing power, easy to target, growing; [Offers p156] risk reversal is the single largest conversion lever; [T-OffersLM] a commoditised offer must win on speed, risk or ease, and Antam's chosen vector is unambiguously **risk**.

This framing matters operationally: it means intake discipline is a solvency function, not a marketing nicety, and it means the fee percentage is the wrong thing to argue about (A6, B1).

## A4. Who to deliberately reject

1. Any flat where the SVJ prohibits short-term accommodation or the building is hostile. **[ANTAM]** already your stated rule; make it question two of every conversation, not a late discovery.

3. Owners who want the flat for themselves for much of the year. Every blocked night reduces the floor pro rata (2/365, 3/365 in season) which is correct, but a high-blocking owner is a low-revenue, high-friction unit.

# PHASE B. OFFER

## B1. The 30 % fee: keep it, stop revisiting it

**[ANTAM]** You moved 30 → 28 on 28. 8., reverted to 30 on 30. 8., and reopened 25 vs 30 pending real costs. **[ANTAM]** competitors sit at roughly 20 % + VAT (Blahobyty), ~21 % of gross (Bohemian Estates), 30 % all-in (Investerra).

**Decision: 30 %, final, and remove it from the list of open questions.**

Reasons, in order:
1. You are carrying a long-term-rent-plus-energy floor and damage cover up to 25 000 Kč per flat per year. Nobody at 20 % is carrying that. The fee is not the same product. **[SOURCE]** [Offers p62] compete on price only if your costs are one tenth of competitors'; you have no cost advantage, you have a risk-transfer advantage.
2. **[SOURCE]** [Offers p69] "99 percent of businesses need to raise their prices to grow"; [Offers p67] charge the premium once you have delivered many times. You have delivered; you have not yet proven the operator factor, which is the real condition (B7).
3. Price instability is itself a cost. Changing the fee after launch invalidates every quoted floor and every published example. You said you do not want to change price after launch: then stop moving it before launch.
4. **[SOURCE]** [T-Sales 02:31] never change the price to close, change the terms or the components. Your discount instrument is the launch fee and the feature set, not the percentage.

**What must change is not the number but where it appears in the conversation.** The owner's decision variable is their net crown amount and their floor. The percentage is an accounting detail that you disclose plainly when asked and never lead with. **[SOURCE]** [Offers p39-40] sell in a category of one, avoid the commodity comparison; [T-Moats #7] segment by what the buyer actually values.

## B2. The launch fee: keep, and measure its margin

**[ANTAM]** 25 000 Kč locked 27. 8., plus 20 % project management on build-outs above ~30 000 Kč, materials and trades at cost.

**Decision: keep it, unchanged, and never waive it to rescue a wobbling owner.**

Three reasons the library supports it, one warning:
1. **It qualifies by investment.** **[SOURCE]** [Leads: Affiliates] qualify partners by requiring an investment; not enough starts, lower it; not enough follow-through, raise it. Applied to owners: an owner who will not pay 25 000 Kč to prepare their own asset is the owner who will block the calendar, refuse the photographer and dispute the first statement.
2. **It satisfies the 30-day rule.** **[SOURCE]** [MM: Section I] the bare minimum money model returns more gross profit in the first 30 days than the cost to acquire and serve the customer; [Leads: Paid Ads II] client-financed acquisition. **[INFER]** If your acquisition cost per signed flat stays below the *margin* in the launch fee, you can acquire owners without financing them out of working capital, which is the single most important structural permission a small business can have. It is also the reason you can afford paid acquisition sooner than the "ads last" heuristic implies, once the funnel is measured (Phase H).
3. **It prices the ramp that the notice period is currently trying to protect.** See B3.

**Warning. [UNKNOWN]** What is the actual margin on 25 000 Kč after furniture, styling, photos and deep clean on a typical flat? If it is 3 000 Kč, it is not a money model, it is a pass-through and point 2 above is false. Compute it on the last three onboardings this week.

## B3. The 4-month notice period: cut it to 2 months

**[ANTAM]** 12 months' commitment, then indefinite, 4 months' notice (reduced from 6). Site copy carries it.

**Decision: keep 12 months' initial commitment, cut post-commitment notice to 2 months.**

**[INFER]** You are currently stacking three separate risk transfers onto the owner at the moment of signature: a paid launch fee, a 12-month lock, and a 4-month exit. Each is defensible alone. Together they read as a trap to exactly the cautious, first-time-delegating owner who is your primary ICP, and they sit in the denominator of the value equation, where the library says differentiation is won or lost. **[SOURCE]** [Offers p75-87] time delay and effort/sacrifice are the hardest and most differentiating variables; [MM: Continuity Discount] make cancelling easy to find, and treat harsher terms as an explicit trade of fewer sign-ups for fewer leavers; [MM: Waived Fee] the sanctioned instrument for buying commitment is a fee that disappears when the commitment is met, not a long exit.

The calendar argument for 4 months is real but it is an operational fact, not a contractual necessity: confirmed reservations are honoured through the notice period regardless, which already solves the guest problem. **[ANTAM]** you yourself recorded that the 8-month calendar horizon is operational policy and not a marketing argument or a contract length.

Expected effect: **[UNKNOWN]** but this is cheap to test, because it only changes what you say on calls; measure objection frequency on the first 20 owner conversations before and after.

## B4. The guarantee: right instrument, wrong exposure control

**[ANTAM]** Written annual minimum, at least long-term rent plus energy, Deloitte Rent Index basis, on every flat Antam accepts, live from day one, 12-month measurement window from the first guest, reduced pro rata for owner-blocked nights (2/365, 3/365 in season), shortfall settled from Antam's fee.

**Keep it. It is the strongest thing you have built.** It answers the owner's real alternative in one sentence, it is falsifiable, and it is the reason the 30 % holds.

Three changes:

1. **Condition it on owner actions, explicitly and in writing.** **[SOURCE]** [Leads: Customer Referrals] set guarantee conditions equal to the actions that best customers take; [Offers p157-158] high fulfilment cost means conditional rather than unconditional; [T-Sales 02:02] only guarantee what you control. You control pricing, listing, guests, cleaning. You do not control: availability, the flat's condition, owner-imposed price floors, furnishing decisions, or SVJ intervention. The floor must therefore be conditional on a named list: minimum available nights, agreed fit-out and photo standard delivered by the agreed go-live date, no owner-imposed minimum prices, no mid-term calendar withdrawal beyond the pro-rata rule. This is not weakening the guarantee. It converts it into a behaviour contract that produces the result it promises.
2. **Cap portfolio exposure before scaling.** **[INFER]** All-flats guarantee with no aggregate cap means one bad winter across a cohort of marginal flats hits fee income on every unit at once. Define now: total guaranteed shortfall you can absorb in a year, expressed as a share of annual gross profit, and a hard rule that you stop signing new guaranteed flats when projected exposure crosses it. **[SOURCE]** [MM: Win Your Money Back] the offer is only run where the refund rate is below 5 % and you can stomach the downside; the library never authorises an uncapped promise.
3. **Never quote a floor before underwriting.** The floor is a contractual number. It appears at the deck, after the address, the photos, the SVJ check and the blocked-days agreement (Phase C, gate list). Nothing on the public site or in cold outreach should state a number the owner can treat as a promise. **[ANTAM]** the site already says only that a written annual minimum exists for suitable flats; keep it exactly that vague publicly.

## B5. The lead offer: rename the deliverable, not the tool

**[ANTAM]** the public tool is "kalkulačka"; the submit action is "Poslat byt k výpočtu"; the promise is a property-specific calculation within 24 hours.

**Decision: keep the public tool called a calculator. Rename what the owner receives.**

The tool should keep the commodity name because that is what people search and click; the *thing you deliver* needs a name, because that is what gets remembered, forwarded and asked for by name. **[SOURCE]** [Offers p173-176] M-A-G-I-C naming, use three to five of magnet, avatar, goal, interval, container; [T-LeadMagnets] four working conventions and the instruction to test the packaging harder than the contents; [Leads: Lead Magnets] a lead magnet is a complete solution to a narrow problem that reveals the problem the core offer solves.

Recommended name: **"Posouzení bytu"** (VI: "Đánh giá căn hộ"), delivered in 24 hours, containing a verdict, a number, a range, and the conditions.

Why an assessment beats a calculation: a calculation is a number the owner can dispute; an assessment is a **verdict** the owner is either given or refused, and refusal is what makes acceptance mean something. It is a reveal-a-problem magnet for a problem that worsens every month the flat sits mispriced or empty. **[SOURCE]** [T-LeadMagnets] reveal-a-problem is strongest where the problem worsens with time; [T-Content] damaging admissions.

Test three headlines against each other once traffic exists (Phase I). Do not agonise now: **[SOURCE]** [Leads: Lead Magnets] headline and packaging tests move take rates 2-10×, which is a reason to test, not a reason to deliberate.

# PHASE C. QUALIFICATION

Qualification here is not lead scoring for efficiency. With an all-flats guarantee, **the intake screen is the underwriting desk**. A wrong yes is a direct loss.

## C1. Hard gates, asked before any scoring

Any single failure ends the process regardless of the numbers. Ask these in the first five minutes of the first conversation, not at the deck.

1. Does the SVJ or house rules prohibit short-term accommodation, and is there known neighbour hostility?
2. Is the unit legally usable for accommodation services (unit type, any change-of-use or licensing issue)?
3. Who owns it and who decides: co-owners, joint marital property, a company, an heir dispute?
4. Is there a tenant or lease in place, and when does it actually end?
5. Any mortgage or insurance condition restricting short-term letting?
6. When is the flat physically available to photograph? **[ANTAM]** the go-live clock starts here, agreed at signing.
7. How many nights a year does the owner want for themselves?

**[SOURCE]** [T-Sales 00:41] BANT before the ask; [T-Sales 01:33] kill the disqualified before asking for money; [T-LeadMagnets] free work only for leads that pass budget, authority, need and timing.

## C2. Property score (0-100)

## E4. How the guarantee and the price are introduced

**Sequence, fixed:** the owner's current position → the underwritten range → the floor → what the floor is conditional on → the fee → the launch fee.

# PHASE F. UNDERWRITING AND THE OWNER DECK

## F1. Inputs required before any floor is quoted

Address; exact m² and layout; real sleeps and bed configuration; floor and lift; condition and fit-out gap with a cost; photos or a visit; building and SVJ status; parking, terrace, garden, view, air-conditioning; owner's blocked nights; owner's current rent and energy cost; go-live-ready date; existing furniture inventory.

## F2. Deck structure

**[ANTAM]** the current concept is around nine pages. Nine is fine if every page carries a decision. The test is not length, it is whether a page changes the owner's mind. Recommended structure, seven to nine pages:

1. **Your flat today.** Their current rent, minus energy, minus vacancy, annualised. Their number, not yours.
2. **What comparable flats in your čtvrť actually did.** 12 months to 7/2026, realised, with the measured-versus-derived note stated plainly.
3. **What we think your flat does.** Range, not a point. The assumptions listed, including the ones that could be wrong.
4. **Why this flat specifically.** The address-level factors that moved it up or down from the district figure.
5. **Your money.** Gross, platform fees, the 30 %, cleaning to the guest, energy to you, annual net. Side by side with page 1.
6. **The floor.** The written annual minimum, what it is based on, what it is conditional on, and how blocked nights reduce it.
7. **What we would change in the flat, and what it costs.** The launch fee, itemised, and the build-out if any.
8. **How it runs and what you see.** Go-live date, the 15th-of-month statement, the damage cover.
9. **Next step.** One action, one date.

**[SOURCE]** [Offers p96-116] dream outcome then problems then solutions; [Offers p75-87] the value equation, with pages 6 to 8 attacking risk and effort, which is where this category is won; [T-CaseSaaS] set expectations deliberately low and precise rather than high and vague.

## F3. The public number versus the private number

**[ANTAM]** you deliberately made the public calculator simpler and slightly optimistic to generate leads, recalibrated so the existing portfolio always beats the public number.

**[INFER]** This is a defensible design and a live risk. Every underwritten figure that lands materially below the public figure spends trust at the worst moment.
