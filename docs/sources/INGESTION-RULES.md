# Source ingestion rules (owner's brief, condensed verbatim where it matters)

Phase: SOURCE INGESTION. Do NOT create a strategy for the owner's business, do NOT redesign anything, do NOT jump to recommendations. Do not apply anything to the owner's company (Antam Homes, Prague short-term-rental management). The notes are about what the SOURCE says.

1. Treat the source as authoritative for what the author says. Report the author's claims as the author's claims.
2. Extract: frameworks, principles, decision rules, examples, warnings, equations/formulas, numbers, implementation details (scripts, steps, checklists, sequences, cadences, ratios, thresholds).
3. Preserve distinctions and conditions. No generic marketing advice. If the author says "do X when Y", keep the Y.
4. Separate clearly, with tags on every line or block:
   - [S] direct principle / framework / rule the author states
   - [E] the author's example, case study, story, number from his own businesses or clients
   - [I] my interpretation or inference (use sparingly and label it)
5. Note connections and tensions with the OTHER sources by the same author when you notice them (Offers 2021, Leads 2023, Money Models 2025, and his workshop/YouTube transcripts). Mark as [I] unless the author himself refers to the other book.
6. Dedupe within the source, but keep meaningful variations (e.g. two different versions of a rule with different thresholds).
7. Pay special attention to these 23 topics: offers, value equation, guarantees/risk reversal, pricing, lead magnets, lead generation, paid ads, organic content, outbound, referrals, affiliates/partners, authority and proof, qualification, sales, follow-up, conversion, onboarding, retention, ascension/monetization, unit economics, scaling, moats, measurement and testing.
8. Capture WHEN a tactic is appropriate: business stage, business type (service / product / SaaS / local / B2B / high-ticket / low-ticket), ticket size, margin, capacity, audience warmth, and any explicit "don't do this if…".
9. Don't assume big-company advice applies to early stage. Flag every place the author distinguishes stage (e.g. "under $1M", "first 100 customers", "when you have a team").
10. Do not try to please the owner or validate his decisions. Later he wants to challenge his business using the evidence in these sources, so the notes must be the evidence, precise and citable.

Note format (Markdown, English; keep the author's terms in his words):
- Header block: source id, title, type (book / transcript), year, length, LIMITATIONS (auto-transcript errors, missing sections, images not readable, unclear speaker, etc.).
- Chapter-by-chapter (or segment-by-segment for transcripts) with a location reference the owner can find again (chapter title / section name / page or file name; for transcripts an approximate position such as "~min 12" or a distinctive phrase).
- Every framework as a compact spec: name, definition, components, formula if any, steps, decision rule, conditions/when, warnings, author's examples with numbers.
- End with two indexes:
  (a) TOPIC INDEX: for each of the 23 topics, list the note sections (with location refs) that cover it, or "not covered".
  (b) KEY EQUATIONS / NUMBERS / RULES OF THUMB: a flat list with location refs.
- Do not pad. Do not add advice the source does not contain. Paraphrase; quote only short formula names or definitions (a few words), not passages.
