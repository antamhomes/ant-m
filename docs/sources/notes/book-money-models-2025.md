## Start Here (file 007)

**Story 1 — storage-unit owner, "How much does a free month of storage cost?"**

[E] Author's first gym: ~$5,000 saved, ~1 month of runway; customer acquisition via "$39 six-week special on a discount site" → 4 customers. Storage-unit owner across the street (27 locations, 23 years in the game) shows him the model: "free month of storage" actually yields $127 per customer:
- Lock: $47 (own inventory; "any goon with bolt cutters" reason-why).
- Boxes, tape, labels, markers.
- Moving: affiliate kickback from local moving company; or dollies, hand trucks, straps rented "for a fee".
- Insurance: $500 free for all; bump to $100,000 for +$10/month, only with "the special locks only I offer".
- Unit upsizing: "everyone has way more stuff than they think… we always offer one size up".
[S] Lesson stated by the mentor: "when you figure out how to make money in one business… you see ways to make money in any business."

**Story 2 — $25,000 hour with a famous marketer, 2.5 years later, six gyms**

[E] Author's gym-opening model (numbers as stated):
- $3,000 down for a lease, a few days of ads, sell customers in the empty building; cash from sign-ups funds ads, equipment, paint, flooring, signage → new location every six months, no debt.
- Advertise "Free 6 Week Challenge" until ~20 leads/day.
- ~50% of leads show for appointments; ~50% of shows buy a $600 program → ~25% of leads become customers.
- +$80 profit per customer from supplements → ~$680 per customer "before you even open your doors".
- Cost per lead: $5 → mentor's math: "one dollar in… 34 dollars out… in 48 hours."
- Back end: a few weeks later, $600 back as credit if they sign up for a year; two-thirds of sign-ups convert to memberships → full gym and $20,000 of monthly memberships "for $3,000 down", all within thirty days.
[E] Mentor's verdict: "you have a level-10 skill in a level-2 opportunity… You should be showing other gym owners how to do what you just showed me."
[E] Result: closed newest gym, sold other five over nine months; founded Gym Launch; 30+ in-person turnarounds, then licensing model; "once they filled their gym in thirty days, they told their friends". ## Section I: What's A Money Model? (file 008)

**Endorsement quoted:** [E] Alex Becker (Hyros) — "highest return on advertising of any business using our… platform… by a mile", among businesses spending ≥$250,000/yr on marketing.

**Rental-car story (Dec 2019):** [E] came for a $19/day car, left paying $100/day — "a 5x difference". The company's money model stated plainly:
1. Vehicle upgrade (pick-up truck for "a big guy")
2. Late return
3. Premium insurance
4. Minimum insurance downsell (after he refused premium)
5. Prepaid gas ($3.75/gal vs ~$3.50 local)
[S] Each offer solved a problem he had or would have ("big man in small car", late checkout, dings, missing flight). "They offered solutions for higher fees and hassles I might have had later for smaller fees in total right now."

**Definition:** [S] "A Money Model is a sequence of offers." Core: find every opportunity to solve a customer's problem, then offer to solve it. Money Models "tend to have many offers in a specific order." "If you offer the right thing when customers realize they need it, you can make as many offers as you like."

### Beware: Bad Money Models Kill Businesses

[S] The failure loop as stated: many businesses pay more to get a buyer than they make in profit from the sale → spend on ads → end of month realise spent > made → cut ads → get fewer customers than they can handle → cut ads altogether → float with personal cash/loans/credit, "pray for profit" → sell percentages of business to keep lights on → wait months/years to get money back "if ever" → fall further behind → lose it all.

[S] The "drip" problem: in traditional business "the slow drip of profits from lots of customers eventually pays for a single customer" — this starves cash; they can only advertise for lots of customers if they already have lots of customers. Big companies / small companies with investors can do this because they "have the money to burn".

[S] Worked logic: spend $100 in advertising to get a customer, make $500 profit from them = great deal — "but what if it takes you two years to make your cash back?" Great business only if you already have cash. Two options: (1) wait two years and pray; (2) "get paid fast and grow as much as you darn well please." "A good Money Model is option 2."

### Author Note: Make Enough Profit To Cover Your Costs In 30 Days Or Less

[S] Exactly as stated: "I like to cover my costs of getting a customer within 30 days. Main reason: any business can get interest-free money for 30 days in the form of a credit card. If you clear your balance before the end of the month, it works just like normal money. So you can use credit to get a customer, pay it back, and then use it again to get the next customer… Rinse and repeat."

[I] The book never writes a formal equation with CAC/COGS symbols. The closest formal statements are (verbatim-level paraphrase, see also [Ten Years In Ten Minutes] and [Section VI]):
- Minimum: profit from a customer in first 30 days > cost to get AND service that customer. ("A good Money Model makes more profit from a customer than it costs to get and service them in the first 30 days. That's the bare minimum.")
- $100M standard: profit from ONE customer in first 30 days > cost to get and service MANY customers ("at least two more customers in less than 30 days" per Section VI).
- [Attraction Offers Conclusion]: "enough cash to cover the cost of the customer and the cost to deliver our thing multiple times over."
[I] Connection: this is the same "client-financed acquisition" / 30-day-cash logic he uses in Leads 2023 (paid ads chapter) and his workshops; the Leads version is usually stated as a ratio against CAC. Verify exact threshold wording in the Leads notes when ingested; Money Models does not restate a 2x figure, only "at least two more customers".

### Good Money Models Make Millionaires

[S] Multiplicative claim: make customers twice as valuable × get twice as many × get them at twice the speed → "your business grows 8x faster"; triple each → 27x. (Value × volume × speed.)

[S] Next-up framing: "Different offers solve different problems. So if you want to win, you have to figure out what to offer next."

## The Four Types of Offers That Make Money Models (file 009)

[S] "Making one offer works better than making none. And making more offers works better than making one. Combining offers in a sequence makes a Money Model."

[S] The four types and their jobs:
- **Attraction Offers** turn strangers into customers.
- **Upsell Offers** get people to spend more cash.
- **Downsell Offers** get people to say yes when they would have said no.
- **Continuity Offers** keep people buying.

[S] Can use one, two, multiples of one, or all four. "When I look at my most profitable businesses, I used all four." Reasoning chain: no attraction offer → fewer customers; only one thing → far less money; upsell → "finally get some cash"; many still say no → downsell; want guaranteed monthly cash → continuity "to top it off".

### Important Notes (author's ground rules — warnings)

[S] **All businesses have Money Models.** Replace "this won't work for my business" with "how will I make this work for my business?"
[S] **Some models work better in some businesses.** Copying "what they do" disappoints; you must design your own.
[S] **If a customer asks for their money back — give it back.** "Avoid the headache… Next time, spend the time and resources to get better customers."
[S] **Hard selling is for weak products.** Don't convince against their will; make offers available at the time the customer has the problem. "It's a numbers game."
[S] **Obey the law.** Plays learned in different places/times/platforms; advertising laws change and "tend to only get tighter — especially when it comes to 'free'." Check with lawyers. "This book is intended to be Money Model inspiration."
[S] **Be transparent.** "If the facts aren't compelling, change reality to make them compelling or learn to frame them in a way that is. Don't lie." Bad reputation cannot be erased like credit-card debt.
[S] **Any offer can be used on its own, at any time, in any order.** Most offers could meet the minimum "make a profit" requirement alone; in the right sequence they make a $100M Money Model.

[S] **First up: Attraction Offers** — "Most businesses spend too much to get customers and make too little from them. They are cash-constrained… So I always solve this first with an Attraction Offer."

## Section II: Attraction Offers — intro (file 010)

[S] Attraction Offers "generate leads and convert them into customers. They turn advertising into money by offering something free or at a discount." Rationale: strangers can only take your word on value, but "absolutely understand the price" → discounts make anything a great deal to almost anyone; the greater the discount the better; "the greatest discount of all being free."

[S] **Free / discount / $1 continuum:** anywhere the author says "free" you can substitute "discount" or "$1" and vice versa — "they all discount a product to some level — even if you discount it by 100%."

[S] How free makes money: "people look for one thing and then buy another by accident all the time. Attraction Offers get them to do it on purpose." One free thing is good, two free things better — "and, maybe to get those two free things, they have to buy one."

[S] The five attraction offers covered: Win Your Money Back, Giveaways, Decoy Offer, Buy X Get Y Free, Pay Less Now or Pay More Later. (The "Free Goodwill Offer" chapter in the Contents is a review request, not a sixth model — see below.)

## Win Your Money Back (files 011–012)

**Tagline:** [S] "If you do x within y time within z rules, you can get it free."

**Origin story (June 2013):** [E] Gym owner "Danny" — a hard-to-close prospect proposed: pay $500, train 8 weeks, hit goal → money back; in return gym can market his results. He hit the goal, then used the refund to buy more training; marketing his before/after brought 13 referrals. Danny then offered it to everyone: better results, "people love the offer", free advertising from customers. [E] Author adopted it for private training, group training, private and group nutrition coaching; then put it in ads for new customers → "cost of getting customers went way down and my leads exploded."

**Definition:** [S] Set a goal for the customer and tell them how to reach it. If they reach it, they qualify to get their money back as cash or store credit. [E] "This offer grew my gyms better than any other" and was "the first Grand Slam Offer that Gym Launch taught to gym owners." [I] "Grand Slam Offer" is the Offers-2021 term; the author uses it here without redefining it.

**Three criteria options:** [S]
1. **Results** — whatever they do, if they get the result (make $X/month, get Y customers, lose Z lbs) they win. They bet on their own ability.
2. **Actions** — whatever the result, if they do what you ask (attend all sessions/calls/meetings, log progress, take pictures, homework) they win. They bet on following directions.
3. **Actions AND Results** — must do both. Rationale: people wanting a goal often lack skills; a good goal + directions gives "a fighting chance".
[S] Both results and actions must be simple to track.

**Examples (author's templates):** [E]
- B2C "Free 28-Day Blueprint": deposit $X, get it all back if you attend all consulting calls, post progress in the group weekly, journal daily in app, attend feedback session and transformation session. (Hint: "calls and meetings become opportunities to make more offers.")
- B2B "5 Customers In 5 Days Free Challenge": deposit $X, back if you send 100 messages/day, report stats, attend daily training, post finished homework, attend day-5 consulting call. (Hint: offer more/better/new products there.)
- Physical product "Put 1,000,000 miles on your car, get a free car": buy new car, drive 1,000,000 miles, turn it in, photos + press release → full original price credited toward next car. "(This was an actual offer.)"

**Important notes:**
- [E] "This offer has generated over $1 billion in sales industry-wide."
- [S] Works with **new, current and previous** customers: new (steepest discount possible — 100%), current (mixes them with new), previous (bigger incentives bring them back).
- [S] Works well with stuff people **start and quit**: starting businesses, learning skills, weight loss, fitness, beauty, self-care, time management, mental health. "It keeps motivation during the early pains of learning."
- [S] **It makes money because:** (1) many won't qualify even with realistic conditions; (2) those who qualify often stay — but only if they have something else to buy → "have an upsell ready to apply their winnings (Section III)."
- [S] **Only offer it if you are OK giving money back.** [E] Data "from thousands of gyms": about **10% of all customers will ask for their money back.** "If you can't stomach it, don't do this."
- [S] **Store credit vs cash:** [E] testing showed store credit and cash back got "the same number of customers" → offer store credit. If you still want to advertise it as "free", pair with an **unconditional satisfaction guarantee**; adding it "never materially affected the number of people who wanted their money back." Check legal counsel.
- [S] **Don't take blood money:** if a customer asks for a refund, entitled or not, give it.
- [S] **Criteria design — three characteristics:** (1) **Easy to track** (bonus if already tracked automatically: phone steps, word count, dated photos); (2) **Gets customers results** — realistic criteria; "if you think the criteria look too easy, you've probably gotten close to realistic"; make everyone do what the best customers do; (3) **Advertises the business** — posting participation, tagging, referring, reviews/testimonials as part of criteria.
- [S] **How to apply store credit [IMPORTANT]:** apply winnings over a longer period or to a bulk package costing more than the winnings. [E] Worked example: product costs $200/month; customer wins $600 credit; do NOT give three free months; apply $600 over 12 months → $50/mo discount → they pay $150/month. Reason: "people fall off if they don't pay something"; keeps skin in the game. Customer may still use credit however they want; present this first. Cross-ref Rollover Upsell.
- [S] **All meetings/calls = offer opportunities.** Make check-ins part of the criteria and required. [E] Author's gym sequence: Nutrition Orientation → before pictures → supplement offer; Progress Check-in → membership offer; Transformation Feedback → after pictures → membership offer again; if they bought membership at last meeting → discount if prepay a year.
- [S] **Make everyone a winner:** sell as if credit only on meeting criteria, but "about halfway through" make the next offer as if they already won (script: credit this program toward the next one "whether you hit the short-term goal or not"). Lowers anxiety, keeps them longer.
- [S] **Let the "losers" win at the end:** if they refused the first upsell and failed, act like they won ("you met ours — which was finishing what you started"), credit entire deposit toward staying long-term. "We don't get customers to make a sale, we make sales to get customers."

**Summary points adding to the above (file 012):** [S]
- "Magical" for businesses requiring continuous customer effort. Benefits listed: up-front cash; more yeses (lower risk); results; long-term customers; they advertise for you; meetings-as-terms = offer moments.
- "Everyone thinks businesses make money on people who fail the program. No. The real money comes from people who succeed with it and you have something else to offer them."
- **Threshold:** "Only use a Win Your Money Back Offer if your refund rate is below 5%. Otherwise, fix your product before doing this." [I] Two distinct numbers: 5% = your existing refund rate as a precondition; 10% = observed money-back requests once you run this offer.
- Make everyone a winner "in private".

[I] Connection to Offers 2021: this is a conditional guarantee ("performance"/"effort" conditions) turned into the front-end offer itself; the criteria doubling as retention and referral mechanisms echo Leads 2023 referral logic.

## Giveaways (file 013)

**Tagline:** [S] "Many will enter… many will win."

**Legal disclaimer (author):** [S] Sweepstakes/giveaways are "heavily regulated… when done wrong, can become illegal lotteries… Jail time." Follow local advertising law; the chapter is no guarantee of lawfulness.

**Origin (Aug 2020):** [E] Fitness-certification business advertises a "full-ride scholarship" to its program; applicants give contact info and answer "Why should we pick you?"; one wins the full-ride; everyone else is called and told they got a partial scholarship — "most of them join our program on the spot." Applicants don't know the actual price; the scholarship value anchors it. They "have to cap it to make sure we can service all the new sign-ups." They teach the same play to certified trainers for getting fitness clients.

**Definition:** [S] Advertise a chance to win a big prize in exchange for contact info (and anything else you want); after picking a winner, offer everyone else the big prize at a discounted price. Also called scholarship, sweepstakes, raffle.

**Steps:** [S]
1. **Pick a Grand Prize** = the thing you want everyone to buy; assign a monetary value as price anchor ("if you sell $5,000 worth of value for $2,000, then advertise the $5,000 value!").
2. **Pick your promotional offer** (= the "partial scholarship"): core offer enhanced with a discount, bonus, or minor change from the Grand Prize "in order to ethically justify a price reduction". Bigger discount = more compelling; the bigger the assigned Grand Prize value, the better. Name it whatever: scholarship, gift card, dollars off, store credit, voucher.
3. **Ask for contact information** + permission to contact for follow-up promotions; survey for **eligibility** ("Do you own a vet clinic?", "Why should you be selected?") and set **qualifying actions** (attend call/event, make a post, join group) — used to get promotion and show interest.
4. **Deadline for urgency:** author likes **3–7 days** from start of promotion. Update entrants **daily** across email/DM/text/social (once a day across all platforms); provide value + countdown + social proof. "Keep the hype alive!" Pro tip: treat the countdown as a mini product launch ("Whisper–Tease–Shout"); [S] author refers to the Affiliates and Partners chapter of $100M Leads for launch detail.
5. **Announce winner publicly, contact everyone else privately** by text/email/DM; ask them to book a call because "they qualified for something else"; reason-why: their answers/story were compelling. Promotional offer = "participation trophy". Add a **second deadline: claim within seven days**, with the same countdown/value/social-proof cadence and a call-booking path.

**Discount sizing rule:** [S] "make your core offer discount equal to 10%–30% of your gross margins." [E] Worked math: Grand Prize valued $5,000, retail $2,000; everyone else gets it for $1,800 (10% off retail); present as "$5,000 in value for $1,800" → a 10% discount becomes "a 64% difference in cost-to-value".

**Examples:** [E]
- Dentist "Free Perfect Smile": Grand Prize invisible braces, $6,000 retail; promo $2,000 gift card for braces.
- Physical product "Free Year of Organic Dog Food": Grand Prize $1,000 retail; promo $300 gift card usable only with a one-year subscription.
- Services "Free Ultimate Giveaway": Grand Prize 1-year package $5,000; promo $2,000 voucher toward 1-year service agreement.
- Consulting "Free 16-Week Turnaround": Grand Prize $12,000; promo $6,000 partial scholarship.

**Important notes:**
- [S] **Legal structure no-brainers:** somebody actually has to win the Grand Prize; Grand Prize and qualifications clear in rules; make clear more than one person can win a prize. Ask counsel about the rest.
- [S] **Eligibility questions = sales ammo:** "Why should we pick you? Why this program? Why now? Why does this matter to you? What's your goal?" Trade-off: more work to enter → fewer entrants but more qualified; "find your sweet spot."
- [S] **If it doesn't work, the Grand Prize wasn't grand enough.** [E] Portfolio company gave away event tickets → barely any interest; retried with a $50,000 equipment bundle from a well-known industry supplier + their core product for a year → "crushed".
- [S] **Two prizes for twice the leads (referral mechanic):** if someone you refer wins, you win too → "endless entries" via referrals; referrers stay invested in referrals' success → quality stays high. [E] Done for Skool.com (image not in text).
- [S] **Scarcity:** limit by time (e.g. seven days), entries (e.g. 5,000), or both — author likes both. Match entry cap to "the number of people I have the time and resources to connect with inside seven days."
- [S] **Urgency in three places:** to enter, to claim, to use. Schedule claim calls same day or next day; usage window "I like hours, but I have gone up to five days."
- [S] **Have downsells available:** at call start say they qualified for **two** prizes; present promotional offer (discount on Grand Prize thing) first; if no, "offer the same discount by percentage on any other product you have that makes sense for them."
- [S] **Recurring-revenue businesses:** apply the discount "over the longest period of time they'll agree to", then bill automatically at normal rates after the discounted period.

**Summary points (new items only):** [S] Advertise seven days OR until leads exceed what you can call in seven days, whichever first; entry information "becomes important for making offers later."

[I] Connection: Leads 2023 treats lead magnets as free value that reveals a problem; here the giveaway is a lead magnet whose entry form doubles as qualification. Tension: prospects "don't know the actual price" until the call, alongside the "Be Transparent" ground rule — the author's answer is that the discount is real and the anchor value must be believable.

## Decoy Offer (file 014)

**Tagline:** [S] "Which one do you think will get you the best results?"

**Origin (June 2014, mentor "John"):** [E] "The 5-Day $5 VIP Tanning Pass": everyone thinks they can get tan in five days; they can't without burning ("the turkey talk" — double the oven temperature and you burn the turkey; 5–10 sessions with rest between = always more than five days). Then: "Let's just credit your VIP pass toward your first month. Why buy so many $25 day passes when members get unlimited access for just $19.99?"
[E] Five years later, Gym Launch fitness leads got "way too expensive"; most gyms barely breaking even. New offer: two options — free (one session/week) vs "Ultimate" $399 (unlimited sessions, 1-1 coaching, personalised, guarantee: results or repeat free). **Take rate: ~8 of 10 chose the $399 option.**
[S] John's principles: "giving customers what they want now, so you can give them what they need later"; "You have to know what gets results for customers better than they do. This makes our premium offer the clear solution."

**Definition:** [S] Advertise something free or discounted (the decoy). When leads ask to learn more, also present a more valuable premium offer (more features, benefits, bonuses, guarantees) side by side. Either outcome is fine: premium = great; decoy = "gives you time to upgrade them rather than losing them… you can close everyone." "Any business can use it."

**Steps:** [S] (1) Advertise a lesser/smaller/simpler version of your premium offer as the decoy; (2) when leads engage, offer both options but emphasise the premium.

**Examples:** [E]
- Lemonade stand: "Free Week of Lemonade" or "$1 Week"; decoy = water + powder + corn syrup; premium = organic imported Italian lemons, delivered, extra flavours.
- Float tank centre: "Free 6-Week Stress Release" or "$6"; decoy = one float/month + DIY exercises; premium = 2 floats/week for 6 weeks, 1-1 consulting, journal, sleep routine, satisfaction guaranteed.
- Gym: "Free 21-Day Transformation" or "$21"; decoy = daily workouts in a Skool.com group, general nutrition plan, recordings, no support, no guarantee; premium = unlimited workouts, personalised nutrition, 1-1 accountability, results guaranteed or another 21 days free.

**Important notes:**
- [S] **Build the decoy:** fewer components, older models, less personalised; **remove any guarantees**. "Your Attraction Offer only has to get leads to engage. Nothing more."
- [S] **Advertise benefits, not features:** sell the dream outcome ("transformation in 21 days", not workouts/meal plans); product details belong in the sales presentation, not the ad.
- [S] **Four ways to state the same discount** (year-long thing at $100/mo sold for $900): Percentage off (25% off); Absolute amount ($300 off); Free portion (3 months free); Total package ("One Year For $900 ($1,200)"). "Worth testing to see which one converts better in your market."
- [S] **Make the contrast huge:** decoy as basic as reasonable, premium as awesome as possible.
- [S] **Discount offers have higher show-up rates than free offers:** free → more leads; discount → fewer leads but higher % show. "If you have low show-up rates… try a Discount Offer" — especially where no-shows are costly (doctors, lawyers, dentists).
- [S] **Present premium first if possible;** keep decoy "in your back pocket".
- [S] **Permission script** (if they ask for the decoy, or you are legally required to present it): "Are you here for free stuff or lasting results?" → "results" → skip to premium. If "free stuff" → present decoy, immediately contrast with premium, then ask "which do you think will get you to your goal faster?" or "which would you prefer: XXX less valuable benefit or YYY more valuable benefit 1, 2, 3…?"
- [S] **Get excited about the premium**; use an "assumed close" — "This is what everyone does. This is just a formality. Let me get your ID and credit card… No hype… Almost bored over how regularly people buy."
- [S] **Surprise benefit (optional):** if they take the decoy, throw in a few low/zero-cost premium features — "they're still leads!"

**Summary (new items):** [S] Decoy-takers teach you "the best way to deliver your decoy product and maximize upsells from it." "Expect to make money fast. If you're not, then make the contrast between offers larger."

[I] Connection: the decoy/premium contrast is the Offers-2021 value-equation logic expressed as two side-by-side offers; "results or repeat free" is the Offers-2021 conditional guarantee.

## Buy X Get Y Free (file 015)

**Tagline:** [S] "Buy one puppy, get two puppies free!"

**Origin (Nashville 2020):** [E] Boot Factory sign "BUY 1 PAIR GET TWO PAIR FREE"; a single pair marked down twice to a "final offer" of $600 — i.e. they charge 3x the price of one pair because it comes with two more. Store had grown; bachelorette parties buying matching boots; tourists making one purchase ever.

**Definition:** [S] When customers buy something, they get other stuff free. More free stuff and higher value → works better. Free offers get "way more attention" than discount offers, but if you only have one thing and give it away "you go hungry" → businesses fall back on seasonal "sales". By selling more than one thing at once you can turn a discount into a stronger free offer.

**Math as stated:** [E]
- Reframe (no discount): 3 t-shirts at $10 = $30 ↔ 1 t-shirt for $30 + 2 free. Same price, "way more free stuff".
- With a discount: 3 t-shirts at $6.67 = $20 (33% off) ↔ 1 t-shirt for $20 + 2 free.
- Boot Factory: one pair $200 → "Buy one pair for $600, get two pairs free" → they still buy three $200 pairs for $600.
- **Three versions of 18 months of service, all $1,800:** Good "Buy 12 Get 6 Free"; Better "Buy 9 Get 9 Free"; Best "Buy 6 Get 12 Free". Same price, same service; third is most compelling ("most free stuff").

**Important notes:**
- [E] Author's service businesses used to take a whole year to make their money; "Buy 6 Months Get 6 Months Free" attracted far more customers than month-to-month and paid up front.
- [S] **Raise prices before giving stuff away to preserve profits.** "Permanently raise prices to accommodate the discount. Don't lie. Actually raise your prices." Since all new customers come in on this, change it "for a season at least"; some will even pay the doubled a-la-carte price.
- [S] **More free than paid:** "buy two get ten free" beats "buy ten get two free"; "Buy one get two" instead of "buy two get one".
- [S] **Free things can differ from paid things:** e.g. buy a $10 shirt, get $20 of socks free.
- [S] **More cheaper free things can beat fewer expensive free things:** test "Buy 1 Shirt Get 1 Shirt Free" vs "Buy 1 Shirt, Get 3 Socks Free" (same cost to you).
- [S] **Rather than 33% off, try Buy One Get Two Free** ($10 shirts at $6.67 vs "buy one for $20 get two free"); "More people know the value of free than the value of one shirt." Test it.
- [S] **WARNING — do not make these offers if you can't manage money.** A year's payments arriving in a month must fund a year of delivery; "Don't be a goon and buy yourself a house with the cash meant for servicing your customers. Selling stuff you can't deliver on breaks the law and ruins your reputation."
- [S] **Fast cash from existing customers:** in a recurring business needing cash, offer existing customers "Buy Ten and Get Two Free" at current price — **limit to 10% of your customers** to keep recurring cash flow healthy.
- [S] **Prepaid customers keep buying — keep selling to them.** "These are people who spend the most money"; their wallets "refresh" after months.
- [S] **If customers only buy once, make them buy big** (Boot Factory tourists) — "provide the value to back it up."

**Summary (new items):** [S] Works for stuff that makes sense to buy more of or get longer access to; lengthens customer stay ("if normal customers stay for three months, then 'Buy 2 Get 2 Free' will keep them for four months" → more offer opportunities).

[I] The 10% cap for cash-pop offers to existing customers is a specific threshold not in the earlier books.

## Pay Less Now or Pay More Later (file 016)

**Tagline:** [S] "Time is money." (Franklin)

**Origin (June 2016):** [E] "Double your reading speed in 3 hours, or it's free." Registration options: (a) card down for $0, billed $297 tomorrow unless you email to cancel — but "you must attend in order to be eligible"; (b) pay $97 now and get the recordings as a free bonus. Author chose (a); speed doubled in ~2 hours; presenter earned $297; then pitched an eight-week program (author declined).

**Definition:** [S] Give people the choice to pay full price later OR a discounted price now. Removes all risk (pay later, only if satisfied) = delayed payment + satisfaction guarantee. "Anyone can sell this." Once they agree to pay later, get them to pay now with hefty discounts and bonuses. Pay-later lets you advertise "free" and gets **card on file**; if they hate it they cancel before the charge. **Pay-now options: 20–50% discount plus greater bonuses.** Card already on file makes paying now easy. To take full advantage, have something else to sell (upsell) afterwards.

**Examples:** [E]
- Real estate "Free 3-Day Workshop": Pay Later $0, billed $500 at end unless cancelled; Pay Now $299 + recordings + 1-1 call with distressed-property expert + printed materials; Upsell $30,000 to close first deal within six months (legal templates, advisor, inspection checklist).
- Local service "Trim Your Hedges For Free": Pay Later $0 lawn cut + hedges, then $599; Pay Now $369 lawn + hedges + lawn treatment; Upsell $199/month lawncare. Rep makes estimate, offers both, upsells after the work.
- Physical product "14-Day Clothing Trial": Pay Later $0 now, billed $149 in 14 days (must return in like-new condition before billing to qualify for guarantee); Pay Now $97 clothing + matching accessory; Upsell monthly clothing subscription.

**Important notes:**
- [S] **Promise a clear yes/no result** deliverable within the time frame; measure before/after (e.g. shoulder pain rated 1–10 before and after). Simple, clear, measurable → fewer cancellations.
- [S] **Conditional satisfaction guarantee:** can only cancel billing if they qualify (attendance, appointment, turning in data). "They can't say you suck if they never try it." Make criteria what people do to get the most value.
- [S] Bonuses for pay-now: author refers to the bonuses chapter in $100M Offers (acquisition.com/training/offers).
- [S] **Optimisation rule:** too many take pay-later → discount pay-now more, add better bonuses, or both; too many take pay-now → do the opposite.
- [S] **Threshold: if >10% of pay-later people cancel their payment** → you promised too much, guarantee conditions too low, or price too high. Some will always cancel — factor into cost of doing business.
- [S] **Recurring version:** pay a higher ongoing rate starting 30 days later, or pay less today and keep the lower rate for good, plus bonuses.
- [S] **Events/workshops: hint at the next offer early** ("Everyone wants to know when my next intensive starts… I'll get to it at the end"). [E] Nutrition consults: "everyone wants to know what supplements to buy. We'll get there" — implied everyone buys, head nods confirmed demand, more bought later.

**Summary (new items):** [S] Offer pay-now AFTER they accept pay-later; pay-now buyers get discount/bonuses **instead of** the guarantee; give extra attention to those claiming non-delivery before the cancellation deadline. Author's positioning: does "exceptionally well with digital products and short duration services"; easy to teach salespeople.

## Section III: Upsell Offers — intro (file 019)

[S] Upsell = "whatever we offer next." "When an offer solves a problem, another appears. You upsell the solution to the problem your offer reveals." Every offer opens the door to an upsell — "even upsells!" "Often, upsells make the majority of the profit."

**Burger math:** [E] $2.00 burger, $0.25 profit → would need ~10,000 burgers/day to "barely eek out a living". Fries +$0.75 profit; "make it a meal" (drink) +$1.75 → profit $0.25 → $2.00 = 8x; supersize "for just a buck more" → $3.00 = 11.6x (author's figure; [I] arithmetically 12x). "If McDonald's didn't upsell fries and soda, there wouldn't be a McDonald's."
[S] Lesson: "the thing you sell the most isn't always the thing you make the most profit on." Find your version of "Do you want fries with that?"

[S] **Upsells fail when:** (1) you offer something they don't want (too different / doesn't solve their problem); (2) wrong time (before they've experienced the problem); (3) wrong way (they don't believe you); or combination.

[S] **Upsells offer:** more of what they got (quantity), better versions (quality), new/complementary stuff (different).

[S] Four upsells: Classic, Menu, Anchor, Rollover. "Warning: This section is brutally effective and must be used ethically."

## The Classic Upsell (file 020)

**Tagline:** [S] "You Can't Have X Without Y!"

**Origin (summer 2016):** [E] Fourth-generation fur-coat dealer (childhood mentor). Restaurant salmon: ~$3 cost, $32 menu price — "but we pay it." Son's play: advertise free earmuffs with coat storage; when customers collect muffs and store coats: "And we'll store those as well for $30. You don't want to store anything else do you?" → customers pay $30 to store a free gift by saying "no".

**Definition:** [S] Offer a solution to the customer's next problem the moment they become aware of it. "Extremely profitable, easy, and anyone can do it" because current customers always have a higher chance of buying than strangers; timed right, "customers upsell themselves." Relies on knowing more about the customer's problem than they do. Core offer solves one problem and creates another → "You can't have X without Y" (car without insurance, gas, late checkout). "If a problem appears, and you can solve it immediately — in exchange for money — do it!"

**Examples:** [E] Car wash → sealant; Bicycle → helmet, lights, puncture-resistant tires; Exercise course → nutrition course ("You can't out-exercise a bad diet").

**Important notes / tactics:**
- [S] **Actually do it.** [E] Businesses that only sell one thing: "You barely have a business — you have a front end." Those that added an upsell "actually 5x'd their business."
- [S] **Offer more profitable upsells first.**
- [S] **"Say no to say yes":** "You don't want anything [besides what I just offered] do you?" — trained "no" becomes a yes.
- [S] **Surprise and delight with bonuses:** if you hold four bonuses for fence-sitters, add one at a time; if they say yes early, still give all four — everyone gets the same thing.
- [S] **Hyper-buying cycle:** when people decide to do something new (weddings, new hobbies, babies, moving) they spend a lot in a short window — "don't shy away from Upsell Offers."
- [S] **Use free bonuses to create problems upsells solve** (the earmuffs cost materials/labour but generated $30 storage).
- [S] **Faster access = more value:** "A $10,000 thing you get later is worth less than a $10,000 thing you get now." Put it in their hands before they say yes — "It's way harder to give something back than it is to say no."
- [S] **Bundle and name upsells:** one ask, nine sales; name by customer type/result ("Fastest results", "Transformation package", "Minimum package"); you can "peel" items out later as a downsell (Section IV).
- [S] **Integrate upsells into delivery:** [E] meal plans included optional supplement suggestions → people asked about supplements; Gym Launch training suggested optional software → owners bought it.
- [S] **BAMFAM — Book A Meeting From A Meeting.** End every appointment by scheduling the next. Quote attributed to "Sharran" (public-company CEO friend): "A customer should know the next time they see you — and why — before they leave."
- [S] **Upsell as many times as makes sense.** "The second worst thing that happens is they say no. The worst thing is if they would've said yes but you never asked."
- [S] **How to upsell more of the same thing (three-option pricing, movie-theatre example):**
  - To push Large: Small $5, Medium $8 ("rather than the rational price of $7"), Large $9 → medium buyers move to large.
  - To push Medium: Small $6 ("rather than $5"), Medium $7, Large $9 → small buyers move to medium.
  - Rule: "If you have a lot of customers buying small, bump them to mediums. If… medium, bump them to large. If… large, raise all your prices."
- [S] **Upsell guarantees, warranties, insurance:** instead of free, "add 5–50% onto the price in exchange for a guarantee." [E] Art studio replaced damaged portraits free; started asking for +10% → now 30% of customers pay for what used to be free — "pure profit".

**Summary:** [S] "Use The Classic Upsell for immediate problems revealed by your previous offer"; "You lose nothing by offering to solve someone's problem."

[I] Tension with Offers 2021: there guarantees are primarily a free risk-reversal to lift conversion; here the author also treats them as a paid upsell (5–50% uplift) and later as a removable feature (Feature Downsells). Both positions co-exist; the condition is whether the business "already gives it away".

## Menu Upsell (file 021)

**Tagline:** [S] "You don't need that… you need this."

**Origin stories:** [E]
- Dec 2013: $5,000 of supplement inventory not selling despite full shelves and "science" pitches; 19 consults, zero sales; on the 20th he asked "do you like chocolate or vanilla?" → sale; "kiwi or strawberry lemonade?" → sale; "You just wanna use the card we have on file?" → yes. Next 20 customers in a row bought. → **A/B Upsell** and **Card On File**.
- Aug 2014: $5,000–$10,000 of supplements per monthly challenge group. Wrote step-by-step dosing instructions on the order form and asked "use the card on file?" → bought everything unprompted. → **Prescription Upselling**; "my 30-day profits skyrocketed."
- Nov 2016: 40–50 consults/day, two every 30 min, 12 hours; supplement sales "covered the flight there, my hotel and advertising costs." Out of stock → told a customer to buy a cheaper substitute ($20 less) down the street, crossed out weight-gainer and testosterone products she didn't need, prescribed the rest → "bought without hesitation." → **Unselling**; "Later, I kept products just to cross them out!"

**Definition:** [S] Tell customers which options they don't need, then what they do need, their preferences, and how to get value. Up to four tactics in order:
1. **Unselling** — tell them what they don't need to emphasise what they do; cross out options.
2. **Prescription Upsell** — tell them what they need; works when offering a choice is inconvenient and only one thing solves the problem. Two components: explain how it integrates with what they already bought; personalise and detail how to maximise value. "Explain how to use it as if they already have." Removes the option of not buying.
3. **A/B Upsell** — ask preference between two things that solve the same problem; "either choice results in an upsell."
4. **Card On File** — "Do you want to use the card on file?" Lowers hidden costs of buying (choosing a card, taking it out, being reminded of past purchases, rush). Pro tip for first purchases: "What card do you wanna use?"
[E] "This took me ten years to learn."

**Examples:** [E]
- Massage therapist: Unsell lymphatic massage ("you're not pregnant or just out of surgery right?"); Prescribe heat → trigger points → dynamic stretches; A/B "before work or on the way home?"; Card on file.
- Dog food: Unsell small bag, puppy food, vitamins (already in the food); Prescribe joint chews each meal, heartworm wafer every 90 days, book next month now; A/B beef or chicken; Card on file.
- Digital product: Unsell "You don't need all eight courses yet… I'll send you some free stuff for X and Y… you'll just need one course for Z"; Prescribe "an hour a day"; A/B DM or phone support, start today or Monday; Card on file.

**Important notes:**
- [S] **Make anything A/B sellable:** quantity, start dates, payment preference, flavours, time slots, media, delivery speed, sizes, colours, materials, personnel, communication channel.
- [S] **Add a nudge to A/B** for inexperienced customers: "This is my favorite", "X is usually a safe bet", "Tuesday's sessions are a little smaller"; nudge the product you want to move.
- [S] **Sold out? Take payment, delay delivery** — sell, then order; lets you offer more selection without inventory.
- [S] **Employees love unselling** — let them help customers "game the system" on purpose.
- [S] **The Economist play (three options, sell both):** [E] late-1990s: A Digital $59/yr, B Digital+Print $125/yr → print sales plummeted. Fix: A Digital $59, B Print $125, C Digital+Print $125 → customers took C. Rule: "Present three options… make the price of (C) the same as the more expensive option (B)… so long as you price the options to preserve your margins."

**Summary (new items):** [S] "Menu Upsells work best when you have multiple offers available"; "Unselling lower-margin stuff where appropriate incentivizes higher-margin upsells."

## Anchor Upsell (files 022–023)

**Tagline:** [S] "The only thing worse than making a $1,000 offer to a person with a $100 budget… is making a $100 offer to someone with a $1,000 budget." (In the first you lose $100; in the second, $900.)

**Origin (2016, early Gym Launch):** [E] Budgeted $500 for a suit; first suit $16,000 → blush ("The Gasp"); owner: "Do you care about the designer much?" → "Not at all" → second suit $2,200 (already pulled — "he already had the 1/8th price suit pulled before my reaction"); plus socks, handkerchief, shirt ~$300. Spent 5x his budget and "felt OK about it."

**Definition:** [S] Offer premium stuff first; if the customer gasps, offer a cheaper but acceptable alternative. Present a premium version **5x–10x the price** first; most say no; the main offer then "looks like a much better deal." Works best when the lower-price offer has the **same core functions** as the premium. Two bonuses: anchored customers spend more than they normally would; some still buy the super-expensive thing.

**Steps:** [S] (1) Present the Anchor; (2) Get "The Gasp"; (3) Come to the rescue — ask if they care about what makes it premium; (4) Present main offer — relief, better deal; (5) Ask how they want to pay — "Which card do you prefer?"

[S] Always have premium upsells ready: "Only a handful of customers buy them, but that handful… bring big profits… you won't lose customers by offering premium stuff first, but you WILL lose money if you don't."

**Examples:** [E]
- Lawn care: Premium $1,000/week (owner's cell, fancy mulch, natural pest control, bi-weekly maintenance) vs Main $200/week (team's number, generic mulch, normal pest control, bi-weekly).
- Painting: Premium $1,000 (protective packaging + 20-yr insurance + gift wrap) vs Main $200 (normal packaging + 1-yr insurance + sticker).
- Newsletter: Premium $199/mo (all past issues + new + 24h early) vs Main $19/mo (new issues, on time).

**Important notes:**
- [S] **If you treat the anchor like a fake, so will the customer.** Actually sell it; only move on after they pause, hesitate, or ask for something else.
- [S] **Make a premium offer you actually want people to buy.** [E] A friend's anchor was "some BS he didn't really want them to buy"; fixed → tripled his profits.
- [S] "The bigger the gasp, the more they bought."
- [S] If no gasp, they find the premium reasonable → "ask them if they wanna use the card on file."
- [S] **Main offer = same primary features, different secondary features.** "Offering the primary features for a fifth of the price makes the main offer a great deal."

**Summary (new items):** [S] "Expensive premium offers add outsized profits with fewer sales"; anchored customers spend "a bit more than they plan to."

[I] Connection: Offers-2021 price anchoring turned into a two-step sales sequence, with the concrete 5–10x multiplier and "1/5 price" main offer added.

## Rollover Upsell (file 024)

**Tagline:** [S] "Wanna just roll it forward?"

**Origin (June 2014):** [E] Author's $600 Win Your Money Back program "crushed" but he had no recurring revenue: winners put $600 toward three months of membership and churned before the first out-of-pocket payment — effectively "buy six weeks get three months free". Revenue "started at zero every month." Friend Justin added 100 members with the same attraction offer; difference: "I just roll over their winnings into a year long membership… fifty bucks off per month for a year… They still get their money back… it just takes a year." "The missing link in my Money Model." Author: "changed my life, thousands of gym owners' lives".

**Definition:** [S] Credit some or all of a customer's previous purchases toward your next offer; "gets way more people to take it." Decide: **who** to upsell, **what** to upsell, **how** to roll credit. You don't need a Win Your Money Back offer to use it — "You can Rollover Upsell anyone anything. (Even stuff people bought at other businesses.)"

**Who (four situations):** [S] (1) re-engage customers who left a while ago; (2) rescue upset customers instead of refunding; (3) "rescue" other people's upset customers; (4) upsell regular customers.
**What:** [S] more, better, or new/different — "Roll their credit over to something more expensive."
**How:** [S] all or part of the discount, up front or spread over time.

**Examples:** [E]
- Chiropractor "Winback" campaign: who = 6+ months since last purchase; what = new plan; how = up front. Script: "I wanted to give you your money back… $500 of your money back as credit towards staying pain-free for good."
- Dentist: upset $200 cleaning customer whose teeth aren't whiter → credit $200 toward a whitening package (multiple sessions, at-home kit, deep cleanings); front-loaded credit.
- Software: competitors' upset customers (from negative reviews) → credit remaining payments owed to competitor toward a longer agreement with you. Script: "I'll credit whatever payments you have left with them to switch to ours."
- Membership: customer buys a small block → immediately offer to apply the entire amount toward 12 months as a spread discount: **$600 first purchase → $50/month off for 12 months.** Author prefers to do it "right then".

**Important notes:**
- [S] **As an attraction offer:** roll over what customers paid someone else; find leads by scraping negative product reviews; or "create a way for people to complain about products in your industry… Then, Rollover Upsell all of them."
- [S] **Rollover before refunding:** roll over for a "do over" or toward something different.
- [S] **Previous customers are still customers.** [E] Winback: personalised videos to 200 past customers offering $4,000 credit to return; ~20% took it; "One day of recording videos got us an extra ~$1,900,000 of annual revenue."
- [S] **Urgency: one-time only** — "the moment you present the offer [is] the time to take it… If not, no big deal. They can still pay full price later."
- [S] **Pricing rule:** upsell offer **at least 4x the rollover credit** → even full credit discounts at most 25%. "Bigger discounts make you less profit per sale, but they get more sales."
- [S] **Partial credit is fine** — roll over whatever incentivises the next purchase; test.
- [S] **"Famous" Gift Card Play:** advertise gift cards at 90%+ off (e.g. $200 gift card for $20); limit two per customer; usable only on other people → bought as gifts, holiday offer. Ask who it's for and for an introduction. When the recipient comes in, roll the card over. Rule: **gift-card value = 20% of the price of what you sell next** ($200 card → offer with at least a $1,000 price tag). "People pay you to refer their friends." Plus breakage from unused cards.

## Section IV: Downsell Offers — intro and The Rules of Downselling (file 026)

[S] "Downselling tweaks the original offer to find the highest value solution for the customer's budget. So any offer you make after someone says 'no' is a downsell." Two levers: **how they pay** (balance pay-now vs pay-over-time) and **what they get** (quantity, quality, or something different).

**How NOT to downsell (friend's car-insurance story):** [E] Salesman offered insurance at $5,000; on "no", kept lowering to $400 for the same insurance; she refused because she no longer trusted him — and then didn't want the car either. [S] Lowering price on the same thing "trade[s] trust for a buck." Note: "You can offer something different for less. You just can't offer the same thing for less."

**The Rules of Downselling:** [S]
1. **They said no to this offer, not all offers.** "No means no for this thing, not no for everything."
2. **Downsells are trades.** "If you're gonna give something, get something."
3. **Personalize, don't pressure.** Offer more of what they like, less of what they don't, price to match (large soda refused → small, juice, or coffee). "If I can better serve them, it would be offensive not to."
4. **Offer the same things in new ways.** Don't create "a hundred businesses' worth of products"; think "a hundred ways to offer the stuff you already have."
5. **Don't drop your price just to get somebody to buy.** That's discounting, not downselling. "If someone wants what you have, and just doesn't want to pay the price — tough cookies." Payment plan is allowed.
6. **Customers talk about price.** Test prices deliberately (specific price, specific number of people, planned ahead) — not in-the-moment fear discounts; it's also "an ethical problem".

[S] Three downsell processes: Payment Plan Downsells (how they pay), Trial With Penalty (how they pay), Feature Downsells (what they get).

## Payment Plan Downsells (file 027)

**Tagline:** [S] "How much can you put down today?"

**Origin (Aug 2013):** [E] First real month in business, one month's rent left; lead said "I can't afford it"; "OK, when do you get paid?" → "The first." Offered half now/half then, then thirds — she could do nothing today but could pay in full on the first; rent due the fifth → charged card on the second. "My first ever payment plan."

[S] Payment plans "work no matter how many zeroes the price tag has"; [E] "tens of millions of dollars with them." **They are a gamble:** win one way (more customers who complete payments); lose two ways (people cancel before you profit; **worst: people who would have paid in full take a plan and cancel early**).

**Definition:** [S] Downsell by offering the same product again, charging some up front and the rest in scheduled payments. Key insight: "'it costs too much' really means 'this costs too much up front'." Discounts work because "they pay less in the moment", not because the total is lower. Payment plans get more buyers (less now) AND keep full price over time.

**The seven-step process (stop when they buy; shifts from most up-front to most over time):** [S]
1. **Reward paying in full rather than punish paying over time.** Present the price with "interest" included, then offer prepayment as a discount: not "$10 now or $15 over time", but "It's $15… but it's $10 if you prepay it. You save $5… that's what most people do." Same math, friendlier, plus a price anchor.
2. **3rd-party financing, credit card, layaway.** Financing: another company pays you now (car-dealer model; "takes work to get… set up. But, totally worth the effort"). Credit card reframe: "would you rather I decide your payment terms or you decide?" → they decide → use a credit card ("It's wild to me that this reframe works. But, it does"). Layaway: pay in instalments, get product only after paid in full — most flexible for them, lowest risk for you.
3. **Half now, half later.** "When's the next time you get paid?" → "put half down today and the rest… when you get paid?" → if not, "What's the most you can put down today?" → "we'll put that down today and put the rest when you get paid. Fair enough?" Schedule off paychecks (most people paid every two weeks) — "boosts 30-day profit way more than monthly payments."
4. **Check they still want it.** "On a scale from 1–10 how bad do you wanna do this?" **8+** → "We're gonna figure out a way to make this happen for you", keep going. **7 or below** → "Why not a 10?" then "we may have something that could be a better fit" → Feature Downsells.
5. **Three payments:** ⅓ now + ⅓ on next two paychecks, or ⅓ now + ⅓ next two months.
6. **Evenly spread payments** over the rest of service. [E] Gym Launch was 16 weeks → charged weekly, 16 times.
7. **Free Trial** (the special Trial With Penalty — next chapter). "The sale ends here. At least for now."
[S] "This Payment Plan Downsell process makes up to nine offers."

**Important notes:**
- [S] **"Seesaw" downselling** (fewer steps / less experienced salespeople): "Would you rather have giant monthly payments or tiny ones?" → tiny → "It normally costs XXX. And if you prepay it today, you get a huge discount and zero monthly payments. That work?" If they can't: "We'll simply adjust the down payment until you get a monthly rate you like." If still no, ask if they still want it; if yes, "pull your chair to their side of the table" and walk through options together.
- [S] **Payment plans have built-in upsells:** periodically offer the original paid-in-full discount if they pay off the balance; customers forget; some jump. Give salespeople the same bonus for closing the balance. "If you give people the option to pay slower, they will pay slower. If you incentivize them to pay faster, they will pay faster."
- [S] **Fewer declined payments:** align charges to paycheck days; if declined, run the card a few times that day (paychecks deposit at different times). [E] Learned from John; "I often recoup a third of my declined payments."
- [S] **Diagnostic — how to make sure plans make money:** close rate should rise, but **if the number of paid-in-fulls goes down, you have a problem** — you moved would-be prepayers onto plans. Target: more closes overall, same % paying in full. [E] 10 leads → 3 sales; with downsell → +3 = 6; up-front cash from the first three plus payments from the next three.
- [E] **Profitwell churn data (14,000 businesses) — billing cadence vs monthly churn:** Monthly billing → 10.7% monthly cancellation; Quarterly → 5%; Annual → 2%. [S] "So start high (fewer bigger payments) and work your way down" — presenting most-cash-up-front first also makes customers more valuable long-term.

**Summary (new items):** [S] "Payment Plans only grow your business if they get more customers and those customers actually pay"; step 7 = "Offer a Free Trial in exchange for putting a card down."

## Trial With Penalty (files 028–029)

**Tagline:** [S] "If you do X, Y, Z, I'll let you start for free."

**Origin (spring 2018):** [E] Gym Launch at 100+ employees; Leila chose an HR software that "looked like all the others" because of a trial with a spin: do their training → free onboarding; skip the training → pay for it. She did the training, learned the complicated software, and "now I don't want to learn anyone else's complicated software… so we're sticking with them!" [S] That company used it as an attraction offer; the author prefers to **downsell** trials — only after they say no to the first offer — and structured so it "only changes what they pay today — not how much they pay in total."

**Definition:** [S] Customers try the product/service free as long as they meet your terms; **they only pay if they don't meet the terms.** Compare Win Your Money Back (get money back if they meet terms). Terms should mirror WYMB actions/results — things that "make excellent customers" — using fee-avoidance instead of winnings as the incentive. "It's 'here's my thing, you get it for free so long as you do this stuff… which makes you a perfect fit for my next offer. And if you don't, then you have to pay for it.'"

**Math as stated:** [E] Normally close 3 of 10 on the attraction offer; downsell another 4 onto Trial With Penalty; after the trial, upsell 3 of them → 3 sales become 6 — "doubling your customers! If you only have one offer, you lose everyone who says no." [E] "I'm still irritated at the thousands of customers I've lost on free trials over the years before learning this."

**Examples:** [E]
- B2C "28 Day Kick-That-Habit Blueprint": to avoid fee — attend all consulting calls; post progress weekly; journal daily in app; attend feedback and transformation sessions ("upsell opportunities").
- B2B "5-Day Get Your First 5 Customers Challenge": 100 outbound messages/day; report stats; attend daily training; post daily after homework; attend graduation call.
- Software: "$500 Onboarding for HR Software, then $99 Per Month" — skip the $500 if you attend onboarding (three 60-min Zoom calls = upsell opportunities), do homework, activate employer profile, get employees set up by end of third call; otherwise pay the fee.

**Important notes:**
- [S] **What they get free / what they must do:** the free part is either your bare-bones offer (like the Decoy) or your WYMB offer; "giving more rather than giving less — if you can afford it." Criteria should activate and retain; swipe from WYMB.
- [S] **Broken-up fees vs one lump fee:** $500 product with ten tasks → "I'd rather bill $50 for each mess up than one $500 fee on their first mess up"; but if one miss ruins success, make the fee reflect that. Both work.
- [S] **How to downsell the trial** — five-step graphic missing (see LIMITATIONS). [I] Reconstructed from the surrounding text: (1) offer the trial last; (2) always get a credit card + ID; (3) always sell "staying and paying"; (4) explain the fees after getting the card; (5) make check-ins required.
- [S] **Offer the trial last.** Script: "How about we just get ya started for free… if you like it, you can stay. Let me get your ID and we can get the process started — fair enough?"
- [S] **Always get a credit card.** Hold the ID, "What card do you wanna use?" If they balk: "That's just how we've always done it." If they still refuse, show them out. Pro tip: "If someone doesn't agree to put their card down and do the work, I won't sell them. They complain more and they convert less."
- [S] **Always sell staying and paying:** "If this program got you the result, will you stay long-term?" If no, "there's no point in giving them a trial." Frame as if they'll stay; script for hesitation: "I don't want you to try it. I want you to get results… You are not going to hit your long-term goals during this trial. But, you will establish habits… if you wanna get to your long-term results, you're gonna have to stay on after… I can't ethically promise you [a quick fix]."
- [S] **Explain fees AFTER getting the card** ("this is just how we've always done it" attitude) — explaining before "will get more resistance." People still must agree; author has customers **initial separately next to fee clauses** so salespeople are forced to explain them. Script: "We will do our part so long as you do yours… if you miss or skip any stuff, your results will suffer. We charge to keep you on track… If you follow through, you get all this for free."
- [S] **Make check-ins required** and tie each to a benefit ("the first we do X so that you can [benefit one]…"); "we charge if you miss these because it's the only way we can get you results."
- [S] **How to upsell from a trial (three outcomes):**
  - **Like it:** already on auto-billing; meet anyway; offer longer term and/or higher-value version — successful customers get more value from better, more profitable stuff.
  - **Hate it:** ask what should have been different; agree; "Only one person can be angry — and it needs to be you"; ask for a chance to make it up; position them as a better fit for the higher-level thing; offer it. [E] "I can get about half of these people to buy."
  - **Didn't use it:** reach out multiple times first; require a meeting; offer to waive the fee if they attend; get them back on track or offer something better. Author dislikes billing non-starters: "A small fee isn't worth a 1-star review."
- [S] **Tuning:** nobody takes the trial → lower requirements or penalties; take it but don't follow through → explain how fees help them, make sales meetings mandatory; don't stay on the back end → sell the value of staying, deliver better, ensure back-end offer matches front-end; "If you start printing money, don't stop."
- [S] **Let people make up for goofs** once; if they miss again, "you're justified in billing them."
- [S] **Just call it a "Free Trial."** If asked why it works this way: "This is just how we've always done it" / "People get the best results this way."
- [S] **When to use which:** Pay Less Now or Pay More Later = downsell for **physical products or one-time services**; Trial With Penalty = downsell for **recurring products or services**. "I have only made this work in businesses where the customer has to do work to get results."
- [S] **Discounts get cards on file:** a super-low price justifies asking for a card and proves the card works — "first month for $1" then $X/month, instead of a free month.

**Summary (new items):** [S] "Trials With Penalties get more paying customers than normal free trials because they use your product more"; author prefers broken-up fees; "You make money by getting people results and turning them into customers, not nickeling and diming people with fees."

## Feature Downsells (files 030–031)

**Tagline:** [S] "Why don't we try this instead?"

**Origin (2019):** [E] High-ticket seller tripled close rate 25% → 75% — not via payment plans ("take too long") or discounts ("devalue my product") — by cutting a feature: the full money-back guarantee. Script: "If you don't want the option to get your money back, you can pay less. Or, you can keep your money-back guarantee — which would you prefer?" Many then keep the guarantee. Numbers: before, 100 calls → 25 buy full price; after, **35 buy the main thing + 40 take the downsell** (75 total). "It upped your full-price buyers, total close rate, and up front cash."

**Definition:** [S] Lower prices by changing what customers get: lesser quantity, lower quality, lower-price alternatives, or cutting optional components. Every feature has a price and a value; removing lowers both. **Deal logic:** remove stuff they hate + lower price a lot → better deal (they take the downsell); remove stuff they love + lower price a little → worse deal (they take the original). "People will see the value in the thing you removed after they see the difference in price" → clever downselling gets customers to "re-upsell themselves." Therefore **remove features from highest to lowest value.** Formula: "take something away, lower the price, and… ask 'how about now?'"

**Examples:** [E]
- Product quantity: three-month supply → one month. Service quantity: four sessions/month → two.
- Product quality: leather → vinyl. Service quality: 5-minute → overnight response.
- **Service-quality feature list** (also works to *increase* quality): time availability (specific times vs anytime); days (Mon/Wed/Fri vs all); times of day (9–5 vs 24h); amount of time (15-min vs 60-min calls); location (one vs all); cancellations (reschedule fees vs free); speed of response; speed of delivery (queue vs priority; same-day vs next week); service ratio (1:1 vs 1:many vs many:1); communication method (text/chat/video); provider qualifications (owner vs long-time vs new employee); live vs recorded; in-person vs remote; DIY/DWY/DFY; expirations (forever vs X time); personalisation; insurance/guarantee — length (1 year vs life), coverage (specific vs any), terms (unconditional vs conditional).
- Removing an entire feature: drop calls, keep chat + email.
- **DFY → DIY product downsell** if all service downsells refused: chiropractor → home tools/foam rollers/mats; painter → paint + lease a spray machine daily; Alex Hormozi → "Instead of me and my team buying your company… why don't you just attend a workshop?"

**Important notes:**
- [S] **Never negotiate the price.** "People who demand to pay less for the same thing are business terrorists. I don't negotiate with terrorists." Pay less now → payment plan; pay less overall → feature downsell; never "just because".
- [S] **Stay a helpful guide** — collaborative, not competitive; pushy offers "exhaust customers faster"; a guide can downsell "as many offers as necessary".
- [S] **Standardise:** as you learn what the same customer type values, pre-plan feature combinations; "Feature Downsells close more people when you know what feature combinations to present ahead of time."
- [S] **Author's standard sequence:** first cut something valuable and lower the price a little (to make them reconsider the original); if that fails, keep removing and lowering until they buy — "I'd rather people get something rather than nothing."
- [S] **Name combinations:** most expensive after an aspirational status ("The Whale Package", "Total Transformation", "High Roller"; airline First → Business → Economy). Cheapest = **"The Minimum"** ("implies they have to get at least that thing"); closing line: "so nothing more than the minimum package then?" (no-to-say-yes).
- [S] **Temperature check after two downsells:** "On a scale from 1–10 how bad do you want this?" 8+ → payment-plan downselling; 7 or below → "What would a 10 look like?" → recombine features. "You can alternate between payment plan and feature downsells. When you use both, you become very difficult to refuse."
- [S] **After each downsell ask "Deal?" or "Fair enough?"** — few will say "No that's not fair." Author points to podcast The Game, Episode 202, "How to close everyone: downselling like a pro."
- [S] **Free orientations boost DIY downsells:** after all DFY refused — "How about you just come to a free orientation on X tomorrow?"; at the end offer a DIY product solving the same problem. [E] Fitness refusers: ~half showed to orientation; "almost all of them bought supplements."
- [S] **Feature-downsell your guarantees** — removing it makes people realise its value; often flips no → yes.
- [S] **Feature-downsell current customers:** customers who use all features they pay for stay longer; when someone isn't using a feature, offer a lower price for only what they use — they either keep and re-engage, or are happy with a better deal. [E] "Customers who we've downsold into a lower package just for them have the second highest value of all my customers."
- [S] **Barter with reviews, testimonials, referrals:** [E] script "I'll knock $100 off if you: 1) Leave me a review on all review sites 2) Leave me a video testimonial 3) Make a public social post at the beginning, middle, and end… 4) Introduce me to two friends… Deal?" — advertising worth more than $100 to him; $100 worth more than the advertising to them.

**Summary (file 031, new items):** [S] "The first downsell gets them to reconsider my first offer. The rest of my downsells get them to consider the best deal for them"; if they like a feature combination but not the price → payment plan downselling.

## Section V: Continuity Offers — intro (file 033)

**Epigraph:** [S] "You can shear a sheep for a lifetime, but you can only skin it once." — John.

[E] Author "a continuity guy" through fitness, gyms, licensing, supplements, software, Acquisition.com.

[S] **Definition:** Continuity Offers "provide ongoing value that customers make ongoing payments for — until they cancel." Boost profit per customer and "give you one last thing to sell"; "you sell once, but get paid again and again."

**Math as stated:** [E] Offer a $1,000 thing to 100 people, 10 buy → $10,000 now, $0 over time. Offer the same thing at $50/month → 40 of 100 buy; keep them 20 months → still $1,000 each → **$2,000 now and $40,000 over time.** Plus 4x the customers to upsell later.
[S] **Pros/cons:** continuity attracts more customers than an expensive one-off but "you make way less money now" → "tough to use as an Attraction Offer on its own… Continuity Attraction Offers leave you strapped for cash today." Hence the author makes continuity **last**: cash today from Attraction/Upsell/Downsell; a little today and lots tomorrow from Continuity. But: continuity can be used anywhere (attract, upsell, downsell, re-engage).

[S] **What fits continuity:** not a one-day workshop "forever" (that's a payment plan until cost is covered); but "you probably make a mistake to offer a single price (even a big price) to provide a service forever. If your customers get ongoing value, it probably makes sense for them to make ongoing payments."

[S] Three continuity offers combine bonuses, discounts, and fees: Continuity Bonus Offers; Continuity Discount Offers; Waived Fee Offer.

## Continuity Bonus Offers (files 034–035; file 035 opens with the "Digital Product Offer" example)

**Tagline:** [S] "If you like this, you're gonna love what I have next…"

**Origin (fall 2019):** [E] A formerly struggling gym outperformed top Gym Launch performers. Instead of selling the advertised six-week challenge, after the pitch: "want to get it for free?" → free if they become a member, plus member-exclusive bonuses (better class times, tanning booth, VIP events); then immediately "wanna save even more money?" → prepaid discount + bonuses for six months. **Numbers:** before — 34 of 100 signed up for the challenge, half (17) converted to stay a few weeks later; after — ~15 take the challenge, **40 go straight into continuity**, and ~8 of those 40 take the six-month prepay. "Tripled membership sales… still get up front cash from challenges… stack even more up front cash from prepaid memberships."

**Definition:** [S] Give the customer "an awesome thing if they sign up today. Typically, the bonus itself has more value than the first continuity payment." Bonus = adding value (products: many small things or one big complementary product; services: a defined program, onboarding, setup, or feature). Discount = lowering cost — "anything you offer for free you can also offer as a discount"; use both. Add urgency ("if they join now"). Bonus can be standalone-purchasable or continuity-only.

[S] **How the author keeps 30-day profit while using continuity:** first do the big-cash Attraction, Upsell, Downsell offers; continuity brings the first month's payment; then offer month-one buyers a discount to prepay more months → more 30-day cash for ads plus stacked recurring revenue.

[S] **Author Note (stage/structure):** "No successful continuity businesses I've seen has a standalone membership offer. They all have other bells and whistles to upsell." Continuity is hard to advertise profitably ("Nobody wants to make a recurring commitment to something they haven't tried") → attract with trials etc., then upsell features and prepay options.

**Examples:** [E]
- Pet food: one-time bonus — every dog toy ever made ($800 value) with monthly food at $59/month; monthly bonus — a new toy each month.
- Service "Short-Term Accelerator": costs $1,000 alone; free with $100/month membership; VIP community perks (first-in-line events, longer support hours, better reps).
- Digital product (newsletter): all past 40 newsletters "valued at $15,880" for $399/mo after a 30-day free trial; pay today → lifetime discount to $299/mo + early digital access + physical copy monthly. Note: "Use the elements from the Feature Downsell chapter to create better bonuses."

**Important notes:**
- [S] **Focus on the bonus, not the membership:** advertise "get this free valuable thing", explain the rest after interest.
- [S] **Bonuses work like upsells:** more of the same (two years of past newsletters); complementary (nutrition with fitness); upgrade (free gold when buying bronze, limited).
- [S] **Keep bonuses related to the core offer** — a free t-shirt attracts the wrong customers for tech services but the right ones for t-shirt printing.
- [S] **Make bonuses things you already have and do** (past newsletters; onboarding you must do anyway — "slap a price on it").
- [S] **Cross-media bonuses:** physical bonus on digital product (hat, shirt, tool); digital bonus on physical/service (live-stream classes for a boxing gym). "Often lowers the cost of getting a customer more than the cost of the bonus"; even if some "take the bonus and run", lower ad cost can compensate. "If customers are too expensive, give it a try."
- [S] **Realistic bonus pricing:** made-up values "won't anchor the customer and you'll lose trust"; use products you've actually sold at those prices.
- [S] **Titles as bonuses:** silver/gold/diamond after 3/6/12 months; [E] a friend's customers "cared more about the title than any other bonus" and introduced themselves by it.
- [S] **Free bonus ↔ steep discount are interchangeable:** "Become a member for $200 then you get this $1,000 program as a free bonus" vs "Get the $1,000 program for $1 if you become a member for $200."
- [S] **Anchor the bonuses in the pitch:** sell the bonus's benefits → anchor its high value (shock is OK) → "Do you want to know how you can get this for free?" → "Become a VIP member today and you'll get it all as a free gift for joining. Or, you can just buy it for $XXX — which would you prefer?" Then stack: "on top of that… amazing thing 1, 2, 3" with individual dollar values.
- [S] **Force continuity** by making bonuses available only to members.
- [S] **Pricing continuity vs up-front cash (author's tested table, $199/mo membership baseline):**
  - 50% choose continuity when standalone = **1.33x** → $399 standalone ($266/mo equivalent) vs $199/mo
  - 60% → **1.66x** → $499 ($333/mo)
  - 70% → **2x** → $599 ($399/mo)
  - 80% → **2.33x** → $699 ($466/mo)
  - 90% → **2.66x** → $799 ($532/mo)
  "The exact numbers matter less than the principle": smaller standalone premium → more standalone buyers; larger → more continuity. [I] The "$/mo equivalent" figures are the author's; note $399 ≈ 2x $199, so the "1.33x" label refers to a different base than the printed monthly price — treat the table as directional, as the author himself says.
- [S] **Want more up-front cash:** make bonus-only a single payment 1.33x–2.66x the first month of continuity+bonus. "People pay 33% more to avoid continuity… even if you charge 33% more for a one-time purchase, half will buy it!"
- [S] **Even more cash — bulk prepaid discounts:** e.g. "buy five months get one free" — "Only one out of every eight people has to take the upsell to raise 30-day profits by 50%!" Laws of discounting apply.
- [S] **Want commitments — trade bonuses for them:** bonus only with 3-6-12+ month commitment; you lose month-to-month sign-ups who wanted the bonus → fewer sales, more committed customers.

**Summary (new items):** [S] "If you use Continuity as an attraction offer, advertise what you give away, not what you sell"; "To discount — take away the cost of actual products, services, and features you sell."

## Continuity Discount Offers (file 036)

**Tagline:** [S] "If you sign up today, you get X time free."

**Origin (spring 2018, Austin neighbour, "Trash"):** [E] With a truck and a credit card, offered big apartment complexes a whole year of trash service free if they contracted the next five years paid. "Toughest thing I ever did… No one would invest"; after the one-year mark "the cash came floodin'"; sold the business "for a pretty penny."

[S] Relationship: "Buy X Get Y Free done continuity-style", with enough continuity-specific differences for its own chapter.

**Definition:** [S] Give products/services free if the customer commits to buying more over time. Common in internet, pool cleaning, gyms, landscaping, "anything rentable." Two things to know: how you apply the discount (four ways) and your cancellation policy.

**Four ways to apply the discount:** [S]
1. **Up front** — free time first, "official" term starts after. Best where contracts are historically enforced (cell phones, storage, real estate, equipment, anything with collateral). Warnings: skip if you have historically high churn; "this does not get customers profitably. It gets customers, but delays cash."
2. **At the end** — bonus time equal to the discount if every payment is on time; "They earn their free time."
3. **Spread over time** — [E] three months free on a one-year commitment at $200/mo = $600 → $50/month off for 12 months; can add: keep the discount for life if all payments on time.
4. **After the first 1–2 payments** — collect some cash first (covers advertising and some delivery); present as "first and last month", "last month up front", or an activation fee before the bonus; also verifies a valid payment method.

**Important notes:**
- [S] **"Highest Value Per Word Note In This Book" — bill in weeks, not months:** 12 months vs 13 four-week cycles = 8.3% difference. "$100 every four weeks" vs "$100 a month" → same number buy, 8.3% more annual revenue; at 20% margins that "skyrockets annual profit by 41%"; "literally made me millions in pure profit."
- [S] **Extend the term, don't eat into it:** "three months free with a year" = pay 12, get 3 free (15 months), not pay 9 get 3. Start with the extended version, then Feature Downsell a shorter one.
- [S] **+3% for five extra words:** "it's $X plus a 3% processing fee." Never lost a buyer to it; in a 10% profit business, +3% topline = +30% profit.
- [S] **Two forms of payment:** recurring businesses lose "mountains of cash" to expired/changed cards and insufficient funds. Ask: "Do you want to save the processing fee?… Give us a second form of payment in case anything happens to the first one." Reason-why if asked: it costs man-hours to chase payment info; savings passed on.
- [S] **Get ACH** as the second form if you can — cheapest besides cash.
- [S] **Gift cards:** give the discounted time as a physical gift card usable after the first ~three payments; giftable to a friend → lead magnet; many forget → full-priced sign-up.
- [S] **Lifetime discount at your most common churn point:** advertise a lifetime lower rate earned by staying past month X, where X = month your average customer drops off; remind them as it approaches. [E] Rice company: one-time price; 5% off subscription; 15% off if you stay five straight months — "just beyond where most people canceled."

**CANCELLATIONS:**
- [S] **Decide the policy ahead of time** (30/60 days' notice, fees, cancel anytime). Author's favourite: **cancellation fee = the discount they received by committing** ("if they got $600 in discounts… they can pay $600 whenever they want to cancel") — puts them back at the month-to-month rate.
- [S] **Make sure customers know how to cancel:** no internal complaint channel → external complaints, vanishing, 1-star reviews; a clear contact path gives "a real chance to save it."
- [S] **Exit interview:** let them vent; "get more angry about the problem than them"; solve solvable problems; rollover-upsell into higher service if they wanted more ([E] "I've had many people buy a lower cost offer and then complain because they wanted the higher cost features… they buy").
- [S] **Cancellation fee as leverage for feedback:** "I'll waive your cancellation fee if you come in and tell me what I could do better." [E] "I routinely save a third of customers that agree to exit interviews."

**Summary (new items):** [S] "Frontloaded discounts convert more customers, but may have higher churn. Backloading discounts converts fewer customers, but they lower churn. Spreading the discount keeps cash flowing while providing the full discount." "Light cancellation terms get more people to sign up but more people leave. Harsher terms get fewer sign-ups but fewer leave."

## Waived Fee Offer (file 037)

**Tagline:** [S] "You can sign up month-to-month with a setup fee, or I'll waive it if you commit to a year."

**Origin (Jan 2021):** [E] A "legend of high-ticket sales" with zero employees: two options — month-to-month with a big setup fee, or commit a year and the fee is waived; "I make the fee huge, so buyers commit to avoid it"; customers initial that quitting early means paying the waived fee. Once past the early period "it costs about the same to cancel as it does to stick it out. So, they just stick it out."

**Definition:** [S] Ask for a startup fee to join month-to-month — **typically 3–5x the monthly rate** — then offer to waive the entire fee for a longer commitment; cancel inside the term → pay the fee. Risk logic: month-to-month = more risk for you, mitigated by the fee; commitment = more risk for them, mitigated by waiving it; early quitters pay "as if they had chosen 'month-to-month' from the beginning." "Customers will stay longer if leaving costs more than staying."

**Example:** [E] 12-month commitment; $1,000/month; fee $5,000. Option A: $5,000 + $1,000 first month, then $1,000/mo, cancel anytime. Option B: waive $5,000 with 12-month commitment; $1,000/mo; pay $5,000 only if you break early.

**Important notes:**
- [S] Fees get them to start (avoiding a fee is immediate value) and to stick (the cost to quit exceeds the cost to stay).
- [S] **Presenting the fee:** "It costs us money to get you started. If you only wanna test us out, you cover those costs. If you commit longer, I'll cover them."
- [S] **Threshold: if more than 5% want to cancel early, look into it** — "nudge them, not handcuff people into paying for something they hate."
- [S] **Want more up-front cash → smaller fee (1.5–3x monthly)**: more choose month-to-month and pay it. Larger fee → more commitments.
- [S] **Drop the fee after the commitment is fulfilled** — free cancellation is earned; "It doesn't stick forever."
- [S] **Best for commitments of one year or longer**, and for services that take long to work (SEO, investing, weight loss) — "keeps people committed when they get emotional."
- [S] **Cancellation fee for a cause** (optional): donate the setup fee to a cause the customer hates if they cancel early — two reasons to stay.

**Summary (new item):** [S] "At minimum, the commitment length should be a year."

## Section VI: Make Your Money Model — "How To Take Over Your Entire Market" (file 039)

### Gym Launch's money model, in the order it was built (worked example with numbers)

[E] Stage-by-stage history (author says it "only looks clean looking back"):
1. **Decoy Offer (attraction):** lots of free courses/books/video/live training on growing a gym, each with a free call. On the call: Decoy = "you do it on your own for free" vs Premium = "we help you implement… for $16,000 over 16 weeks." → **$476,000/month within three months.**

3. **Still some nos →** a more personalised **Menu Upsell** with service levels: done-for-you advertising; sales-team training; turnkey cash campaigns; and a **minimum package** = continuous access to original materials + tech support at a discounted monthly rate; **Feature Downsells** to find the best option. 4. **Prestige Labs** (supplements) integrated with Gym Launch — a separate business with its own money model → **$4,400,000/month by month 20.**

### Definition and the three stages

[S] "A Money Model is a deliberate sequence of offers. It's what you offer, when you offer, and how you offer it to make as much money as you can as fast as you can. Ideally, to make enough money from one customer to get and service at least two more customers in less than 30 days."

[S] Three stages:
- **Stage I: Get Cash** — Attraction Offers get more customers for less.
- **Stage II: Get More Cash** — Upsell & Downsell Offers make more money from them faster.
- **Stage III: Get The Most Cash** — Continuity Offers maximise total money spent.

[S] **Stage warning (explicit):** "if you try to start a bootstrapped business, from zero, on your own, with a 'finished' Money Model… it will collapse on top of you. In fact, none of my businesses started with a fully forged Money Model. They all start at Stage I. Even Acquisition.com!"

[S] **Evolution sequence:** (1) get customers reliably → (2) make sure they pay for themselves reliably → (3) make sure they pay for other customers reliably → (4) maximise each customer's long-term value → (5) spend as many advertising dollars as possible. "Each stage pays for the next… reliability" = financial AND operational. "Fair warning: when your Money Model starts working, your business starts breaking" → find someone to build and lead the team ("When I did, I married her").

[S] Author Note: "Lots of $100M Money Models exist… I just show the ways I have actually done it."

### Example Money Models (four archetypes)

### Make Your Own Money Model — the four steps

[S] **Step 1) Start with an Attraction Offer.** Goal: strangers → customers and cover costs. Choose what to sell, the best way to present it (WYMB, Giveaway, Decoy, BXGY, Pay Less Now), advertise. "Figuring out what works best may take up to a year." For advertising, see $100M Leads.
[S] **Step 2) Pick an Upsell Offer.** Goal: 30-day profits "well above" cost of getting a customer + delivering. Match the upsell to the problem your attraction offer creates; make the offer "at their time of greatest need."
[S] **Step 3) Pick a Downsell Offer.** Goal: turn nos into yeses → more cash from the same leads. Keep price the same → change how they pay (Payment Plan, Trial); charge less → change what they get (Feature). Alternate within one sale. "The more flexible you make your offers, the more people will buy them."
[S] **Step 4) Pick a Continuity Offer.** Goal: "one last sale in our 30-day window and stack recurring cash." Sometimes the right timing is after the first thirty days — "better to make the offer at the right time than… force it at the wrong time."

[S] **Author Note — Bootstrapped businesses must get customers at a profit:** without outside investors, a starting fortune, or "an endless source of free customers", "achieving a Money Model is the only way you can profitably scale. Otherwise, you run out of cash and go out of business before you even have a chance."

### Important Notes (build rules)

- [S] **Perfect one offer at a time.** "Stick to your stage. Pick one offer. Try it. Keep doing it until it works reliably. Then… do it so many times it gets automatic. Then, go to the next stage."
- [S] **Measure in quarters, not weeks.** "You either build it right or you build it again."
- [S] **Raise price in stages:** new offers cheap at first; as yeses come, raise price; "keep raising the price until you cannot make up for the nos with the extra cash you make from the yeses… until you make less money."
- [S] **Simple scales. Fancy fails.** "It's less about having 100 products to offer, and more about having 100 ways to offer your product." [E] Personal training → 1, 2, 3, 4 sessions/week = many offers from one product.
- [S] ***IMPORTANT* Affiliate products can fill Money Model gaps.** Sell other people's stuff for commission — with no offer (start a business), with one offer (add offers), or at $100M (more money without operational headache). [E] Dental agency → refers dentist clients to a braces manufacturer for commissions; massage therapist → sells home tools/bands/medicine balls drop-shipped by another company; educator → recommends customer-service software, gets commission per sign-up.
- [S] **Turn Attraction Offers into Continuity with automatic renewal:** e.g. Buy 6 Get 6 Free rolls into month-to-month after 12 months — "a two-for-one."
- [S] **Mix and match however you want.** Upsell tactics inside attraction offers; downsell process on every offer; continuity as attraction. "Start with the way I suggest it. Then, as you get better, experiment."
