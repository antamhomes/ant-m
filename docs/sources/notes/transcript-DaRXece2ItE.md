## Segment-by-segment notes

### 0. Hook (00:00:00–00:00:32)
- [E] 500,000 people registered for the $100M Leads launch; "almost a third" did not know who he was — they were referred by other people for free.
- [S] Most businesses do not know how to use this advertising method; the video gives the tactics, not just theory, so they can be copied.

### 1. What an affiliate is; the offer to affiliates (00:00:33–00:01:37)
- [S] Read from the Leads book: an affiliate is a **lead getter** — an independent business that tells its audience to buy your stuff. Affiliates look like referrals from outside but differ "under the hood": (1) they own their own businesses and do their own advertising; (2) they agree to offer your stuff to their engaged leads in exchange for **money, free stuff, or both**.
- [S] Rule: as any business starts with the offer, the affiliate programme starts with the offer *to the affiliate* — they are a new customer to think about. Payment options: money, free stuff, or both, in any combination.
- [E] For both the Leads and the Money Models launches he uses **free stuff**. Reasons: (1) paying "a gazillion people" with 1099s is a paperwork nightmare; (2) non-monetary value can be worth more to the affiliate than a 10% kickback — especially when the product is inexpensive (a book), where cash commissions would be small.

### 2. Two-tier prize structure: killers at the top, long tail at the bottom (00:01:37–00:06:14)
- [S] Rule: always a **two-tier structure** — something for the "killers" with big audiences who send thousands, and something for "the everyman" who brings 3, 5 or 10 friends. "You don't want something that's only for one or the other."
- [E] **Top 10**: an in-person fireside one-on-one Q&A session with him, plus a dinner.
- [S] Design principle behind it: the prize must benefit the affiliate **and their audience**. Affiliates disengage if they feel they are hard-closing their own audience and spending their goodwill; make the prize a gift to their audience so it adds to their goodwill. Goal: **win-win-win** (you, the affiliate, their people).
- [E] Mechanism: the affiliate's audience clicks through the affiliate's link to submit questions, which the affiliate then asks on their behalf in the fireside (e.g. "we're in the ATM niche — how does Alex's big-business stuff apply to ATMs?" — a question they would otherwise never get answered).
- [E] **Top 50**: in person at his headquarters, ~3 hours of Q&A; a chance at one question; they meet one another — "approximations" of the top prize.
- [S] **Minimum activation point** for the bottom tier depends on how hard the referral action is:
  - bring someone physically into your facility (e.g. a gym) → maybe **2** people;
  - make a text introduction → maybe **4 or 5**;
  - get someone to opt in to something (very low friction) → set it higher; he uses **10+** opt-ins ("what I did last year, it worked great, so I'm doing it again").
- [E] **10+ opt-ins** get: (1) a separate audiobook recorded specifically for affiliates — "70 plus one-minute tactics that drive profit" — not the launch book; (2) entry into a raffle for the remaining 50 in-person seats (rather than simply "top 100", which he judged less valuable); (3) a recording of the event.
- [S] Prize design rules: (1) make it "big and sexy"; (2) prefer things that are **unscalable, especially at the top** (a fireside with 1,000 people would work for nobody); (3) create "varying levels of approximation" to the top prize so every tier gets a version of it; (4) it should benefit their audience and them, and ladder up to performance.
- [E] He spent two days on the permutations to make it clear, simple, compelling and beneficial to every party; "it always seems obvious in retrospect". "If you make the offer good enough, you can move mountains" — the highest-leverage step.

### 3. The affiliate sales page (00:06:14–00:07:12)
- [S] Once the offer exists, there must be a place where people learn about it: an **affiliate sales page** that sells people on why to become an affiliate / refer friends. Content: each prize, exactly what they must do, and how you will help them do it.
- [E] What they must do: opt in (name, email etc.) → receive a personal link → all their traffic goes through it → they rank on a leaderboard.

### 4. Supporting the affiliates (00:07:12–00:08:22)
- [S] Do not ask affiliates to "go make a campaign for me" — too big an ask. Give them pre-written, pre-approved emails, posts and ads that already worked last year, so they copy-paste and maybe change a couple of things. "Make it easy to become an affiliate to get the benefits."
- [E] Extra, "dotted-line" benefit for his audience specifically: during the launch they share **behind the scenes** — how each email performs, which ads are working — so affiliates learn the launch process "from the outside in" for their own products. He calls this "probably one of the most underrated components of becoming an affiliate".

### 5. Promoting the affiliate programme (00:08:22–00:11:31)
- [E] Channels, in order:
  1. **Email his list — only twice** ("FYI we have this" + "in case you missed it"), because "I want to promote the book, not promote promoting the book". After that all focus is the launch itself.
  2. **Last year's affiliates** ("30-something thousand"): "you did this — want to run it back?" Emailed 1–2 additional times; acceptable because they already opted in as affiliates and it is a small segment, not the whole list.
  3. **Every book buyer**: the pre-order thank-you page (which carries the event reminder sequence) has a button at the bottom — "want to send anybody else / have an audience / want more free prizes?" — leading to the affiliate sales page.
  4. **This video** — free content that is itself affiliate recruitment.
- [S] Mapping to the core four: warm and cold outreach (to existing affiliates), email, posting free content (this video). He is **not running ads to recruit affiliates** — ads go to book pre-orders/registrations, whose thank-you page then feeds the affiliate page.
- [S] The cycle: use the core four to get affiliates, then hand the affiliates the same core-four tools (posts, emails, ads) so they do it for you. "You tell people about your stuff, and then other people tell people about your stuff. That's the game."

### 6. Onboarding, activation and follow-up for affiliates (00:11:31–00:15:24)
- [S] Warning: if you are missing any of the three earlier pieces (worth it, they know about it, easy) "you could just stop right there — it's not going to work". And you cannot expect affiliates to promote by magic; this is "another place where businesses fall off" — you must follow up.
- [S] **Onboarding + activation** steps: (1) opt in; (2) collect any extra data on the affiliate that helps you help them; (3) **fast reward** — they immediately get their custom link, because if they do not believe your attribution tracking, they will not send traffic; (4) **demonstrate attribution** with a **real-time leaderboard** so they see their referrals counted and how they rank.
- [S] Then give **collateral** — the point where "a lot of people fall off again": if you expect them to build an entire campaign, "good luck". Make it fast and easy.
- [E] **"Blackbooks"**: plug-and-play collateral for every channel — content, ads, outreach, email — plus text messages people can paste into work Slack channels or group threads "so people don't have to think about it".
- [S] Side benefit of writing the collateral yourself: messaging is represented properly (no "get rich quick"), everyone arrives with the same expectations, so you can deliver on them. Compliance guidelines included; they reduce to "don't lie, don't exaggerate, state the facts".
- [E] Collateral is split in two: (a) the starting set; (b) "**the winners**" — as data comes in, new ads or the subset that is really converting — so affiliates double down on what works "and use your data to win for their audience".
- [S] **Regular communication** has two components: (1) "cool stuff" — data and behind-the-scenes; (2) the **leaderboard, sent as frequently as possible** — competitors want to check it daily; it is the feedback loop "you're doing a good job, keep going".
- [E] As people climb the board, his director of marketing / team talks to the top movers to help them do more — an "additional unstated incentive": if you crush it, we help you crush it more.

### 7. Who is an affiliate — one thing, many names (00:15:24–00:17:10)
- [S] Anyone who promotes your stuff is a promoter. Industry labels for the same thing: influencer advertising (physical products), UGC / user-generated content (customer-driven), affiliates (direct response), referrals (brick and mortar), centres of influence / COIs (finance), joint ventures (elsewhere). Fundamentally: people who tell others about your stuff for money, free stuff, or because they love the product.
- [S] Rule: speak each type's language and set each up in their format —
  - warm audiences on Instagram or YouTube → posts framed for that;
  - text-based people with an email list, Substack, X, LinkedIn, blogs → text assets;
  - pure paid-ads operators → hand them the pre-approved ads that already work. [E] Last year some top-50 finishers had no audience; they just ran the ads.
- [S] "As soon as there's friction, they'll stop." Make it easy, fast and personalised to them.

### 8. Recap of the system (00:17:10–00:19:10)
- [S] Selling through other people's audiences is free "with the caveat that you give them some strong incentive". Sequence:
  1. **The offer to the affiliates** — beneficial for you, for them, and for their audience, so it is not an ask from them to their people.
  2. **A place** — affiliate / influencer / partner / centre-of-influence / JV page.
  3. **Promotion** — outreach to people who know you, content posts, ads flowing through the existing funnel (pre-order → thank-you page → affiliate page).
  4. **Follow-up** — onboarding and activation with fast feedback loops (custom link, leaderboard), regular communication (data, behind the scenes, leaderboard), the winners (ads, posts, angles, hooks that outperform), and personal outreach from his team to the top 100 / top 50: "how can we help you promote it and crush it even more?"
- [S] Claim: this is "how you advertise for free" and "one of the most scalable ways to advertise in any niche"; a core pillar of how he advertises.
- [E] Skool: the majority of traffic is from people telling people. ALAN (the software company he sold) was "almost entirely" affiliate-driven. Prestige Labs: almost 100% affiliate sales, via brick-and-mortar distributors (gyms) selling in their locations.
- [S] Both ends must be able to win: a gym owner with 100 members will not make the top 50 but can hit 10+ and get a shot at the room.

### 9. Outro (00:19:10–00:20:30)
- [S] The affiliates chapter in the Leads book was cut in half to fit; "there's so much stuff to really do it right".
- [E] CTA to become an affiliate or just send friends, employees, gym members; he would rather give free stuff to people than "a king's ransom to the social media platforms". Transcript cuts off.
